\section{The kill-test}
\label{part:killtest}

\textbf{The decision Phase 0 was built to make.} Tier~2 \S 0.6 states it as a
question about a matrix; this part asks it of data and reports what came back.

% =====================================================================
\subsection{The question, restated}
\label{sec:question}

\begin{invariant}
Let $\aff = \{j : \kap_j > 0.1\}$ be the \textbf{active set} --- the columns
not in near-balance. Restricted to \aff, is the map $\netflux \mapsto
\nu\netflux$ well-conditioned, and does it carry the physics?
\end{invariant}

If yes, \textbf{Target A} with an equilibrium mask is the architecture: the
model predicts a per-reaction flux, the mask switches off the equilibrated
columns, and $\dd Y = \nu\netflux$ conserves everything exactly. If no ---
either because the net flow spreads over too many near-cancelled columns, or
because the restricted $\nu$ is ill-conditioned --- the project falls back to
\textbf{Target B}: predict $\dd Y$ directly in a linear space and project onto
the constraint manifold.

Six measurable clauses, from \code{CLAUDE.md}:

\begingroup\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{0.6cm} Y L{3.2cm} L{3.4cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{\#} & \textbf{quantity} & \textbf{pass} & \textbf{fail} \\
\midrule
1 & fraction of $|\Delta\Ye|$ carried by \aff & $\ge 95\%$ & --- \\
2 & fraction of dominant-isotope $|\Delta X|$ carried by \aff & $\ge 95\%$ & --- \\
3 & share of net columns near the \kap{} floor & --- & $> {\sim}30\%$ \\
4 & $\cond(\Sact)$ & $< 10^{6}$ & $> 10^{8}$ \\
5 & maskable-set size under the Guidry $\eps$ sweep & non-empty & empty $\Rightarrow$ full-width \\
6 & mask churn per step & $< 5\%$ & $> 5\%$ $\Rightarrow$ freeze \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\endgroup

Plus two that were carried as assumptions and became measurements: the
\textbf{timescale separation} (``6--8 orders'', never sourced) and the
\textbf{energy consistency} residual.

\subsection{The instruments}
\label{sec:instruments}

\code{src/gnn\_nucleo/killtest/} is four small modules, and their design is
worth reading as an argument.

\paragraph{\code{strata.py} --- one definition of the grid, imported
everywhere.}

\begin{srclisting}
T9_EDGES = [1.6, 2.5, 3.3, 4.0, 5.0, 6.3, 7.95]
YE_EDGES = [0.45, 0.4667, 0.4833, 0.5001]
\end{srclisting}

Six \Tn{} bins $\times$ three \Ye{} bins. The bin edges are the regime box's,
with extra resolution across the QSE window. Lifting them into a module ``so
scripts stop redefining them'' is a small thing that prevents a large class of
almost-but-not-quite comparisons.

\paragraph{\code{active\_set.py} --- primitives that measure and never
conclude.} Its docstring says so explicitly: \emph{``Numbers only --- pass/fail
thresholds live in docs/phase0-checklist.md and the verdict document, never
here.''} Six functions:

\begingroup\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{3.6cm} Y@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{function} & \textbf{what it computes} \\
\midrule
\code{guidry\_masks} & maskable columns per $\eps$, \textbf{strictly} via
  \code{qse.diagnostics.eligible\_mask} so weak columns are excluded
  structurally (invariant \#2) \\
\code{cond\_s\_active} & cond of $\nu$ restricted to active columns,
  \textbf{rank-revealing} (ratio of extreme \emph{nonzero} singular values;
  $A\cdot\nu = 0$ always, and $Z\cdot\nu = 0$ on the strong columns / the
  lepton-extended $\nu$, so the naive condition number is infinite) \\
\code{carried\_fractions} & per-species $\mathrm{cov}_i = \sum_{j\in\aff}
  |\nu_{ij}\netflux_j| \,/\, \sum_j |\nu_{ij}\netflux_j|$ \\
\code{topk\_concentration} & cumulative share of the top-$k$ entries \\
\code{mask\_churn}, \code{timescale\_separation} & per-step flips;
  fastest-balanced-gross / slowest-bottleneck-net \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\endgroup

Two details that are easy to get wrong and are got right here.
\textbf{\code{cond\_s\_active} excludes the structural null vectors}:
$A\cdot\nu = 0$ and $Z\cdot\nu = 0$ hold by construction (Tier~2 \S I.2), so a
naive condition number would be infinite for every possible active set and the
gate would be vacuous. And \textbf{\code{carried\_fractions} expects $\nu$ and
$\netflux$ restricted to net columns} (forward-or-unpaired), because counting a
forward/reverse pair twice would double the denominator and halve every coverage
number.

\paragraph{\code{manifold.py} --- row assembly, with a refusal.} It builds the
relaxed manifold from FluxStore trajectory runs, and:

\begin{srclisting}
if scr not in ("None", "none"):
    raise ValueError(f"{run_id}: kappa/delta analysis requires the UNSCREENED run ...")
\end{srclisting}

\code{CLAUDE.md}'s two-\kap{} rule, encoded as code that will not run. Given
that the screened \kap{} floor at NSE is ${\sim}7\times10^{-2}$ and the
active-set threshold is $10^{-1}$, using a screened run would put a large
fraction of genuinely balanced pairs on the wrong side of the gate. This is the
single highest-value guard in the node.

It also computes the \textbf{phase split}: \code{attractor} marks rows at or
after the \code{first\_quiet} arrival row (\S\ref{sec:stall}), so every
statistic can be reported separately for the \emph{relaxing} phase (strong
sector still equilibrating) and the \emph{attractor} phase (strong sector done,
weak drift continuing).

\subsection{The two distributions --- and why they are different worlds}
\label{sec:twodistributions}

This is the measurement that reframed the project, and it is the one to
understand before reading any gate.

\textbf{Distribution A --- the training grid.} 1.03M in-strata states per
network, random Sobol compositions. \resultsref{2026-07-11}:

\begin{itemize}
\item median $\log_{10}\kap \approx$ \textbf{$-0.05$ in every stratum} --- i.e.\
  $\kap \approx 1$ everywhere;
\item fraction $\kap > 0.1$: \textbf{0.985--0.999} (\msn{80}), 0.974--0.997
  (\msn{151});
\item fraction $\kap < 10^{-3}$: \textbf{0.000};
\item per-state active fraction, median \textbf{0.987--1.000};
\item consequently the \kap-active union is \textbf{all} net columns (327/327,
  846/846) in every stratum, so $\Sact \approx$ full $\nu$ and
  $\cond = 41.8$ / $57.4$.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Every gate passes, and the pass is vacuous.} There is no cancellation
structure on the training distribution at all. The reason is physical, not
statistical: random compositions carry free nucleons at percent level, so every
capture reaction runs hard in one direction and nothing is near balance.
\textbf{The training states are \emph{relaxing}, not \emph{relaxed}.}

\textbf{Distribution B --- the relaxed manifold.} Pre-terminal rows of 20
shipped $+$ 209 rerun trajectories per network, 4,693 sampled rows each.
\resultsref{2026-07-12}:

\begin{itemize}
\item fraction $\kap > 0.1$ falls to \textbf{0.70 / 0.64} in the \Tn{}
  $[5.0, 6.3)$ stratum;
\item \kap-balanced pairs ($\kap < 10^{-3}$) are \textbf{$\le 0.4\%$ of
  carrying pairs everywhere} --- clean detailed balance essentially never
  occurs;
\item the cancellation structure is a \textbf{continuum} of
  $\kap \in 10^{-3} \ldots 10^{-1}$, not a clean split into ``equilibrated''
  and ``active''.
\end{itemize}

So the QSE cancellation structure --- the thing the whole architecture is
designed around --- \textbf{lives on the relaxed manifold and is absent from the
training distribution}.

\textbf{Why this is a regime shift, not a covariate shift.} Tier~2 \S III.9
gives the dynamical reason and it is worth stating here in the form that
matters for Tier~4: relaxation \emph{onto} the slow manifold and motion
\emph{along} it are different functions. A model trained on the first and
deployed on the second is not extrapolating --- it is being asked a different
question. No amount of data augmentation on the training distribution fixes
that; the fix is training data from the right distribution, which is what
\code{CLAUDE.md}'s Sobol $\to$ real-MESA gate (retrain if real-track
99th-percentile \Ye{} error $> 3\times$ the Sobol-measured value) exists to
detect. \textbf{That gate is predicted to fire}, and \foreignreg{2}{R-07}'s
measurement --- the $20\,\Msun$ track sits within 0.12--0.18 dex of a
\emph{line} in the box, and only 35\% / 62\% / 82\% of the box is within 0.1 /
0.2 / 0.3 dex of a visited point --- is the thermodynamic half of the same
mismatch.

\textbf{And it is why the kill-test verdict had to be rendered on distribution
B.} A verdict on the training grid would have been a confident, fully-passing,
completely uninformative ``yes''.

\subsection{Gate 1 --- \texorpdfstring{\Ye}{Ye} coverage: a structural pass}
\label{sec:gate1}

\textbf{PASS at 1.0000}, and the reason is structural rather than fortunate.

Weak columns are stored unpaired in the flux engine ($\fm \equiv 0$), so
$\kap = |\netflux|/(\fp + \fm) = 1$ wherever a weak column carries any flux at
all. Verified exactly on \textbf{8,825,170 / 33,189,419} (weak column $\times$
relaxed row) samples with $\fp > 0$ \resultsref{2026-07-12}; fluxless weak
columns have $\kap = 0$ by the zero-denominator convention and contribute
nothing. Weak columns are also never maskable, by invariant \#2, enforced
structurally in \code{eligible\_mask}.

So the $|\dd\dYdt|$ coverage by any active set of the form $\{\kap > \theta\}$,
$\theta < 1$, is exactly 1.

\textbf{Read this carefully, because it is a pass that proves less than it
looks.} The gate asks ``does the active set carry the \Ye{} physics?'' and the
answer is ``yes, by construction of the active set''. It is not evidence that
the flux head will \emph{predict} those columns well; it is evidence that the
mask will not delete them. Tier~1 \S VIII.D's habit applies: what would still be
wrong if this passed? Answer --- everything about the accuracy of the weak
columns, which the gate does not touch.

What the gate \emph{does} deliver, as a side effect, is the concentration
measurement: top-5 weak channels carry \textbf{0.96 / 0.75} and top-20 carry
\textbf{1.00 / 1.00} of $\sum|\dd\dYdt|$ on high-\Ye{} QSE-window rows, \#1
being \nuc{Ni}{56} EC at 0.62 / 0.28 (\S\ref{sec:yecarriers}). That is a real,
actionable result: it tells the loss function where to spend.

\subsection{Gate 5 --- the Guidry sweep returns EMPTY}
\label{sec:gate5}

The most consequential single number in the tier.

The Guidry departure criterion (\citealt{guidry2013a} --- their
near-equilibrium test $|y_i - \bar y_i|/\bar y_i < \eps_i$, applied with
$\eps_i = 0.01$ in their examples; Tier~2 \S III.10) declares a species
equilibrated when $|y - \bar y|/\bar y < \eps$ against a reference equilibrium,
and a \emph{reaction} maskable when all its participants are. The $\eps$ sweep
is $\{3\times10^{-3}, 10^{-2}, 3\times10^{-2}\}$.

\textbf{Measured: maskable columns per row have median AND p90 $= 0$, at every
$\eps$, both networks.} \resultsref{2026-07-12}.

Not ``few''. Zero, at the 90th percentile, everywhere.

\textbf{Why.} $\delta_r$ is measured against \textbf{true NSE}, and the label
manifold's own equilibria are Appendix-B-displaced (\S\ref{part:pathology}).
The manifold does reach \emph{an} equilibrium --- that is what the attractor
phase is --- but not the one the criterion tests against. The two are
6.7--13.0 dex apart at $\Tn \gtrsim 5$ (\msn{151}; 6.9--11.5 for \msn{80}) and
displaced by an unknown amount below that.

\textbf{Consequences, in order of severity:}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{$\cond(\Sact) = \cond(\nu$ restricted to all net columns$) =
  41.8$ / $57.4$ at every $\eps$} (full $\nu$: 41.6 / 57.9) --- five orders
  below the $10^{6}$ gate. Gate 4 passes, but for a degenerate reason: the mask
  is empty, so there is nothing to restrict.
\item \textbf{The hybrid design's maskable set is empty. Target A operates
  full-width.} Component B's ``Guidry prior $+$ learned $L_0$ gate'' loses its
  prior; the mask becomes a purely learned object with no physics
  initialisation, or is dropped.
\item \textbf{Gate 6 (churn) is moot.} Measured churn is 0.00--0.03 flips/step
  ($\le 0.002\%$/step) at every $\eps$ over 35 QSE-window trajectories ---
  trivially below the 5\%/step freeze trigger. But the mask is empty, so the
  hybrid-versus-frozen decision is settled by \textbf{emptiness, not churn}.
  Recording both, and saying which one decided, is the right way to report it.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{What this result is and is not.} It is a measurement about \emph{this
label manifold}, and the manifold is (a) constant-$(T, \rho)$, (b) started from
unphysical compositions, (c) bug-displaced above 5 GK, and (d) evaluated
against a criterion whose reference is true NSE rather than the manifold's own
attractor. Every one of those is a reason the result might not transfer to a
physical silicon-burning trajectory. It is \emph{not} a demonstration that QSE
does not occur in stars.

The correct statement, and the one the verdict document makes: \textbf{on the
data the model will be trained and evaluated on, there is no maskable set.}
That is a statement about an engineering decision, and it settles that
decision.

An open question the notes should carry: $\delta_r$ is measured against
\textbf{true NSE} when the physically meaningful comparison might be against
\textbf{the manifold's own attractor}. Testing departure from an equilibrium the
system is not heading toward is arguably the wrong test. Redoing the sweep with
the attractor as reference is cheap and might well produce a non-empty mask.
Registered as \reg{T-04}.

\subsection{Gate 2 --- the split verdict, and the \texorpdfstring{$1/\kap$}{1/kappa} amplification}
\label{sec:gate2}

The gate that did not cleanly pass.

Coverage is measured two ways per row: for the \textbf{median} dominant
isotope, and for the \textbf{worst} dominant isotope (min over species with
$X > 10^{-3}$). \resultsref{2026-07-12} (the two ``$\ge$'' entries below are
from the verdict document, \code{docs/phase0-killtest-verdict.md}, not
\code{RESULTS.md}):

\begingroup\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{3.6cm} L{4.2cm} Y@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{stratum $\times$ phase} & \textbf{median-dominant coverage} &
  \textbf{worst-dominant coverage} \\
\midrule
$\Tn < 3.3$ & 1.00 typical & $\ge 0.61$ \\
$[3.3, 4.0)$ & 1.00 & $\ge 0.36$ \\
$[4.0, 5.0)$ relaxing & $\ge 0.95$ & \textbf{0.1658 / 0.0700} \\
$[5.0, 6.3)$ & \textbf{0.62 -- 0.80} & \textbf{0.0022 -- 0.0290 / 0.0084 -- 0.0475} \\
$[1.6, 2.5)$ relaxing & 1.00 & 0.90 / 0.75 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\endgroup

So: the \emph{typical} species' evolution is carried by the active set almost
everywhere, but in the 4--6.3 GK band there exist dominant species
($X > 10^{-3}$!) whose net evolution rides almost entirely on
\textbf{near-cancelled columns} --- $\kap \in 10^{-3} \ldots 10^{-1}$, i.e.\
outside the active set.

\textbf{Why that is dangerous, quantitatively.} If $\netflux$ is inferred from
features that scale with the \emph{gross} flux, then an error $\delta$ in a
gross-scale quantity produces an error $\delta/\kap$ in the net flux, because
$\netflux = \fp - \fm$ and the relative error of a difference is amplified by
$(\fp + \fm)/|\netflux| = 1/\kap$. At $\kap = 10^{-3}$ that is a factor of
1000. This is exactly the ``\kap{} as a condition number'' reading of Tier~2
\S II.4(b), and here it is being measured on the species the composition is
made of.

\textbf{Why it is nevertheless not a Target-B switch.} Two arguments, both in
the verdict document, and both correct:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{ADR 0001's switch conditions do not fire.} $\cond(\Sact) =
  41.8/57.4$, not $> 10^{6}$. Low-\kap{} share is 0.9--24\%, not $> 90\%$.
\item \textbf{Target B faces the same problem.} The cancellation is in the
  \emph{labels}: $\Delta Y$ for those species is itself a small difference of
  large opposing contributions. Predicting $\Delta Y$ directly does not remove
  the cancellation, it just hides it inside the label. Target B would surrender
  the per-reaction structure (which the concentrated bridge and EC channels
  reward) without removing the conditioning problem.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{What it does force} is supervision-level mitigation: \Phifl-level
supervision where feasible, plus loss weighting on the affected columns.
\S\ref{sec:phisupervision} is the feasibility.

\subsection{Gates 3, 7, 8 --- spread, timescales, energy}
\label{sec:gates378}

\textbf{Gate 3 (spread) --- PASS.} The low-\kap{} ($\kap \le 0.1$) column share
of active net columns has median 0.9\%--24\% across every stratum $\times$
phase, worst case 0.24/0.21 in the attractor phase of $[5.0, 6.3)$
\resultsref{2026-07-12}. The ${\sim}30\%$ FAIL line is not approached. So the
failure mode ``net flow spreads diffusely over hundreds of near-cancelled
columns'' does \textbf{not} occur: the problematic carriers of
\S\ref{sec:gate2} are \textbf{few and identifiable}, which is what makes
targeted mitigation possible.

\textbf{Gate 7 (timescale separation) --- an assumed number, RETIRED by
measurement.} The design documents carried a working figure of ``6--8 orders of
magnitude'' of separation between the fast equilibrated sector and the slow
bottleneck flow --- unsourced (the phrase ``6--8 orders of magnitude'' does
occur in \citet{guidry2013a}, but there it compares a realistic network's
fastest rates to an $\alpha$-network's --- a different quantity;
\citet{guidry2012} gives 10--20 orders between the fastest and slowest
timescales of a thermonuclear network). Measured as
$\log_{10}[(\text{fastest \kap-balanced gross rate})/(\text{slowest
95\%-bottleneck net rate})]$: per-stratum medians span
\textbf{$-12.2 \ldots +1.4$}, with p10 at $-25$ and p90 at $+3.5$
\resultsref{2026-07-12}.

The distribution is broad and \textbf{mostly negative} --- i.e.\ there is no
cleanly separated fast sector at all, because \kap-balanced pairs are
$\le 0.4\%$ of carrying pairs and are mostly low-flux. The assumed figure is not
merely imprecise; it describes a structure that is absent.

\textbf{This retirement has teeth.} The rollout governor specified for
Component C (the \citealt{ono2026} timescale-rescaled update) was adopted for
row 15 on the premise of ``a separated fast sector'' --- a project premise, not
the paper's: Ono \& Sugimura's own stated premise is that at short $\Delta t$
the physical change is smaller than the prediction error, and their rescaling
$\Delta t/\tau_\eps \cdot \Delta x_{\mathrm{pred}}(\tau_\eps)$ presumes
approximately linear evolution over $\tau_\eps$. That premise is now measured
false. \tierone{VIII.F} ranks ``a design with a falsified premise and no study
node'' as the highest-leverage open item in the project, and it is right to.

\textbf{Gate 8 (energy) --- two readings, reported separately.} The verdict
document does something unusual and correct here: it declines to collapse two
different measurements into one verdict.

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{The gate as written} ($\enuc = \sum_j Q_j \netflux_j$ from the same
  net fluxes as composition): \textbf{PASSES} with $\ge 3$ orders of margin ---
  residual median $6\times10^{-6} \ldots 2.5\times10^{-5}$, max
  $8.7\times10^{-4}$, fraction $\le 1\% = 1.000$ in every stratum
  \resultsref{2026-07-12}. With the caveat that with constant mass-derived $Q$
  on both sides this comparison is near-algebraic (Tier~2 \S IV.8): it bounds
  $Q$-table rounding, not route physics.
\item \emph{The independent-leg check} (engine-rate energy vs label-$\Delta X$
  energy, \S\ref{sec:compositionroute}): median ratio \textbf{0.979 / 0.996}
  \resultsref{2026-07-11}. \msn{80} at a 2.1\% median is \textbf{outside} a
  1\% band, and is carried as an \textbf{open margin item} into Phase 1.
\end{itemize}

Reporting ``passes with margin'' and ``fails by $2\times$'' side by side, with
the explanation of why they measure different things, is what an honest gate
looks like.

\subsection{The verdict, and the statistics it does not carry}
\label{sec:verdict}

\begin{result}
\textbf{VERDICT: Target A, full-width, no Guidry masking, weak sector
structural.} Unconditional for $\Tn < 4.0$; conditional for $[4.0, 5.0)$
(worst-species coverage fails, mitigation measured-feasible); deferred for
$[5.0, 6.3)$ (label physics bug-displaced, \Phifl{} labels blocked on
integrator cost). No ADR-0001 switch condition fires; no PIVOT criterion is
met.
\end{result}

\textbf{Now the part the verdict document does not have, and should.}

Every headline number above is a median or a fraction over ${\sim}4{,}693$ rows
per network, and \textbf{those rows are not independent.} They are $\le 24$
rows sampled from each of 229 trajectories, and rows within a trajectory share
a $(T, \rho)$, an initial composition, and a continuous dynamical history.
Tier~2 \S V.3 measured the intraclass correlation: \textbf{ICC 0.22--0.70 across
trajectories, design effect 6.5--18.6} (\citealt{kish1965}'s
$\mathrm{deff} = 1 + (m - 1)\rho$; \citealt{shrout1979} for the ICC). So
\code{RESULTS.md}'s ``657k carrying-pair samples'' carry roughly \textbf{300
independent systems}.

What that does and does not affect:

\begingroup\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}Y Y@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{affected} & \textbf{not affected} \\
\midrule
every p-value and confidence interval (all currently unstated) &
  point estimates --- medians and fractions are unbiased \\
any claim of the form ``significantly below the 30\% line'' &
  the \textbf{empty-mask} result, which is p90 $= 0$ (a hard zero cannot be a
  sampling artifact) \\
the Spearman correlations' stated significance ($+0.491$/$+0.498$,
  ``$p \approx 0$'' over 657k samples --- the p-value is meaningless at
  $n_{\mathrm{eff}} \approx 300$) &
  the structural results ($\kap \equiv 1$ on weak columns) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\endgroup

The fix is one afternoon's work: \textbf{bootstrap over trajectories, not
rows.} \foreignreg{2}{R-08}, and this tier's \reg{T-05}. Until it is done, the
verdict's point estimates stand and its inferential language should be removed.

\textbf{A second missing control}, and it is the one a hostile referee reaches
for first: \textbf{there is no null distribution for any of these statistics.}
Is 0.87 dex of intra-group \rQSE{} spread large? Is a Spearman of $+0.49$
between $\log\kap$ and $\log\delta$ good agreement or mediocre? Nobody knows,
because neither statistic has been computed on a case where the answer is known
--- e.g.\ on states drawn from exact NSE (where $\delta \equiv 0$ by
construction), or on a synthetic manifold with an imposed known mask.
\foreignreg{2}{R-18} raises this for \rQSE{} specifically; it generalises.
Registered as \reg{T-06}.

\subsection{What a complete statistics package would look like}
\label{sec:statspackage}

\S\ref{sec:verdict} names two gaps. This section designs the fix, because ``add
error bars'' is not actionable and the design is where the difficulty is.

\textbf{1. Cluster bootstrap over trajectories} (\citealt[\S 3.8]{davison1997};
\citealt{field2007}; \citealt{cameron2015}). Resample \emph{trajectories} with
replacement (229 of them per network), recompute every headline statistic on
each resample, report the 2.5/97.5 percentiles. Two design points that are easy
to get wrong:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Resample whole trajectories, not rows.} The whole point is that
  rows within a trajectory are correlated; a row bootstrap reproduces the false
  657k.
\item \textbf{Stratify the resample by (\Tn, phase)} if the statistic is
  reported per stratum, otherwise a resample can empty a cell. With ${\sim}30$
  trajectories per stratum, the intervals will be \emph{wide}, and that is the
  honest answer rather than a problem with the method.
\end{itemize}

Expected outcome, predicted here so it can be checked: with $n \approx 300$
nominal rows per stratum $\times$ phase cell, the 95\% interval on a fraction
near 0.8 is roughly $\pm 0.045$ if the rows were independent and
\textbf{$\pm 0.12$ -- $\pm 0.20$} at design effects 6.5--18.6
\derivedhere[from $1.96\sqrt{p(1-p)\cdot\mathrm{deff}/n}$: 0.045, 0.115,
0.195; an earlier version wrote ``$n_{\mathrm{eff}} \approx 300$'', which would
double-count the design effect --- corrected in the 2026-08-18 audit]. So the
\S\ref{sec:gate2} coverage numbers --- 0.62, 0.80, 0.95 --- are separated by
less than their intervals. \textbf{The split verdict's \emph{boundaries} are
statistically soft even though its extremes (0.002 vs 1.00) are not.}

\textbf{2. Threshold sensitivity: sweep $\theta$.} The active set is
$\{\kap > \theta\}$ with $\theta = 0.1$, unsourced. Every gate that mentions
the active set is a function of $\theta$. Sweeping $\theta \in \{0.03, 0.1,
0.3\}$ over the already-computed flux stores costs minutes and answers a
question the verdict cannot currently answer: \emph{is the $[4.0, 6.3)$
coverage failure a fact about the physics or an artifact of where the line was
drawn?}

Two possible outcomes, both useful:

\begin{itemize}
\item coverage rises steeply as $\theta$ falls $\to$ the failing species'
  carriers sit just below 0.1, the failure is a threshold artifact, and
  \foreignreg{2}{R-01}'s entropy-weighted active set
  ($\sigma_j = 2\kB s_j \kap_j \artanh\kap_j$ --- the flux--force relation of
  \citealt{beard2007} in \kap{} form; parameter-free) should replace the
  threshold outright;
\item coverage is flat in $\theta$ $\to$ the carriers are genuinely deep in
  the cancelled regime, the failure is real, and \Phifl-level supervision is
  the only fix.
\end{itemize}

Registered as \reg{T-14}, and it is two hours on data already on disk.

\textbf{3. Null distributions.} Three statistics currently have no scale:

\begingroup\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{3.4cm} L{2.6cm} Y@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{statistic} & \textbf{measured} & \textbf{null needed} \\
\midrule
intra-group $\mathrm{std}(\rQSE)$ & 0.87 / 0.80 dex & its value on states
  drawn from \textbf{exact NSE} (where the answer should be $\approx 0$) and on
  random compositions (the upper bound) \\
Spearman($\log\kap$, $\log\delta_r$) & $+0.491$ / $+0.498$ & its value when
  \kap{} and $\delta$ are computed from \emph{independent} random states ---
  the floor --- and at exact NSE --- the ceiling \\
maskable-set size & 0 & its value on a synthetic manifold with an
  \textbf{imposed known mask} --- does the instrument find a mask that is
  there? \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\endgroup

The third is the important one and it is the standard missing control in this
whole class of work: \textbf{the mask-detection instrument has never been shown
to detect a mask.} Constructing the positive control is easy --- take an NSE
composition, perturb a handful of species, and check that \code{guidry\_masks}
returns everything except the perturbed reactions. Without it, ``the maskable
set is empty'' and ``the detector does not work'' are indistinguishable.

Registered as \reg{T-06}, and it should block any external claim of the
negative verdicts.

\subsection{What would have changed the answer}
\label{sec:whatwouldflip}

A useful exercise for reading any verdict: what result would have flipped it?

\begingroup\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{5.6cm} Y@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{finding} & \textbf{would have implied} \\
\midrule
$\cond(\Sact) > 10^{6}$ on the relaxed manifold & \textbf{Target B},
  immediately --- ADR 0001's first switch condition \\
low-\kap{} share $> 90\%$ of carrying columns & \textbf{Target B} --- the net
  flow is everywhere-cancelled and per-reaction fluxes are unrecoverable \\
low-\kap{} share $> 30\%$ (but $< 90\%$) & FAIL on gate 3: diffuse spread, no
  identifiable carriers, no targeted mitigation possible \\
a non-empty maskable set with churn $> 5\%$/step & freeze to the imposed
  Guidry mask (the \code{CLAUDE.md} rule) \\
$|\dd\dYdt|$ coverage $< 95\%$ & would have indicated a \emph{bug}, since it
  is structurally 1 --- a useful tripwire \\
median-dominant coverage failing below 4 GK & Target A unviable in the QSE
  window, i.e.\ in the regime the project exists for \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\endgroup

None of those fired. What did fire --- worst-species coverage in $[4.0, 6.3)$
--- is the one outcome the threshold list did not anticipate, because
\code{CLAUDE.md}'s gate says ``dominant isotopes'' without specifying median or
worst-case. Measuring both and reporting the split, rather than picking the
flattering one, is the single best decision in the verdict document.
