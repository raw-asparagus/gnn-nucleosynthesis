\section*{What this tier covers}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{What this tier covers}
\label{sec:covers}

Six nodes:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{S11} --- bbq and MESA as \emph{operated}: what a one-zone burner
  is, what hydrostatic mode does, how the shipped labels were generated, and how
  the 209-run-per-network rerun campaign that supplies the verdict distribution
  was designed, executed and validated.
\item \textbf{S12} --- the trajectory files: format, the \code{eps\_nuc}
  convention that had to be \emph{pinned by measurement} rather than read off a
  header, and the ``stall'' that turned out to be an attractor arrival.
\item \textbf{S13} --- silicon burning on actual burning histories, and
  \textbf{the label pathology}: the shipped training labels carry a MESA bug
  that displaces the equilibrium the network relaxes to, at $\Tn \gtrsim 5$, and
  therefore gets \Ye{} itself wrong there. This is the tier's headline result
  and its most consequential.
\item \textbf{S14} --- the kill-test: the active set, $\cond(\Sact)$, coverage,
  spread, churn, timescale separation, measured on two distributions that turn
  out to be different worlds.
\item \textbf{S15} --- the ML target decided: Target A, full-width, with the
  equilibrium mask measured empty; why the decision is \emph{not} about
  expressivity; and what the split verdict costs.
\item \textbf{S16} --- the baseline: what the NNN actually is, what it
  achieves, what ``beating it'' has to mean, and the evaluation protocol that
  claim requires.
\end{itemize}

Plus the front part the map does not show: \textbf{Part~0}, the astrophysics.
Tier~0 Part~I gave the motivation chain as a sequence of claims to defend;
Tier~2 Part~0 gave the equilibrium theory the algebra needs. This part gives the
\emph{whole astrophysical setting}, derived: the star as a thermodynamic engine,
the burning staircase and why it accelerates, silicon burning in its hydrostatic
and explosive forms, the collapse in detail, the observables at the far end, and
the computational situation that makes emulation worth attempting. It is long on
purpose. Everything in Tiers~0--2 is \emph{for} this, and none of it is derivable
from the code.

\item Measured project numbers quoted here originate in \code{RESULTS.md} with

\item \textbf{Bibliographic details were verified in the 2026-08-18 literature

  externally-attributable claim now carries an inline author-year citation
  resolved in the \textbf{References} section at the end; ${\sim}700$ claims

  \code{RESULTS.md}. Corrections found by the audit were applied in place, with
  an inline ``(corrected in the 2026-08-18 audit)'' note wherever the physics
  content itself changed. The headline ones: MESA has \textbf{no} NSE switch
  (\S\ref{sec:nsetransition}, \reg{T-21} --- the ``5--7\,GK handoff'' is a

  the \nuc{Sc}{45}(p,$\gamma$)\nuc{Ti}{46} bottleneck is WAC73/HT96's, not the
  NNN paper's (\S\ref{sec:twoclusters}); the NNN's architecture is 12/9 layers of
  1024/2048, not the code's 4-layer default, and its author list is Grichener,
  Renzo, Kerzendorf et al.\ (\S\ref{sec:nnnis}); the NuGNN column of the novelty
  table understated that paper (\S\ref{sec:decodestep}, \S\ref{sec:nugnn});
  Ovadia et al.\ 2019 was cited against its own conclusion
  (\S\ref{sec:calibrated}, \reg{T-37}); the binomial in \S\ref{sec:multiplicity}
  (5\% is $\ge 63/108$, not $\ge 60$); and the ``${\approx}75\%$'' premise of
  \reg{T-41} is sourced (WAC73 via HT96) but for a different quantity.
  Pre-arXiv classics were verified only bibliographically this session (ADS
  abstract pages were unreachable; CrossRef/INSPIRE and cached bibliographies
  were used) --- their content is marked ASSUMED-with-attribution where no cached
  secondary states it. Part~0 leans on the CCSN and nucleosynthesis literature
  more heavily than any earlier tier; the References provenance notes say,
  entry by entry, what was actually opened.
\item Astrophysical numbers that are \emph{representative of a model class}
  rather than measured --- pre-supernova core masses, explosion energies,
  \nuc{Ni}{56} yields --- are tagged \typical. They vary by progenitor, code and
  mechanism, and quoting them as if they were constants is the single easiest
  way to write something false in this subject.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Maths formatting.} In the source, every \emph{equation} --- anything
with its own line --- is \LaTeX{} in a \code{\$\$ \ldots \$\$} block with the

inline \code{\$\ldots\$}. Symbols named in running prose stay in unicode; maths
is never set in \code{monospace}, which is reserved for code identifiers,
filenames and literal values. The delimiter convention is moot in a \LaTeX{}
document, but the \emph{separation} it enforces is preserved here: displayed
equations are numbered, inline conditions stay inline, and \code{monospace}
never carries mathematics.

Tier~3 is where this loop changes character. In Tiers~0--2 the oracle was
usually an exact algebraic statement or an independent implementation. Here
there are three nodes (\textbf{S11}, \textbf{S12}, \textbf{S13}) whose
``oracle'' is \emph{an experiment on external software you do not control}: run
bbq under two MESA versions and see which one reproduces the shipped labels. The
epistemics of that are different and worth naming ---
\S\ref{sec:epistemics} does it explicitly.

The other change: \textbf{this tier's headline results are all negative or
qualified.} The QSE single-cluster plateau is not confirmed, the maskable set is
empty, the timescale separation the design assumed does not exist, and the
training labels are wrong above 5\,GK. Not one of those was the expected answer.
A tier that returns four surprises either has broken instruments or is measuring
something real; \S\ref{sec:verdict} is the argument that it is the latter, and
it is the argument you should be most sceptical of.

\section*{A note on this edition}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{A note on this edition}
\label{sec:edition}

The source document is organised \emph{node by node}: Part~0 the astrophysics,
then S11 bbq/MESA, S12 the trajectory files, S13 the label pathology, S14 the
kill-test, S15 the target, S16 the baseline, then the handoff, the open register,
the boundary audit, and the emulator-as-an-object pass. Each of those parts
interleaves five different \emph{kinds} of statement --- astrophysical theory,
description of the object, a derivation, the code that implements it, and the
measured claim that closes it. That interleaving is the
DERIVE\,/\,READ\,/\,ORACLE\,/\,SEE study loop the source was written around.

This report re-architects the same material into a \textbf{physics arc}, in
which the S11--S16 curriculum labels are dissolved and the argument runs in the
order the physics does --- the order of the source's own organising claim
(\S\ref{part:engine}): a one-day episode of nuclear burning sets a single scalar,
which propagates through a collapse to become a number an astronomer measures;
the episode is stiff and expensive, so it is emulated; the emulator's data,
labels, verdict, target and baseline follow.

\emph{No content has been omitted.} The cost of the physics arc is that the
source's part boundaries no longer align with this report's sections. Three
things hold that together: every relocated argument carries cross-references in
both directions; Appendix~\ref{app:sectionmap} reproduces the source document's
own contents table and full section list verbatim; and
Appendix~\ref{app:concordance} is a heading-by-heading concordance from the

Three blocks of material were re-homed across the source's part boundaries,
rather than merely re-ordered:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Where \Ye{} comes from (source \S0.7) moves up} to
  \S\ref{part:weak}, directly after silicon burning and before the iron core:
  the weak sector is where the scalar the whole chain propagates is \emph{set},
  and $\Mch \propto \Ye^2$ (\S\ref{sec:mch}) and the collapse are what consume it.
  Honestly stated, this buys narrative order rather than reference direction:
  nothing in the source's \S0.4--0.6 refers to \S0.7, while two sentences in
  \S0.7 now point forward (its opener, and ``the runaway of \S0.5.2''); both
  are reworded to say so and marked with a TeX comment in the source file.

  returns (\S I.2b) join the computational setting} in \S\ref{part:host},
  beside the call-site contract of \S0.8.6 that \S I.2b explicitly says is
  ``the same thing from inside MESA''. The label generator
  (\S\ref{part:generator}) refers back to them.
\item \textbf{The boundary audit and the emulator-as-an-object pass (source
  Parts IX and X) precede the register and the handoff}, so that the
  register's second- and third-pass tables and the handoff's ``requirements the
  plan does not know it has'' point backward at material already read.
\end{itemize}

The source's Part~0 self-check (sixteen questions) is split across the five
sections that now hold the material it tests; Appendix~\ref{app:selfcheckmoves}
records every move. The source's other six blocks travel whole.
