\section{Trajectories and the \texorpdfstring{\code{eps\_nuc}}{eps\_nuc} pin}
\label{part:trajectories}

\textbf{A short node with one long lesson}: a column in a data file whose
meaning is not documented has to be established by \emph{experiment}, and doing
it properly looks like hypothesis testing.

% =====================================================================
\subsection{The file format}
\label{sec:fileformat}

One trajectory file, from \code{lib\_hydrostatic.f90}:

\begin{srclisting}
age  dt  eps_nuc  eps_neu  <iso_1> <iso_2> ... <iso_n>
0.0  1e-10  ...                                      <-- row 0: initial state
...
\end{srclisting}

\begin{itemize}
\item one row per output interval; \textbf{row 0 is the initial state}, with
  \code{dt} a $10^{-10}$ placeholder;
\item \code{dt} is the \emph{per-row burn duration}, not a cumulative age;
\item the isotope columns are mass fractions in the network's own order --- and
  \code{load\_trajectory} \textbf{validates that order against the project's
  isotope table} and raises if it differs. That check has no cost and closes an
  entire class of silent-corruption bug;
\item the shipped files encode (logT, logRho) in the filename; rerun files do
  not, so \code{load\_trajectory} \textbf{refuses} to guess for
  \code{source="rerun"} and requires them explicitly from the campaign manifest.
\end{itemize}

That last design choice is worth naming: the function could have parsed a rerun
filename and been right most of the time. Refusing instead makes the manifest
authoritative and makes a mismatch impossible rather than unlikely.

\subsection{What a trajectory row is \emph{not}}
\label{sec:rowisnot}

Four things that a row in these files looks like but is not, each of which has
bitten someone.

\paragraph{Not a solver step.} The output cadence is \code{times.txt}; the
solver takes as many internal substeps as it needs inside each interval. So the
composition change across a row is an \emph{integral}, and any comparison
against an instantaneous rate is a quadrature question. This is why the
handshake restricts to linearisable cells ($\tau > 10\Delta t$) and why the
flux-route energy comparison uses a trapezoid and inherits its error
(\S\ref{sec:gates378}'s 2.1\% open margin).

\paragraph{Not full precision.} The shipped label CSVs are float32 upstream;
this project parses everything as float64 but cannot recover what float32
destroyed. For a cancelled quantity --- and \S\ref{sec:gate2} shows dominant
species whose net evolution rides on $\kappa \sim 10^{-3}$ columns --- float32
label noise is ${\sim}10^{-7}$ relative, which is \textbf{larger than the net
signal divided by the gross scale} in the worst cases. \tierone{VIII.E.4} flags
the float32/float64 boundary; the sharpest form of it is: \emph{the labels may
not contain the cancellation the model is being asked to learn.} That is a
genuinely alarming sentence and it has never been quantified. Registered as
\reg{T-28}.

\paragraph{Not uncensored.} \code{final\_*} is floored at exactly $10^{-15}$
(measured attained), so trace species are clamped, not measured. A model trained
on $\log X$ will learn the clamp as a target. The Step-5 handshake already found
that 25.1\% / 12.6\% of its rate-level failures are the ``subfloor controller''
class --- the dominant channel's reactant is below the label floor, so the label
cannot express what drove the change. \resultsref{2026-07-10}

\paragraph{Not a state a star was in.} Constant $(T, \rho)$, a hand-built
initial mixture, no mixing, no expansion (\S\ref{sec:onezone},
\S\ref{sec:convection}). It is one operator of a split, exercised alone.

\subsection{The composition route to energy --- derived}
\label{sec:compositionroute}

To pin the \code{eps\_nuc} column you need an \emph{independent} way to compute
the energy released over an interval. The composition route is the one that
needs no rates at all.

Nuclear energy release is the decrease in total rest mass. Over an interval
where the composition changes by $\Delta Y_i$ (molar abundances, per gram),
%
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ecomp-restmass}
E_{\mathrm{comp}} \;=\; -\,N_A \sum_i m_i\,\Delta Y_i \;\times\; c^2 ,
\end{equation}
%
and using mass excesses $\Delta m_i = m_i - A_i m_u$ (Tier~0 \S 0.3.1) with
$\sum A_i \Delta Y_i = 0$ exactly by baryon conservation, the $m_u$ term drops
and
%
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ecomp}
\boxed{\;E_{\mathrm{comp}} \;=\; -\,N_A \sum_i \Delta m_i\,[\mathrm{MeV}]\;\Delta Y_i \times (1.602\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{erg/MeV})\;}
\end{equation}
%
in erg per gram (mass excesses from AME2020, \citealt{wang2021}).
\derivedhere{} This is \textbf{exact and quadrature-free}: it uses only the
endpoint compositions and a table of nuclear masses. It does not care about
rates, screening, the solver, or the timestep. That independence is what makes
it a valid oracle. ($E_{\mathrm{comp}}$ as written is the \emph{gross} release;
the file quantity the pin compares to is $E_{\mathrm{comp}} - \int \epsnu\,\dd t$,
\resultsref{2026-07-11}.)

The flux route, for contrast, is
%
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eflux}
E_{\mathrm{flux}} \;=\; \sum_j Q_j \Phi_j ,
\end{equation}
%
which needs the rates, a quadrature over the interval, and the Q-values. It is
the route the \emph{emulator} will use (invariant \#5), and it is therefore the
one that must be checked --- not the one to check against.

\begin{warn}
\textbf{A trap Tier~2 \S IV.8 names and this node inherits.} If the Q-values
are themselves derived from the same mass table as $E_{\mathrm{comp}}$, then
$E_{\mathrm{flux}} \equiv E_{\mathrm{comp}}$ is an algebraic identity given
$\Delta Y = \nu\Phi$, and comparing them checks arithmetic, not physics. The
comparison is only informative when the two legs have independent provenance
--- which is exactly why \S\ref{sec:pin}'s pin compares against the
\textbf{file column} (bbq's own accounting) rather than against the project's
flux route.
\end{warn}

\subsection{The pin, as a hypothesis test}
\label{sec:pin}

The question: what does the trajectory file's \code{eps\_nuc} column mean? The
candidates, from reading bbq and knowing the training CSVs' conventions:

\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{3.0cm} Y@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{axis} & \textbf{options} \\
\midrule
integration & \textbf{integrated} over the row's dt [erg/g] vs \textbf{rate}
  [erg/g/s] \\
normalisation & $\times 1$ vs $\times 10^{16}$ (the training CSVs divide by
  $10^{16}$) \\
neutrinos & gross vs \textbf{net of neutrino losses} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

Eight candidates. \code{scripts/step6\_eps\_pin.py} evaluates all of them
against $E_{\mathrm{comp}}$ (\S\ref{sec:compositionroute}) on 15,746 / 13,506
pre-stall intervals per network, with dt spanning $10^{-10}$ to
$9.5 \times 10^{9}$ s --- twenty decades.

The discriminating statistics, and \emph{why each one discriminates}:

\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{5.2cm} Y@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{statistic} & \textbf{what it rules out} \\
\midrule
median $\log_{10}$ of the ratio & the normalisation axis --- a factor
  $10^{16}$ is a 16-dex offset \\
\textbf{slope of median log-ratio vs dt decade} & the integration axis --- an
  integrated quantity read as a rate has a ratio $\propto$ dt, i.e.\
  \textbf{slope exactly $+1$} \\
sign agreement & the neutrino axis --- gross vs net differ in sign exactly
  where burning and neutrino losses nearly cancel \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

The result, \resultsref{2026-07-11}:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{integrated, net of neutrinos, no 1e16 normalisation.} Median
  $\log_{10}|\text{ratio}| = -0.000$ with IQR $[-0.000, +0.000]$; sign
  agreement 0.9933 / 0.9981; \textbf{slope vs dt decade = 0.000 across twenty
  decades}.
\item The rate reading shows the predicted wrong-convention slope
  \textbf{+1.00} and a median offset of $-2.2$ / $-1.3$ dex.
\item Net-of-$\nu$ beats gross decisively: sign agreement 0.91 / 0.81 for
  gross.
\item And the conclusion matches the source read: \code{bbq
  src/lib\_bbq.f90:453}, \code{out\% eps\_nuc = avg\_eps\_nuc * in\% time}.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Why this is a good piece of work and not just a lookup.} The source
read alone would have given the answer in five minutes. The measurement gives
the answer \emph{plus} a slope over twenty decades that would have caught a
per-row dt bug, a sign-agreement statistic that independently confirms the
neutrino handling, and a falsifiable record. Tier~1 \S VIII.D's habit ---
\emph{ask of any green check what would still be wrong if it passed} ---
applied here says: reading the source would not have told you whether the
\code{dt} column you divide by is the right one.

The consequence is pinned in code, and pinned defensively:

\begin{srclisting}
TRAJ_EPS_CONVENTION: str | None = "integrated"
# the converters REFUSE to run if this is reset to None
\end{srclisting}

\code{eps\_nuc\_rate()} and \code{eps\_nuc\_integrated()} both route through
\code{\_resolve\_convention}, which raises if the pin is unset. A module-level
constant that a function refuses to work without is a small piece of executable
policy --- the same pattern as Tier~1 \S VII's guards.

\textbf{A cross-check that arrived free.} With the convention pinned, the
\emph{flux} route could be compared: $\int \sum Q_j R_j\,\dd t / E_{\mathrm{comp}}$
on rate-stable pre-stall intervals gives median \textbf{0.979 / 0.996}
\resultsref{2026-07-11}. \msn{80} sitting at a 2.1\% median is outside a 1\%
band, and the verdict document carries it as an \textbf{open margin item}
rather than declaring the energy gate passed on that leg. Which is correct: the
trapezoid quadrature error, float32 label noise, and rate-config differences
have not been separated, and until they are, 2.1\% is a number with an unknown
decomposition.

\subsection{The stall, and its two reinterpretations}
\label{sec:stall}

This is the tier's best example of a measurement being reinterpreted twice,
each time getting sharper.

\paragraph{Reading 1 (Step 5, 2026-07-10) --- ``the trajectories are
broken.''} Composition freezes (max $|\Delta X|$ per interval $< 10^{-10}$)
from median age $2.2 \times 10^{5}$ / $3.9 \times 10^{4}$ s while
\code{eps\_nuc} keeps rising. The frozen states are \textbf{not} NSE: at
$\Tn = 6.1$, $\rho = 1.8 \times 10^{8}$, $\Ye = 0.467$ the file says
\nuc{Si}{30}-dominated with $X = 0.58$, while NSE says $\nuc{Fe}{56} = 0.69$.
Hypothesis: bbq's implicit stepping stalls once the output dt grows to
${\sim}10^{10}$ s, far beyond any physical timescale. Consequence: use
pre-stall rows only. \resultsref{2026-07-10}

\paragraph{Reading 2 (Step 6, 2026-07-11) --- ``it is an attractor arrival,
and the tail is real.''} Two things changed the reading:

\begin{enumerate}
\item The rerun campaign, with a cadence that cannot produce the output-dt
  blowup, \textbf{still} shows terminal freezing at high \Tn{} --- so the
  blowup is at most secondary.
\item A census over \emph{all} 1508 shipped files found that after the
  first-quiet row, composition \textbf{resumes} super-tolerance changes at late
  giant output dts, with \code{eps\_nuc} rising. The rules differ on
  \textbf{459 / 1508} files. \resultsref{2026-07-11}
\end{enumerate}

The mechanistic picture that explains both: \textbf{the strong sector reaches
its (displaced) equilibrium and freezes, while the weak sector keeps running.}
Weak reactions are slow, one-directional, and not in balance
(\S\ref{sec:yeidentity}; the quasi-equilibrium of \citealt{hix1996} is
equilibrium with respect to the strong and electromagnetic reactions only) ---
so once the strong pairs equilibrate, composition changes at the weak
timescale, which is long. Those tail rows are legitimate relaxed states of the
label dynamics (equilibrated strong pairs + active weak columns) and
\textbf{belong in the kill-test manifold} --- indeed they are the \emph{most}
relevant rows, since deployment happens on relaxed states.

The guard was changed accordingly:

\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{3.2cm} L{5.4cm} Y@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{mode} & \textbf{rule} & \textbf{what it marks} \\
\midrule
\code{terminal} (\textbf{default}) & the row after the \emph{last} interval
  reaching tolerance & excludes only a trajectory-ending quiet tail \\
\code{first\_quiet} & the \emph{first} interval below tolerance &
  \textbf{arrival at the attractor} --- now used as a phase marker, not a
  cutoff \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

And \code{first\_quiet} was retained rather than deleted, for two reasons: it
reproduces Step-5 row selections exactly (so old numbers stay checkable), and
it is the natural \textbf{phase split} for the kill-test ---
\code{RelaxedRows.attractor} marks rows at or after arrival, letting
\S\ref{part:killtest} report ``relaxing'' and ``attractor'' phases separately.
A discarded rule that becomes a useful diagnostic is a good sign about the
original measurement.

\paragraph{The cold-start bug the change also fixed.} Canonical Si mixes at
$\Tn = 2.5$ produce \emph{exactly zero} early-interval changes --- nothing is
burnable in $10^{-8}$-s steps --- so a first-quiet rule truncated whole runs at
row 0. The terminal rule keeps them, and they do evolve at the wall (eps rate
$10^{6}$--$10^{7}$ erg/g/s). \resultsref{2026-07-11} A rule that was merely
conservative on one dataset was catastrophic on another; only running it on
both revealed that.
