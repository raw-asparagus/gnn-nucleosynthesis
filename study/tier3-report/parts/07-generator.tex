\section{The label generator: bbq and MESA as operated}
\label{part:generator}

\textbf{What produced the data.} Every number in Tiers 0--2 is downstream of a
label that a Fortran program wrote to a text file. This node is what that
program is, what it was asked to do, and what the project did when it needed
data the shipped set did not contain.

% =====================================================================
\subsection{What a one-zone burner is --- and what it deliberately is not}
\label{sec:onezone}

A stellar-evolution code solves a coupled boundary-value problem over
${\sim}10^{3}$ zones. A \textbf{one-zone burner} throws all of that away and
keeps only step 4 of \S\ref{sec:mesastep}: given $(T, \rho, X)$ and a duration
$\Delta t$, integrate
%
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:onezone}
\frac{\dd\mathbf{Y}}{\dd t} \;=\; \nu\,\mathbf{R}(\mathbf{Y};\,T,\rho)
\end{equation}
%
at \textbf{constant} $T$ and $\rho$, and report the final composition plus the
integrated energy release.

What is deliberately absent, and why each absence is a modelling decision
rather than an oversight:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{4.6cm} Y@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{absent} & \textbf{consequence} \\
\midrule
hydrodynamics & no expansion/compression during the burn; no explosive-burning
  freeze-out (\S\ref{sec:alpharich}) \\
the energy equation feeding back on $T$ & \textbf{the burn does not heat the
  zone} --- no thermonuclear runaway, no self-consistent ignition \\
mixing & composition is local; no advection of fuel or ash \\
neighbouring zones & no shell structure; no gradient-driven anything \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

The third column of the argument is that this is \emph{exactly} the
operator-split sub-problem a stellar code solves (\S\ref{sec:mesastep}), so a
one-zone burner is not a simplified physics model --- it is a \textbf{faithful
reproduction of one call}. That is what makes it a legitimate label generator
for an emulator that will be dropped into that same call site.

\textbf{And the caveat that carries all the way to \S\ref{part:handoff}:} the
split's own error, and the $(T, \rho)$ feedback the split removes, are outside
everything measured here (\S\ref{sec:mesastep}, \foreignreg{2}{R-11}). An
emulator can be perfect on this data and still misbehave in the loop.

\subsection{bbq's four modes, and why only one made the shipped trajectories}
\label{sec:bbqmodes}

\code{bbq} (github.com/rjfarmer/bbq, pinned at commit 9783df31; \citealt{bbq}) is a thin
driver around MESA's \code{net} module. Its source has five \code{lib\_*.f90}
files --- a shared burn core \code{lib\_bbq.f90} plus one file per mode --- and
\code{main.f90:22--29} dispatches on exactly four flags
(\code{defaults/bbq.defaults:14--17}); choosing among them is the whole of
ADR~0005's first decision. (An earlier version of this table listed five modes
with two non-existent flags and mapped \code{use\_input\_file} to the shared
core; corrected in the 2026-08-18 audit.)

\begingroup\small
\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{2.3cm} L{2.9cm} Y L{4.0cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{mode (flag)} & \textbf{file} & \textbf{behaviour} &
  \textbf{can it produce a trajectory?} \\
\midrule
\code{use\_input\_file} & \code{lib\_sampler.f90}
  (\code{run\_sampler\_from\_file}) &
  burn each row of an input file \textbf{independently} from that row's own
  composition, for its own duration &
  \textbf{no} --- no state carry-over (\code{lib\_sampler.f90:71}) \\
\code{use\_random\_sampling} & \code{lib\_random.f90} &
  draw random $(T, \rho, \text{composition})$ states and burn each & no \\
\code{use\_profile} & \code{lib\_profile.f90} &
  burn a $(t, \log T, \log\rho)$ list \textbf{carrying composition forward}
  through the list (\code{:53--57}); header
  \code{age dt logt logrho <isos>} &
  \textbf{yes} --- the one mode that follows a \emph{non-constant} $(T, \rho)$
  track --- but it \textbf{writes no eps\_nuc} (\code{:128, 160--165}), and it
  was not used for any shipped file \\
\code{use\_hydrostatic} & \code{lib\_hydrostatic.f90} &
  burn a sequence of durations at constant $(T, \rho)$ \textbf{carrying
  composition forward}, one output row each; header
  \code{age dt eps\_nuc eps\_neu <isos>} (\code{:115}) &
  \textbf{yes} --- the mode that made every shipped trajectory \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
\endgroup

Only \code{use\_hydrostatic} gives all three of (i) composition carry-over
between rows, (ii) per-row \code{eps\_nuc}, and (iii) exact cadence control,
because each line of \code{times.txt} is a per-step burn \emph{duration}
(\code{use\_profile} gives (i) and (iii) but not (ii)). The shipped test
trajectories have \code{lib\_hydrostatic.f90}'s exact header ---
\code{age dt eps\_nuc eps\_neu <isos>} --- which is how the project knows which
mode generated them (bbq commit 9783df31, \code{src/main.f90:22--29},
\code{defaults/bbq.defaults:14--17}, \code{src/lib\_profile.f90:53--57, 128},
\code{src/lib\_hydrostatic.f90:115}, \code{src/lib\_sampler.f90:71};
ADR~0005).

The rejected alternative is instructive: \code{use\_input\_file} with cumulative
durations would burn each row from the \emph{initial} state rather than
continuing, so reproducing an $N$-row trajectory would need $N$ sequential bbq
invocations, each paying full network setup. That is not a small constant
factor --- network setup dominates a short burn.

\subsection{The inlist as a configuration surface}
\label{sec:inlist}

\code{scripts/bbq\_campaign/inlist.template} is short enough to read whole and
every line is a decision:

\begin{srclistingtiny}
&bbq
   net_name = '{net}.net'
   max_steps = 1000000
   eps = 1d-8            ! solver relative tolerance
   odescal = 1d-10       ! ODE scaling floor
   stptry = 0            ! 0 = MESA's 'try in one step'; r23.05.1 overrides stptry internally (net_burn.f90:254)
   use_hydrostatic = .true.
/
&hydrostatic
   logT = {logT}
   logRho = {logRho}
   times_from_file = .true.
   input_filename = 'times.txt'
   input_composition_filename = 'comp.txt'
   output_filename = 'output.txt'
/
&nuclear
   screening_mode = 'chugunov'
   use_suzuki_weak_rates = .false.
/
\end{srclistingtiny}

Three observations worth internalising.

\textbf{(i) \code{eps = 1e-8} is the tolerance the reference integrator
inherits.} ADR~0006 sets the project's own BDF integrator to rtol $= 10^{-8}$
explicitly \emph{because} that is bbq's \code{eps} --- so that disagreements
between the two are physics differences, not tolerance differences. Matching a
competitor's tolerance before comparing to it is a small discipline with a
large payoff.

\textbf{(ii) The \code{\&nuclear} block pins what the defaults would have given
anyway.} \code{screening\_mode} and \code{use\_suzuki\_weak\_rates} are written
explicitly even though they match bbq's defaults (\S\ref{sec:netsolver}). This
is the ``pin what you rely on'' habit: a future bbq version changing a default
would silently change the labels, and an explicit inlist turns that into a
diff.

\textbf{(iii) Constant (logT, logRho).} Each run is at a single point in the
regime box. There is no trajectory through $(T, \rho)$ --- the ``trajectory''
is only in composition (bbq's \code{use\_profile} mode could have followed a
$(T, \rho)$ track, but no shipped or campaign file used it). Everything this
project calls a trajectory is a \textbf{constant-$(T, \rho)$ relaxation}, which
is a specific and limited object; \S\ref{sec:twodistributions} is where that
limit becomes a measured problem.

\subsection{The shipped-label pipeline, reconstructed}
\label{sec:shippedpipeline}

The Zenodo dataset (record 14873443, 115~GB uncompressed, 3800 entries) was
produced by the NNN authors, and reconstructing their pipeline from the
artifacts was Steps 1--3 of this project. The reconstruction:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A Sobol grid over the regime box.} $2^{20} = 1{,}048{,}576$
  points in $(\log T, \log\rho, \Ye)$, generated by an \textbf{unseeded
  scrambled} \code{scipy.stats.qmc} Sobol sampler (\citealt{sobol1967}; SciPy,
  \citealt{virtanen2020} --- \code{Sobol(d)} defaults to
  \code{scramble=True, seed=None}; \citealt[\S 2.2]{grichener2025};
  \code{gridGenerator.py}). This is the single most consequential engineering
  fact in the dataset: \textbf{it is non-regenerable.} The shipped grid file is
  the only ground truth, and anything keyed to states must persist explicit
  \code{state\_id} lists (Tier~0 \S III.4/\S III.5).
\item \textbf{A composition per grid point}, constructed to hit the target
  \Ye. Not a physical composition --- random, and free-nucleon-loaded (median
  $X_{\mathrm{neut}} \approx 1.7\times10^{-2}$). \S\ref{sec:twodistributions}
  is the consequences.
\item \textbf{bbq runs} at each state for each of nine timesteps, yielding 18
  training CSVs (2 networks $\times$ 9 dt), 1,041,400 rows each.
\item \textbf{Test trajectories} --- 684 (\msn{80}) $+$ 824 (\msn{151}) $=$
  1508 constant-$(T, \rho)$ hydrostatic runs in total, shipped as
  \code{output\_T\_<logT>\_rho\_<logRho>.txt}.
\end{enumerate}

The audits that made this usable, all \resultsref{2026-07-08/09}:

\begingroup\small
\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{4.4cm} Y@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{audited fact} & \textbf{value} \\
\midrule
rows per training CSV & 1,041,400, identical across all 18 \\
the real dt grid & deviates from nominal by up to ${\sim}5\%$ and
  \textbf{differs between networks} (the ``1e2'' file is 105.08~s for
  \msn{80}, 102.93~s for \msn{151}) \\
row alignment across dt files & byte-identical (logT, logRho) arrays
  $\Rightarrow$ \code{state\_id} $=$ row index is a valid join key \\
(logT, logRho) as a join key & \textbf{rejected} --- 154,405 collisions from
  3-decimal rounding \\
missing Sobol rows & 7,176 (0.68\%), in \textbf{two contiguous blocks} at the
  head and tail of the sequence --- lost job-array batches, spatially
  quasi-uniform ($\chi^{2}/\mathrm{dof} \le 0.1$ on all marginals),
  \textbf{benign} \\
\code{final\_*} floor & exactly 1e-15, censored not physical \\
\epsnu{} normalisation & uniform $\div 10^{16}$ in all 18 files; the hinted
  alternate $10^{13}$ at dt $\ge 10$~s is \textbf{absent} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
\endgroup

That table is what ``read the dataset before trusting it'' produces. Every row
of it retired an assumption that would otherwise have become a silent bug: a
join on (logT, logRho) would have silently mixed 15\% of the rows; a nominal dt
would have introduced a 5\% error in every rate comparison; the 1e-15 floor
treated as data would have taught the model to predict a clamp.

\subsection{Why a rerun campaign was necessary}
\label{sec:campaign}

The shipped test trajectories have a defect that Step 5 discovered and Step 6
diagnosed: they \textbf{stall}. Composition freezes (max $|\Delta X|$ per
interval $< 10^{-10}$) from median age $2.2\times10^{5}$~s (\msn{80}) /
$3.9\times10^{4}$~s (\msn{151}), while \code{eps\_nuc} keeps \emph{rising}
\resultsref{2026-07-10}. \S\ref{sec:stall} is the anatomy; the operational
consequence was that only pre-stall rows could be used, which left too few
relaxed states to render a kill-test verdict on.

So Step 6 generated its own: \textbf{209 runs per network}, locally, with bbq.

ADR~0005 settles four points, and each is worth understanding as a
methodological choice:

\textbf{1. Mode: \code{use\_hydrostatic} with \code{times\_from\_file}} ---
\S\ref{sec:bbqmodes}.

\textbf{2. Cadence: 40 points per decade, log-spaced ages from $10^{-8}$~s, dt
capped at $10^{5}$~s}, to $t_{\mathrm{end}} = 10^{4}$~s ($\Tn \ge 5$),
$10^{6}$~s (3.3--5), $10^{8}$~s ($< 3.3$). This directly attacks the stall
mechanism: the shipped files used ${\sim}10$ points/decade, letting the output
dt grow to ${\sim}10^{10}$~s, at which point a single output interval is longer
than any physical timescale in the problem.

\textbf{3. MESA: stock r23.05.1, NOT 24.08.1.} This is the subtle one. Stock
r23.05.1 carries the \code{gh-575} multi-body-inverse bug (Tier~1 \S VI.6,
\S\ref{part:pathology}); 24.08.1 fixes it. Choosing the \emph{buggy} version
looks perverse until you state the goal: the reruns must be \textbf{comparable
to the shipped labels}, and the shipped labels were produced by r23.05.1-class
code. Choosing the fixed version would have produced better physics and a worse
experiment.

That choice was validated after the fact, and this is the part that makes it
defensible rather than lucky: stock r23.05.1 bbq reproduces the shipped labels
to \textbf{0.01 / 0.03 dex} on the two witness states \resultsref{2026-07-11},
and early-time agreement against shipped trajectories at identical
(logT, logRho, $X_{0}$) is 0.996 / 0.998 \resultsref{2026-07-11}. The reruns
are label-grade.

\textbf{4. Three composition families}, covering three different senses of ``a
state worth burning'':

\begingroup\small
\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{1.7cm} Y L{4.3cm} L{0.7cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{family} & \textbf{construction} & \textbf{purpose} & \textbf{n} \\
\midrule
\code{shipped} & row-0 state of each Step-5 selected shipped trajectory, at the
  file's exact (logT, logRho) & early-time comparability --- the control & 20 \\
\code{canonical} & two-isotope Si-burning-like mixes solving
  $\sum X_i Z_i/A_i = \Ye$: si28+si30 above $\Ye = 0.4667$, si30+ne22 below &
  \emph{physically plausible} fuel, unlike the Sobol compositions & 63 \\
\code{sobol} & nearest training-grid states per $(\Tn, \rho, \Ye)$ cell,
  \code{state\_id}s in the manifest & ties the relaxed manifold back to the
  training distribution & 126 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
\endgroup

A detail worth noticing, because it is the kind of thing that quietly breaks an
analysis: the $\Ye = 0.45$ target is \textbf{unreachable} with the three
Si-burning fuel species the family uses (\nuc{Si}{28}, \nuc{Si}{30},
\nuc{Ne}{22}), whose $Z/A$ floor is \nuc{Ne}{22} at 0.45455 --- the networks do
contain neutron-richer species (\nuc{O}{18}, $Z/A = 0.444$, in both nets, the
only such $A \ge 12$ species in \msn{80}; \msn{151} adds ${\sim}40$ more, e.g.\
\nuc{Si}{32}, \nuc{Ca}{48}, \nuc{Fe}{58}, \nuc{Ni}{64}) but none is
silicon-burning fuel (an earlier version called \nuc{Ne}{22} ``the network's
$Z/A$ floor''; corrected in the 2026-08-18 audit). The campaign substitutes
0.455 and \textbf{records the substitution in the manifest}. That is the
sourced/derived/assumed discipline applied to a composition.

\subsection{The campaign's coverage geometry --- and what 209 runs can and
            cannot say}
\label{sec:coverage}

The verdict rests on this campaign, so it is worth asking what it covers.

\textbf{The design grid} is 7 \Tn{} values $\times$ 3 $\log\rho$ $\times$ 3
\Ye{} $=$ 63 cells, populated by three families (\S\ref{sec:campaign}) to 209
runs per network. Compare with the space it is sampling:

\begingroup\small
\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{2.9cm} L{3.6cm} L{3.4cm} Y@{}}
\toprule
 & \textbf{campaign} & \textbf{training corpus} & \textbf{the box} \\
\midrule
\Tn & 7 discrete values & $2^{20}$ Sobol points, continuous &
  continuous 1.6--7.9 \\
$\log\rho$ & 3 discrete values & continuous & continuous 7--9 \\
\Ye & 3 targets (one substituted) & continuous & continuous 0.45--0.5 \\
compositions & 3 structured families & 1 random per state & --- \\
rows used in the verdict & $\le 24$ per trajectory, 4,693 total &
  1.04M states & --- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
\endgroup

Three honest observations.

\textbf{(i) The relaxed manifold is a lattice, not a sample.} Three densities
is enough to see a trend and not enough to fit one; the $\rho$-dependence of
every kill-test statistic is resolved at three points across two decades. Any
statement of the form ``$\kappa$ structure varies with density'' is an
interpolation between three values.

\textbf{(ii) The composition families are not a probability distribution.}
They are three \emph{purposes} --- comparability, plausibility, and
traceability to the training grid --- which is a good design for a diagnostic
and a bad one for an estimate. When \S\ref{part:killtest} reports ``the median
over the relaxed manifold'', the median is over a set whose composition mixture
was chosen by hand. This does not bias the \emph{structural} results (the empty
mask, $\kappa \equiv 1$ on weak columns) but it does mean the coverage
fractions of \S\ref{sec:gate2} are conditional on the family mix.

\textbf{(iii) Nothing checks representativeness against a real track.}
\foreignreg{2}{R-07} measured that a 20~\Msun{} track sits within 0.12--0.18
dex of a line in the box; the campaign samples a lattice over the whole box.
Whether the campaign's cells include the cells a real star actually visits ---
and with what weight --- was never checked. That is a half-hour of work given
both datasets exist, and it would convert ``the verdict on the relaxed
manifold'' into ``the verdict on the part of the manifold a star visits''.
Registered as \reg{T-13}, and \S\ref{sec:whichstars} / \reg{T-22} is the
question of whether \emph{one} track is enough to define ``visits''.

\textbf{What the campaign nevertheless establishes securely}, and it is a lot:
the structural results (weak $\kappa \equiv 1$, empty maskable set,
$\cond(\Sact)$, the bridge identities, the \rQSE{} negative verdict) are either
exact or so far from their thresholds that no plausible reweighting moves them.
The results that are genuinely conditional on the sampling design are the
\emph{fractions} --- coverage, low-$\kappa$ share, and the concentration shares
--- and those are exactly the ones \S\ref{sec:verdict} also flags for missing
error bars. The two caveats compound and should be quoted together.

\subsection{Execution}
\label{sec:execution}

\resultsref{2026-07-11}: \textbf{418 of 418 runs completed, zero failures.}
19,486~s wall on 10 workers $\approx$ 54 core-hours; \msn{80} $\approx$
2--3~min/run, \msn{151} $\approx$ 4--6~min at high \Tn, and low-\Tn{} runs
(walls at $10^{8}$~s, ${\sim}2600$ output rows) up to ${\sim}1$~h. 1.1~GB of
text output.

Two engineering points from \code{run\_campaign.py} and
\code{make\_campaign.py} worth carrying:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Completion is defined by an invariant, not by the exit code.} A
  run counts as complete iff bbq exits 0 \textbf{and} \code{output.txt} has
  exactly \code{n\_times + 1} rows. bbq aborts a hydrostatic run mid-way if a
  step hits \code{max\_steps}, exiting cleanly with a truncated file. Checking
  the row count is the difference between a validated campaign and a silently
  80\%-complete one.
\item \textbf{The queue is resumable and priority-ordered} (QSE window first,
  via \code{T9\_PRIORITY = [3.3, 4.0, 5.0, 6.3, 2.5, 1.6, 7.9]}), with a
  \code{DONE} sentinel per run directory. A 54-core-hour campaign that cannot
  resume is a campaign you will run twice.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Validation --- four checks, and what each was for}
\label{sec:validation}

\code{validate\_campaign.py} runs four checks. Reading them as a \emph{design}
is instructive, because each one is aimed at a specific way the campaign could
have been worthless.

\textbf{1. Stall census.} Terminal-guard census: 38/209 (\msn{80}) and 56/209
(\msn{151}) runs frozen before their wall, median frozen-run $\Tn = 7.9$ ---
fast arrival at the terminal attractor, which is legitimate. Frozen at
$\Tn < 3.3$: 9 and 7 runs, and these are \textbf{exactly} the canonical-family
inert-fuel cells at 1.6~GK (si28/si30/ne22 mixes cannot burn there).
Crucially: \emph{the shipped-class mid-burn output-dt artifact is absent} ---
every non-inert run either evolves at its stated wall or terminates at an
attractor. \resultsref{2026-07-11}. The cadence fix worked.

\textbf{2. Terminal $\Tn \ge 5$ states vs true NSE.} Median distance 8.6 / 9.0
dex, fraction within 0.05 dex $= 0.000$. This \emph{looks} like a failure and
is not --- it is the displaced pseudo-equilibrium the label-pathology rows
predict for stock MESA (\S\ref{part:pathology}). The check was designed to
\emph{report} rather than gate precisely because the expected answer was known
to be ``far from NSE, for a known reason''. A gate here would have failed the
campaign for being correct.

\textbf{3. Early-time comparability.} Per-pair species-age agreement within
$\max(5\%, 10^{-8})$: median 0.9963 / 0.9977, min 0.9508 / 0.9449 over 20
pairs per network; worst $|\Delta X|$ median $2.3\times10^{-3}$ /
$1.5\times10^{-3}$. Same code, same config, differing only in output cadence
--- the reruns are label-grade comparable.

\textbf{4. The \rQSE{} plateau, on clean data.} The Step-5 measurement had been
deferred on the grounds that the shipped trajectories were anomalous. On clean
rerun data: intra-group $\mathrm{std}(\rQSE)$ median \textbf{0.87 / 0.80 dex},
plateau rows ($< 0.1$ dex) \textbf{0 of 303 and 0 of 316} in the QSE window,
versus non-group spread 2.19 / 1.29 dex. \resultsref{2026-07-11}.

That fourth check deserves emphasis because of what it does: it takes a
deferral (``we cannot tell, the data is bad'') and converts it into a
\textbf{negative verdict on clean data}. The intra-Si-group \rQSE{} plateau of
\citet{hix1996} (their Si group, $A \approx 24$--45, is one of two QSE
clusters; the \emph{single}-group picture is \citet{bodansky1968}'s) does not
describe this manifold. Group-\emph{organised} structure exists --- non-group
spread is 1.5--2.5$\times$ the intra-group spread, which is what a GNN's
group-aware features would exploit --- but single-cluster \emph{algebraic
reduction} does not hold.

\begin{warn}
\textbf{How to read a deferral that comes back negative.} The honest reading is
narrow: the plateau is absent on \emph{the label manifold}, which is
constant-$(T, \rho)$, started from unphysical compositions, and (above 5~GK)
bug-displaced. It is not a statement about silicon burning in a star. The
project's own \foreignreg{2}{R-18} goes further and asks whether the \rQSE{}
diagnostic's null distribution is even known --- i.e.\ whether 0.8 dex of
intra-group spread is large. Until that is answered, the negative verdict is a
fact about a statistic whose scale has not been calibrated.
\end{warn}
