\subsection{Genuine gaps --- nothing in the plan covers these}
\label{sec:gaps}

\begin{warn}
\warnglyph{}~Ordered by how much they could change a project conclusion.
\end{warn}

\subsubsection{Rate-uncertainty \texorpdfstring{$\to$}{->} \texorpdfstring{\Ye}{Ye} sensitivity propagation}
\label{sec:gap-sensitivity}

\textbf{The gap.} \S\ref{sec:uncertainty} establishes factor-${\sim}2$
Hauser--Feshbach uncertainties and GT-placement-dependent shell-model weak ones
(no blanket figure; key FFN$\to$LMP EC revisions were factors
${\sim}10$--350). The project has a \Ye{} \emph{physics floor}
($5\times10^{-3}$--$1.5\times10^{-2}$ per trajectory) anchored on the
FFN$\to$LMP difference. But there is no node that computes
\textbf{$\delta\Ye/\delta\ln\lambda_r$} --- the sensitivity of the target
observable to each rate.

\textbf{Why it matters.} It is the missing denominator for every gate.
``Reaction $r$ carries 40\% of \dYe'' and ``$\lambda_r$ is known to a factor 2''
together determine whether $r$ deserves emulator accuracy at all. It would also
give the kill-test a second, independent importance measure to cross-check the
flux-based active set --- one that is about \emph{physical} importance rather
than \emph{numerical} magnitude.

\textbf{Cost.} Cheap: the compiled engine already evaluates all rates in batch,
so a one-at-a-time log-perturbation over 607 columns on the trajectory set is a
straightforward run. Nothing new needs building.

\subsubsection{Ground-state vs stellar forward rates (the SEF question)}
\label{sec:gap-sef}

\textbf{The gap.} \S\ref{sec:sef}, at the narrower scope established there. The
\code{ths8r} statistical-model forwards must be stellar rates ---
\eqref{eq:dbratio} is a relation between stellar rates, so the project's
mandated \code{DerivedRate(use\_pf=True)} construction already presupposes it.
What is genuinely open is the \textbf{experimentally-fitted subset}
(\code{nac2}, \code{ks03} and similar), which are laboratory ground-state rates
by construction and to which no SEF is visibly applied anywhere.

\textbf{Why it matters.} Those channels carry an unapplied $O(1)$
temperature-dependent multiplier. It cancels in forward\,/\,reverse ratios ---
so $\kappa$, the equilibrium mask, and the kill-test verdict are all safe ---
but it does \textbf{not} cancel in $\Delta Y$ magnitudes or \enuc. It is
precisely the class of error that passes every consistency test in the
repository.

\textbf{Cost.} Now mostly mechanical rather than a literature question: tally
the label classes over both networks, weight by flux carried in the box, and
check Rauscher's SEF tables \citep{rauscher2000} for whatever survives that
filter. Hours, not days --- and the expected answer is ``negligible exposure'',
since the $\alpha$ ladder and the Fe-peak $\alpha$-captures are \code{ths8r}
throughout, with the single exception of
$\nuc{Ca}{40}(\alpha,\gamma)\nuc{Ti}{44}$ (label \code{chw0}, a resonance-based
re-evaluation --- one named channel for the tally to check;
\S\ref{sec:sef}).

\subsubsection{Neutrino losses in the flux route (the dropped NU column)}
\label{sec:gap-nu}

\textbf{The gap.} \S\ref{sec:weak-screening}. \code{compile.py} builds only
\code{TableIndex.RATE}; the tabulated neutrino energy-loss rate (column 6) is
discarded, though pynucastro exposes it as \code{get\_nu\_loss}.

\textbf{Why it matters.} Invariant \#5 requires flux-route vs
composition-route \enuc{} agreement to $\le1\%$, and the trajectory-file
\code{eps\_nuc} is \emph{net of neutrino losses}. (Status: the 2026-07-10
UNRESOLVED flux-route-vs-bbq row was retired by the 2026-07-11 \code{eps\_nuc}
convention pin --- a units\,/\,convention mismatch, not an engine error
\resultsref{2026-07-11}; invariant-\#5 proper passed 2026-07-12 with the
constant-$Q$ caveat of \S\ref{sec:qoft} \resultsref{2026-07-12}.) The gap itself
stands: the engine still cannot compute $\nu$ losses --- the pin leaned on the
labels' own \code{eps\_neu} column --- so a missing loss term remains something
nobody can test flux-route-side, because the quantity is not compiled.

\textbf{Cost.} Small: one more \code{tab\_*} array and a second bilinear pass.

\subsubsection{Imposed \texorpdfstring{$(T, \rho)$}{(T, rho)} versus thermodynamic feedback}
\label{sec:gap-feedback}

\textbf{The gap.} bbq runs one-zone at fixed $(T, \rho)$; the labels are
$\Delta$-composition at imposed thermodynamic state. Tier~0 \S IV.2 analyses the
\textbf{compositional} reachable manifold beautifully. Nothing analyses the
\textbf{thermal} one.

\textbf{Why it matters.} In a real star $\varepsilon_{\rm nuc}$ heats the zone,
$T$ rises, photodisintegration rates rise steeply (\S\ref{sec:why3gk}:
$Q/kT \approx 27$ from the $e^{-Q/kT}$ factor alone; the full fitted slope
$\dd\ln\lambda_\gamma/\dd\ln T \approx 35$ at $\Tn = 3$), and the composition
responds. Deployment couples the emulator into exactly that loop. Two specific
questions: (i) does the operator-splitting error analysis (\S IV.1) still bound
the error when $T$ is \emph{changing} over the step rather than fixed? (ii) is
the Sobol box's $(T, \rho)$ coverage adequate for the trajectories a coupled run
actually traverses, given that self-heating makes $T$ a fast variable near
ignition?

\textbf{Cost.} Analysis, not a new measurement. But it deserves a written
section, because it is the assumption that most directly limits the
deliverable.

\subsubsection{Nuclear mass and Q-value provenance}
\label{sec:gap-masses}

\textbf{The gap.} The DB-inverse band (0.05\,dex) is justified partly by
``mass\,/\,$Q$-table provenance'' differing between MESA's \code{winvn} and
pynucastro's tables. No node explains what those tables are, how they differ, or
how large the induced $Q$ differences actually are.

\textbf{Why it matters.} $Q$ enters the DB ratio as $e^{-Q/kT}$ with $Q/kT$ up
to several hundred (\S\ref{sec:oracle-compile}). A $\Delta Q$ of 10\,keV at
$kT = 0.26$\,MeV is a 4\% rate difference; a 100\,keV difference is 47\%.
\textbf{The band's justification is currently qualitative for a quantity with
exponential leverage.} I measured one instance in \S\ref{sec:db-ratio} ---
pynucastro's internal $Q$ vs the library $Q$ differ by
${\sim}1.6\times10^{-4}$\,MeV --- which is harmless, but that is one channel,
not a survey.

\textbf{Cost.} Cheap and mechanical: dump $Q$ per reaction from both sides (the
probe's \code{dump\_net} already returns \code{q}), histogram the difference,
convert to dex at box temperatures.

\subsubsection{Electron--positron pairs at the hot, low-density corner}
\label{sec:gap-pairs}

\textbf{The gap --- and it is not the small one it looks like.} The screening
module computes $n_e = \rho\Ye/m_u$, the \textbf{net} electron density. At
$\Tn = 7.9$, $kT = 0.68$\,MeV against $2m_ec^2 = 1.02$\,MeV, so thermal pair
production is not negligible. I did the estimate this item used to defer.
Solving finite-temperature charge neutrality
$n_-(\muE) - n_+(\muE) = \rho\Ye/m_u$ over the box \derivedhere{}:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lllrrr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{$\rho$} & \textbf{\Ye} & \textbf{\Tn} & \textbf{\muE{} (MeV)}
  & \textbf{$n_+/n_{\rm net}$} & \textbf{$(n_-+n_+)/n_{\rm net}$} \\
\midrule
$10^7$ & 0.498 & 7.9 & 0.161 & \textbf{1.81} & \textbf{4.62} \\
$10^7$ & 0.498 & 5.0 & 0.412 & 0.20 & 1.40 \\
$10^8$ & 0.500 & 7.9 & 1.210 & 0.041 & 1.08 \\
$10^9$ & 0.450 & 7.9 & 3.598 & $1.4\times10^{-4}$ & 1.000 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\textbf{At the hot, thin corner the positrons outnumber the net electrons}, and
the total charged-lepton density is $4.6\times$ the number the code uses. It is
nowhere near ``below a percent''.

\textbf{Why it matters.} Two things, and the second is larger than the one this
item was originally about:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Pairs add to the \emph{polarisable} lepton density while leaving \Ye{}
      unchanged, so $\Gamma_e$ keyed on net $n_e$ is low by
      $4.62^{1/3} \approx 1.66\times$ there. Bounded in consequence:
      $\Gamma$(Si--Si) at that corner is only 0.40, and for the $\alpha/p$
      captures the network actually carries ($\Gamma_{12} \le 0.21$) the
      enhancement is ${\sim}1.03$--$1.16\times$ either way. The rate error is
      real but not large.
\item \textbf{$\muE = 0.16$\,MeV $< m_ec^2$ means the electrons there are not
      degenerate at all.} That undercuts more than the screening call:
      \S\ref{sec:ec-degenerate}'s cold-degenerate $E_F$ table, the $\muE^5$
      scaling, and \code{chugunov\_2007}'s rigid-background premise all assume
      degeneracy. The corner is outside the design regime of three separate
      pieces of machinery at once, and \S\ref{sec:ec-degenerate} now says so.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Status.} Estimate done; the item is no longer ``probably fine, go
check''. What remains is a \emph{decision}: either state the hot-thin corner as
an explicit validity boundary on the screening and weak prescriptions, or
exclude it from the sampling box. It also bears on the EOS and on the neutrino
losses. This is the cleanest illustration in the whole list of why ``probably
fine'' at a box corner deserves the ten minutes.

\subsubsection{Network-reduction and reaction-importance prior art}
\label{sec:gap-reduction}

\textbf{The gap.} Tier~0 \S VI.5 surveys prior art in \emph{ML} acceleration.
The kill-test is not an ML method --- it is a reaction-importance\,/\,%
network-reduction study, a field with decades of literature (integrated reaction
flows, principal-component and directed-relation-graph reduction in combustion,
adaptive networks in astrophysics). None of it is surveyed.

\textbf{Why it matters.} Two risks. First, novelty: the \code{novelty-checker}
sweeps arXiv for ML-emulator work; a reduction-methods claim could be
anticipated in a literature nobody is sweeping. Second, method: combustion
chemistry solved ``which reactions matter, adaptively'' long ago, and their
importance measures (DRG, \citealp{lu2005}; DRGEP,
\citealp{pepiot2008}; computational singular perturbation,
\citealp{lam1994}) are directly analogous to the $\kappa$\,/\,active-set
machinery. Reinventing them is a waste; citing them is free.

\subsubsection{Isomers, and what a ground-state-only network omits}
\label{sec:gap-isomers}

\textbf{The gap.} \code{canonical.py} deliberately does not map \code{al26-1} /
\code{al26-2}, on the grounds that they are absent from both networks. True, but
the \emph{physics} consequence is unstated: a ground-state-only treatment
assumes isomeric states are either in thermal equilibrium with the ground state
or irrelevant.

\textbf{Why it matters.} Probably genuinely irrelevant here (\nuc{Al}{26}
matters for galactic $\gamma$-ray astronomy, not Si-burning \Ye, and at
$kT \approx 0.3$\,MeV thermal coupling is fast). But it is an assumption,
currently unwritten, and the fail-loud whitelist means an isomer appearing in a
future network raises rather than silently mis-maps --- so the code is safe and
only the \emph{documentation} is missing.

\subsubsection{Coulomb-corrected NSE, quantified in-box}
\label{sec:gap-nse}

\textbf{The gap.} \S\ref{sec:nse-coulomb} explains \emph{why}
\code{use\_coulomb\_corr=False} is right for the $\kappa$ screen. Nobody has
measured \textbf{how different the Coulomb-corrected NSE composition is} at box
conditions, where $\Gamma_e Z^{5/3}$ is large for Fe-peak $Z$.

\textbf{Why it matters.} The $\kappa$ screen's choice is correct regardless. But
S9's QSE work, and any comparison of a computed NSE state against a MESA/bbq
attractor, needs to know whether the ideal-gas NSE is the right reference ---
and at $\rho = 10^9$ with $Z \approx 26$,
$\Gamma = \Gamma_e\cdot26^{5/3} \approx 0.045 \times 228 \approx \mathbf{10}$ at
$\Tn = 4$, rising to $\approx 26$ at the box's cold corner \derivedhere{}. Order
10, not $10^2$ --- and consistent with \S\ref{sec:boxgamma}'s 0.4--25 range
rather than in tension with it. Still firmly out of the weak-coupling regime, so
the item stands; it is the \emph{magnitude} of the claim that needed correcting,
not the conclusion.

\textbf{Cost.} Two calls to \code{get\_comp\_nse} with the flag flipped, at a
few box corners. Minutes.

\subsubsection{Gate-design statistics}
\label{sec:gap-gatestats}

\textbf{The gap.} Gates are stated as point thresholds on medians\,/\,p90s over
63-state grids or 30k subsamples, with no statement of sampling uncertainty.
\S\ref{sec:reading-stats} covers how to \emph{read} the comparison statistics;
nothing covers how to \emph{choose} the sample size or express confidence.

\textbf{Why it matters.} Lowest priority of the ten: most gates here are
separated from their floors by many orders of magnitude ($\kappa$
$2.6\times10^{-12}$ against a 0.1 threshold), so sampling noise is irrelevant.
It matters only for the near-run gates --- the $2\times$ size-transfer falsifier
and the $3\times$ Sobol trigger, both of which compare two measured
distributions and could plausibly land near their thresholds.

\subsection{The habit that finds these: common-mode blindness}
\label{sec:commonmode}

Every consistency test in this tier is a test of \textbf{mutual} consistency ---
forward against reverse, pynucastro against MESA, vectorised against scalar,
rate side against equilibrium side. That is enormously powerful: it is how the
v-flag $\kappa$ floor and gh-575 were both caught with no ground truth at all.

It is also, by construction, blind to \textbf{common-mode} error: anything
identical on both sides of the comparison.

Look at what that predicts, then check it against the list:

\begin{center}

\toprule

\midrule

  an unapplied stellar enhancement cancels in every ratio \\

\S\ref{sec:gap-feedback} (imposed $T$, $\rho$) &

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{$Q(T)$}{Q(T)} --- specified, unimplemented, unmeasurable by the current gate}
\label{sec:gap-qoft}

Fully worked in \S\ref{sec:qoft}. Summary: the energy head is specified with
$Q_j(T)$; it is not implemented; the $\le1\%$ invariant-\#5 gate uses identical
constant $Q$ on both sides and therefore cannot detect its absence; and the
per-channel correction reaches \textbf{$-19.2\%$ on
$\nuc{Ca}{40}(\alpha,\gamma)\nuc{Ti}{44}$ at $\Tn = 7.9$} and $-8.4\%$ in the
QSE window. Needs one convention decision (does MESA/bbq's EOS carry nuclear
excitation energy?) and a three-line implementation using splines that already
exist (\S\ref{sec:pf-spline}).

\subsubsection{Solver-independent ground truth (SkyNet, WinNet)}
\label{sec:gap-thirdsolver}

The PhD-plan report calls for bbq\,/\,MESA as primary ``cross-checked against
SkyNet and WinNet for solver-independent validation''. Nothing in the study map,
the code, or \code{RESULTS.md} touches either.

\textbf{Why it matters, sharply.} \S\ref{sec:uncertainty} and
\S\ref{sec:gap-sef} establish that this project can only make
\emph{fidelity-to-MESA} claims. S6 verified MESA against pynucastro --- but
pynucastro is a \emph{rate library}, not an independent integrator, and both
sides read REACLIB. \textbf{There is currently no independent check of the
integrated dynamics at all.} That matters most where MESA is known to be wrong:
the gh-575-displaced label attractors (S13). A third solver is the only
instrument that distinguishes ``the labels are pathological'' from ``our reading
of the labels is pathological''.

\subsubsection{Determinism, and the float32/float64 boundary}
\label{sec:gap-determinism}

Two connected items, both absent.

\textbf{Determinism.} The conservation gate asserts column drifts of
\emph{exactly} 0.0 and bounds at $10^{-12}$--$10^{-13}$. Floating-point
reductions are not associative, so a threaded or GPU reduction's result depends
on thread count and scheduling. The repo already carries BLAS thread-pinning
(commit \code{1cb6f47}), which suggests the issue has been felt. Nothing states
the contract: \textbf{is the conservation gate reproducible bit-for-bit across
thread counts and devices, and if not, what is the guaranteed bound?}

\textbf{Precision boundary.} The spec says inference in float32 with a float64
cast for the $X_{t+\Delta t}$ update, and separately flags as load-bearing that
``whether even $\asinh$ of a genuinely tiny post-cancellation net flux loses
precision --- is unproven''. Tier~0 \S V.4 covers floating point generically;
\S\ref{sec:lambda-range} here measures 75 decades of $\lambda$. Nobody has put
the two together: \textbf{float32 has ${\sim}7$ decimal digits and a
${\sim}10^{-38}$ normal floor; a post-cancellation net flux at
$\kappa = 10^{-6}$ of a gross flux is exactly the quantity that boundary
destroys.} This is a computable, decidable question and it gates the
architecture's precision plan.

\subsubsection{Gradients through the conservation map}
\label{sec:gap-gradients}

Target A decodes $\varphi$ then applies fixed $\nu$; Target B applies the
projector $P = I - C\Tr(CC\Tr)^{-1}C$. Both are fixed linear maps, so the
backward pass is multiplication by $\nu\Tr$ and $P\Tr = P$ respectively.
Consequences nobody has written down:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$P$ annihilates gradient in the constrained directions exactly.}
      Any loss component that depends on the violating part of $\dd Y$ produces
      \emph{zero} gradient --- which is the point, but it means the network
      receives no signal about three directions of its output space, and
      $\cond(CC\Tr) = 3.9\times10^4 / 1.0\times10^5$ (measured,
      \code{RESULTS.md} 2026-07-08) says those directions are not equally
      conditioned.
\item \textbf{$\nu$ has nullity 1} (measured, same row). So for Target A there
      is a one-dimensional family of $\varphi$ producing identical $\dd Y$: the
      flux head is \emph{unidentifiable} along it, and nothing in the loss
      selects a member. Whether that harms training, or merely leaves a harmless
      gauge freedom, is unexamined --- and it interacts with the flux-magnitude
      regularisation the mask implies.
\end{itemize}

Neither is exotic ML; both are two-line linear algebra on matrices already
exported. Genuinely prerequisite, genuinely absent.

\subsubsection{The deployment coupling contract}
\label{sec:gap-coupling}

\S\ref{sec:gap-feedback} raised imposed $(T, \rho)$. The PhD-plan report is
broader, listing ``coupling-specific gates added by audit'': energy-generation
error feeding back into the structure solve, behaviour across the NSE
transition, composition coupling in convective zones, and Sobol$\to$real-track
transfer. Only the last has a node (S1\,/\,\S III.9, and checklist row 13).

\textbf{The one to name specifically is the NSE transition.} MESA switches to an
NSE solver above some temperature; the emulator would have to either reproduce
that switch or be handed off across it. There is no node for the handoff
contract, and S13's finding --- that the labels sit at bug-displaced
pseudo-equilibria at $\Tn \gtrsim 5$ --- is precisely a statement about what
happens near that switch.

\subsubsection{Residual sourcing gaps in the motivation chain}
\label{sec:gap-sourcing}

Three open checklist items are \emph{sourcing} rather than measurement, and all
three sit in the chain Tier~0 Part~I uses to justify the project:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Farmer 2016's ``30\%/10\%''} --- is it $\eta = 1-2\Ye$ or relative
      \Ye? The numbers themselves are now located verbatim in the paper's
      abstract (``$\approx$30\% variations in the central electron fraction
      \ldots\ a minimum of $\approx$127 isotopes is needed to attain convergence
      of these values at the $\approx$10\% level''; \citealp{farmer2016}) ---
      but the \emph{metric} the percentages refer to is still not defined
      anywhere in the paper. The paper's own tables constrain the reading: the
      $\eta = 1-2\Ye$ interpretation is quantitatively consistent ($\eta$
      spreads cluster around $\approx$30\%) while relative \Ye{} is not
      (6--11\%) --- so the interpretation half of the gap stands. It is an
      interpretation of the \emph{observational} leverage of \Ye, i.e.\ the
      number that says why the project matters.
\item \textbf{$\langle E_\nu\rangle$ per reaction} for the neutrino head.
      Distinct from \S\ref{sec:gap-nu}'s dropped NU column: that is a tabulated
      \emph{loss rate}, this is a \emph{mean energy per capture} needed for
      $\varepsilon_\nu = \sum\langle E_\nu\rangle_j\varphi_j$. Open since
      Tier~0 \S0.4.3.
\item \textbf{Which \Ye-controllers each network contains}, specifically the
      neutron-rich $\beta$-decay partners \nuc{Fe}{61} and \nuc{Co}{61,63}.
      \S\ref{sec:weak-asymmetry} gives the census by count; the per-nuclide
      membership question for those three is still open in the checklist.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Promoted out of that bucket: \S\ref{sec:gap-pairs} ($e^\pm$ pairs).} It
was filed as a probably-fine corner check. The estimate is now done and it is
not fine: at $\Tn = 7.9$, $\rho = 10^7$ the positrons outnumber the net
electrons and the electrons are non-degenerate, so three separate prescriptions
--- \code{chugunov\_2007}'s rigid background, \S\ref{sec:ec-degenerate}'s
cold-degenerate $E_F$, and the $\muE^5$ scaling --- are all outside their design
regime at that corner simultaneously. What remains is a \emph{decision} (declare
a validity boundary, or trim the box), not a measurement, so it belongs with
items 6--8 rather than here.

\textbf{The pattern worth naming.} Items 1, 3, 5 and 9 are all the same shape: a
quantity or convention that is \emph{identical on both sides} of every check the
project runs. \S\ref{sec:commonmode}'s habit --- \emph{ask of any green test what
would still be wrong if it passed} --- is what surfaced all four.
