      \code{derived\_from\_inverse}, \code{weak\_mask}, \code{weak\_type},
      \code{chapter}, and $Q$ all come out of this tier and become columns of

      $R_j = \text{prefactor}\cdot\rho^{\rm densexp}\cdot\prod Y\cdot\lambda$
      \eqref{eq:grossflux} is the definition of gross flux; the pair map,
      $f^+/f^-$, and $\kappa$ are all built on the forward\,/\,reverse structure
      derived in \S\ref{part:db}; and $\kappa$'s two readings --- DB test
      (\S\ref{sec:kappa-db}) and condition number (\S V.1 of Tier~0) --- meet
      here.
\item \textbf{To S9 (NSE/QSE):} the same partition functions, spins, and masses
      feed both the rate side (\S\ref{sec:pf-in-ratio}) and the Saha side
      (\code{qse/coeffs.py}); their consistency is what makes $\kappa$-at-NSE a
      meaningful test at all (\S\ref{sec:pf-spline}). \S\ref{sec:crossover}'s
      crossover table is the mechanism of QSE onset.
\item \textbf{To S13 (the label pathology):} \S\ref{sec:gh575}'s derivation is
      the tool that later identifies gh-575 \emph{in the shipped labels}.
\item \textbf{To S14/S15 (kill-test and target):} the active-set threshold
      $\kappa > 0.1$ is evaluated on \textbf{unscreened} $\kappa$ from
      \textbf{pf-corrected} reverses. Both qualifications are Tier-1 results,
      and either one violated invalidates the verdict.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{The three ideas to carry, if you carry nothing else}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{The three ideas to carry}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The reverse rate is where the physics and the bugs both live.}
      Forwards are bit-identical between two independent codes; every
      consequential disagreement in this project --- pf, gh-575, snapshot
      construction swaps --- is about a reverse. And \S\ref{sec:crossover} shows
      why: near equilibrium the reverse \emph{is} the answer.
\item \textbf{A multiplicative error $\varepsilon$ in $f^-$ becomes
      $\kappa \approx \varepsilon/2$ at equilibrium.} One line of algebra that
      unifies the v-flag floor (\S\ref{sec:vflag}), the screening offset
      (\S\ref{sec:screened-kappa}), and the $\kappa$-as-condition-number reading
      of Tier~0 \S V.1. If you can write this down cold, most of Tier~1
      reconstructs itself.
\item \textbf{Every threshold in this tier sits just above a measured floor.}
      $5\times10^{-12}$ (above $1.3\times10^{-12}$ fp association), 0.004\,dex
      (above bit-identical), 0.05\,dex (above the pf drift),
      $\kappa < 10^{-9}$ (above $2.6\times10^{-12}$). None is a round number
      chosen for comfort. When you meet a new gate, ask what floor it sits above
      --- and if there isn't one, that is the finding.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{And one habit, from the open register}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{And one habit}

The tier's tests are all \textbf{mutual}-consistency tests, which is their
strength (no ground truth needed) and their blind spot (common-mode error
survives them). Five of the highest-priority open items in
\S\ref{sec:priority} are common-mode --- worked in \S\ref{sec:commonmode}.

\begin{invariant}
\textbf{Ask of any green test: what would still be wrong if it passed?}
\end{invariant}
