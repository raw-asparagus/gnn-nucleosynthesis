\subsection{Why screening exists}
\label{sec:whyscreening}

Everything in \S\ref{sec:gamow} assumed two \emph{bare} nuclei approaching
through vacuum. In a plasma each nucleus carries a polarisation cloud: electrons
(and, at strong coupling, a correlated ion distribution) pile up around positive
charge. A second nucleus approaching sees a \textbf{partially neutralised}
target, so the Coulomb barrier is lower and the tunnelling probability higher.

Because the rate depends exponentially on the barrier, a \emph{small} potential
change is a \emph{large} rate change. Define the enhancement factor
%
\begin{equation*}
f_{\rm scr} = \frac{\sigv_{\rm plasma}}{\sigv_{\rm bare}}
= e^{h}, \qquad h \equiv \ln f_{\rm scr} \ge 0
\end{equation*}

\begin{warn}
\textbf{Convention warning, stated once and enforced by the code:} pynucastro's
screening functions return \textbf{$h = \ln(\text{factor})$}, MESA returns the
\textbf{factor}. The cross-check module exponentiates before comparing, with a
comment saying so, and the vectorised module's docstring states it in its first
Conventions bullet. This is a genuine trap: both are dimensionless numbers near
1 for weak screening, so a missing \code{exp} produces plausible-looking wrong
answers.
\end{warn}

\subsection{Weak screening: Debye--H\"uckel from scratch}
\label{sec:debye}

Linearise Poisson--Boltzmann around a test charge $Z_1e$. With ion densities
$n_i \propto \exp(-Z_ie\phi/kT) \approx n_i(1 - Z_ie\phi/kT)$,
%
\begin{equation*}
\nabla^2\phi = -4\pi\rho_{\rm ch}
= \frac{4\pi e^2}{kT}\Bigl(\sum_i n_i Z_i^2 + n_e\Bigr)\phi
\equiv \frac{\phi}{\lambda_D^2}
\end{equation*}
%
giving the screened potential $\phi = (Z_1e/r)e^{-r/\lambda_D}$ with
%
\begin{equation*}
\lambda_D = \left(\frac{kT}{4\pi e^2\bigl(\sum_i n_i Z_i^2 + n_e\bigr)}\right)^{1/2}
\end{equation*}
%
The reacting pair must approach to $r \ll \lambda_D$, where
%
\begin{equation*}
U(r) = \frac{Z_1Z_2e^2}{r}e^{-r/\lambda_D}
\approx \frac{Z_1Z_2e^2}{r} - \frac{Z_1Z_2e^2}{\lambda_D}
\end{equation*}
%
--- the barrier is \textbf{uniformly lowered by a constant}
$Z_1Z_2e^2/\lambda_D$. Since the tunnelling integral responds to the barrier and
the Boltzmann factor to the energy, the net effect is a constant multiplicative
enhancement:
%
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;h_{\rm weak} = \frac{Z_1Z_2 e^2}{\lambda_D\,kT}\;}
\label{eq:debye}
\end{equation}
%
Salpeter's classic result \citep[eq.~21]{salpeter1954}. Two things to notice,
both of which matter for what follows:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$h$ depends on $Z_1Z_2$, not on the reaction.} Screening is a
      property of the \emph{entering pair}, not of the exit channel. This is the
      seed of the \S\ref{sec:screened-kappa} asymmetry.
\item \textbf{$h = 0$ whenever either charge is zero.} Neutron-induced reactions
      are unscreened, exactly. So are photodisintegrations --- a single nucleus
      and a photon have no pair to screen.
\end{itemize}

\begin{warn}
\warnglyph{}~\textbf{The electron term is not innocent, and the code does not
use it.} Writing the screening sum as $(\sum_i n_iZ_i^2 + n_e)$ treats the
electrons as a \emph{classical} Boltzmann gas --- the same $1/kT$ linearisation
applied to the ions. \S\ref{sec:ec-degenerate} will establish that over most of
this box the electrons are \textbf{degenerate}, and a degenerate electron sea
barely polarises: its contribution is set by $\dd n_e/\dd\muE$, not by $n_e/kT$,
and it vanishes in the strongly degenerate limit. The correct weak-coupling
limit for the regime the code targets is therefore the \textbf{ion-only} Debye
length, i.e.\ a rigid neutralising electron background (the one-component
plasma).
\end{warn}

This is not a quibble about a 7\% term: it is what makes
\S\ref{sec:chugunov}'s check work. Dropping $n_e$ from the sum, \eqref{eq:debye}
collapses \textbf{exactly} onto $\sqrt3\,\Gamma_{12}^{3/2}$, which is the
asymptote \code{chugunov\_2007}'s $A_3$ is defined to reproduce. Keeping the
classical electron term instead gives $\sqrt{(Z+1)/Z}\cdot\sqrt3\,\Gamma^{3/2}$
(a 3.5\% excess for Si), and the boundary condition no longer closes.
\derivedhere{} So read \eqref{eq:debye} as the pedagogical two-component result,
and read \S\ref{sec:chugunov}'s $\sqrt3$ as the OCP one --- they are different
limits, and the code implements the second.

Validity: the linearisation requires the interaction energy between neighbours
to be small compared with $kT$, i.e.\ weak coupling.

\subsection{The coupling parameter \texorpdfstring{$\Gamma$}{Gamma} and the strong-screening limit}
\label{sec:gamma}

Define the ion-sphere radius and the \textbf{Coulomb coupling parameter}
%
\begin{equation}
a_e = \left(\frac{3}{4\pi n_e}\right)^{1/3},
\qquad
\Gamma_{12} = \frac{Z_1Z_2e^2}{\ztil\,a_e\,kT},
\qquad
\ztil = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(Z_1^{1/3}+Z_2^{1/3}\bigr)
\label{eq:gammadef}
\end{equation}
%
$\Gamma$ is the ratio of typical Coulomb energy to thermal energy.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$\Gamma \ll 1$}: weak coupling, Debye--H\"uckel \eqref{eq:debye}
      valid.
\item \textbf{$\Gamma \gg 1$}: the ion is inside a correlated ion sphere; the
      enhancement grows only \textbf{linearly}, $h \propto \Gamma$ (ion-sphere /
      Salpeter strong screening), rather than as $\Gamma^{3/2}$ --- what
      saturates is the logarithmic slope, not $h$ itself --- and the physics is a
      \emph{free-energy difference} between the separated ions and the fused
      compound, not a potential shift.
\item \textbf{$\Gamma \sim 1$}: neither asymptotic form is valid; you need a fit
      to Monte-Carlo free energies of the one-component plasma. \textbf{This is
      where the box lives.}
\end{itemize}

The code's $\Gamma$ is exactly \eqref{eq:gammadef}. Read
\code{screening.py:47-49} and \code{:110}:

\begin{srclisting}
gamma_e_fac = constants.q_e**2 / constants.k * np.cbrt(4*np.pi/3) * np.cbrt(n_e)
...
Gamma = gamma_e_fac * z1 * z2 / (ztilde * T_norm * T_p)      # T_norm*T_p == T
\end{srclisting}

\code{gamma\_e\_fac} $= e^2(4\pi n_e/3)^{1/3}/k = e^2/(a_e k)$, so
$\Gamma = \text{gamma\_e\_fac}\cdot Z_1Z_2/(\ztil T)$. That is
\eqref{eq:gammadef} term for term.

\subsubsection{The strong-screening limit, derived as a free-energy difference}
\label{sec:strongscreening}

Worth doing, because it is a \emph{different kind} of argument from
\S\ref{sec:debye} and the difference is the whole reason a single formula has to
interpolate.

At $\Gamma \gg 1$ each ion sits in a neutralising sphere of radius
$a_i = (3Z_i/4\pi n_e)^{1/3}$. The electrostatic energy of one such ion sphere
is the classic \citep[eq.~28]{salpeter1954}
%
\begin{equation*}
E_{\rm sphere}(Z) = -\frac{9}{10}\frac{Z^2e^2}{a_i} \;\propto\; -Z^{5/3}
\end{equation*}
%
Screening enhancement is then the \textbf{change in Coulomb free energy on
fusing} the two reactants into the compound nucleus, since the tunnelling
happens at a separation small compared with $a_i$:
%
\begin{equation*}
h_{\rm strong} = \frac{E(Z_1)+E(Z_2)-E(Z_1+Z_2)}{kT}
\;\simeq\; \frac{9}{10}\,\Gamma_e\Bigl[(Z_1+Z_2)^{5/3}-Z_1^{5/3}-Z_2^{5/3}\Bigr],
\qquad \Gamma_e = \frac{e^2}{a_e kT}
\end{equation*}
%
--- note the $9/10$ is the \emph{same} $9/10$ as in $E_{\rm sphere}$; nothing
else enters. Two cross-checks, both worth doing once \derivedhere{}:

\begin{itemize}
\item In cgs this is the classic Salpeter form \citep[eq.~29]{salpeter1954},
      $h = 0.205\,(\rho/\mu_e)^{1/3}T_6^{-1}
      [(Z_1+Z_2)^{5/3} - Z_1^{5/3} - Z_2^{5/3}]$, since
      $\Gamma_e = 0.2274\,(\rho/\mu_e)^{1/3}/T_6$ and
      $0.9 \times 0.2274 = 0.205$.
\item For an equal-charge pair it gives
      $h/\Gamma_{12} = 0.9(2^{5/3} - 2) = \mathbf{1.057}$, against
      \code{chugunov\_2007}'s own $\gtil \to \infty$ asymptote
      $A_1 + B_1 = \mathbf{1.0346}$ (\S\ref{sec:chugunov}). Two percent apart,
      which is the right amount for an ion-sphere estimate versus a Monte-Carlo
      fit. If you ever see a coefficient here that does \emph{not} land near
      1.05 in these units, it is wrong.
\end{itemize}

--- \textbf{linear in $\Gamma_e$}, versus $\Gamma^{3/2}$ in the weak limit
\eqref{eq:debye}. Two things follow:

\begin{enumerate}
\item The $Z^{5/3}$ scaling is the same one that appears in the NSE Coulomb
      correction (\S\ref{sec:nse-coulomb}). It is not a coincidence: both are the
      ion-sphere self-energy, once entering a rate and once entering a chemical
      potential.
\item \textbf{The weak and strong limits have different powers of $\Gamma$}, so
      no simple formula covers both --- hence a fit anchored at both ends, which
      is exactly what \S\ref{sec:chugunov} shows \code{chugunov\_2007} to be.
\end{enumerate}

The physical picture also changes: weak screening is a \emph{potential shift} at
fixed configuration, strong screening is a \emph{thermodynamic} statement about
rearranging a correlated fluid. At $\Gamma \sim 1$--10, where this box lives,
neither picture is clean --- the reactants are neither free nor caged.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\code{chugunov\_2007}}{chugunov 2007} term by term}
\label{sec:chugunov}

\code{fluxes/screening.py} reimplements pynucastro's scalar jitclass over numpy
state vectors (\S\ref{sec:screening-code}). Reading it as physics rather than
code:

\paragraph{1. The plasma state} (\code{plasma\_arrays}).
%
\begin{equation}
n_e = \frac{\rho\sum_i Z_i Y_i}{m_u},
\qquad
\text{gamma\_e\_fac} = \frac{e^2}{k}\left(\frac{4\pi}{3}\right)^{1/3} n_e^{1/3}
= \frac{e^2}{a_e k}
\label{eq:plasmastate}
\end{equation}
%
Note that $\sum_i Z_i Y_i = \Ye\cdot\sum_i A_i Y_i = \Ye$ for a normalised
composition, so
%
\begin{equation*}
n_e = \frac{\rho\,\Ye}{m_u}
\end{equation*}
%
--- the same combination the weak tables index on (\S\ref{sec:weaktable}). Two
different physics modules, one plasma variable.

\paragraph{2. The ion plasma temperature} (the quantum correction).
%
\begin{equation}
T_p = \frac{\hbar}{k}\,e\sqrt{\frac{4\pi Z_1Z_2\,n_i}{m_i}}
= \frac{\hbar\omega_p}{k},
\qquad
\omega_p^2 = \frac{4\pi (Z_1Z_2) e^2 n_i}{m_i}
\label{eq:plasmatemp}
\end{equation}
%
$\omega_p$ is the plasma frequency of the reduced-mass ion pair --- the scale at
which \textbf{zero-point ion motion} matters. (The single-species $T_p$ is
\citealp{chugunov2007}, eq.~2; the $Z_1Z_2$, $2\mu_{12}$ pair form is
pynucastro's generalization, attributed there to \citealp{yakovlev2006}.)
$T_{\rm norm} = T/T_p$ is the classical-to-quantum ratio; for $T \ll T_p$ the
ions are quantum oscillators, not a classical fluid.
\code{smooth\_clip\_vec(T\_norm, 0.1, 0.2)} floors it with a half-cosine so the fit
never extrapolates into a regime it was not built for. \derivedhere{}
$T_p = 1.23\times10^7$\,K at $\rho = 10^7$ and $1.23\times10^8$\,K at
$\rho = 10^9$ for Si--Si, so $T_{\rm norm}$ ranges ${\sim}13$--640 across the
box: firmly classical, and the clip never engages. Good --- but the clip's
presence is what makes the module safe to call outside the box.

\paragraph{3. The quantum-corrected coupling.}
%
\begin{equation}
\zeta = \left(\frac{4}{3\pi^2 T_{\rm norm}^2}\right)^{1/3},
\qquad
\gtil = \frac{\Gamma}
        {\bigl[\,1 + \zeta\bigl(\alpha_1 + \zeta(\alpha_2 + \alpha_3\zeta)\bigr)\bigr]^{1/3}}
\label{eq:gammatilde}
\end{equation}
%
\begin{equation*}
\alpha_1 = 0.022,\qquad
\alpha_2 = 0.41 - \frac{0.6}{\Gamma},\qquad
\alpha_3 = 0.06 + \frac{2.2}{\Gamma}
\end{equation*}
%
$\zeta \propto T_{\rm norm}^{-2/3}$ is the quantum-diffraction parameter; the
cubic in $\zeta$ is Chugunov's fit to the Monte-Carlo free energy
\citep[eqs.~3, 21]{chugunov2007}, and \gtil{} is the \emph{effective} classical
coupling that reproduces the quantum result.

\paragraph{4. The enhancement} \citep[eq.~19]{chugunov2007}.
%
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
h = \gtil^{3/2}\left(\frac{A_1}{\sqrt{A_2+\gtil}} + \frac{A_3}{1+\gtil}\right)
+ \frac{B_1\gtil^{2}}{B_2+\gtil}
+ \frac{B_3\gtil^{2}}{B_4+\gtil^{2}}
\;}
\label{eq:chugunov}
\end{equation}
%
\begin{gather*}
A_1 = 2.7822,\qquad A_2 = 98.34,\qquad
\boxed{A_3 = \sqrt3 - \frac{A_1}{\sqrt{A_2}}} \\[2pt]
B_1 = -1.7476,\qquad B_2 = 66.07,\qquad B_3 = 1.12,\qquad B_4 = 65
\end{gather*}
%
and the returned value is $\max(h, 0)$.

\textbf{Check the asymptotics --- this is the payoff of \S\ref{sec:debye}.} As
$\gtil \to 0$ the $B$-terms vanish as $\gtil^2$ and the first bracket tends to a
constant, so
%
\begin{equation*}
h \;\longrightarrow\; \gtil^{3/2}\left(\frac{A_1}{\sqrt{A_2}} + A_3\right)
= \sqrt3\,\gtil^{3/2}
\end{equation*}
%
\textbf{because $A_3$ is \emph{defined} as $\sqrt3 - A_1/\sqrt{A_2}$.} That is
the weak-screening (Debye--H\"uckel) limit of the one-component plasma,
recovered exactly \citep[\S IV.C]{chugunov2007}. As $\gtil \to \infty$ the $A_1$
and $B_1$ terms both go linear and partially cancel, leaving
$h \to (A_1+B_1)\gtil + O(\sqrt{\gtil})$ --- the strong-screening\,/\,ion-sphere
limit of \S\ref{sec:strongscreening}. \textbf{One functional form, both
asymptotics, fitted in between.} The $A_3$ line in the code is not a magic
constant; it is a boundary condition, and you can read it as such.

The final $\max(h,0)$ enforces that screening never \emph{suppresses} a rate.

\subsection{Per-reaction pair construction --- and the MESA difference}
\label{sec:screening-pairs}

A reaction with three reactants needs more than one pair. pynucastro walks the
reactants building \code{screening\_pairs}, including \textbf{composite
intermediates} (screen A against B, then the composite A$+$B against C). The
compiled evaluator just records the multiplicities and sums $h$ over a
reaction's pairs --- i.e.\ takes the \textbf{product} of factors, since $h$ is a
log (\S\ref{sec:screening-code}).

MESA's \code{net\_screen} uses a slightly different rule for $\ge3$ reactants (a
neutron-swap two-stage construction, mirrored explicitly in
\code{rates\_compare.compare\_screening} so the comparison is apples-to-apples).
The Step-4 measurement bounded the per-factor difference at
\textbf{$\le 0.0021$\,dex} \resultsref{2026-07-10}, and multi-body channels are
pp-chain territory --- negligible in this box. The module docstring says exactly
this, and it is a good example of a known, bounded, documented discrepancy that
is \emph{not} a defect.

The same measurement is what \textbf{determined} the label configuration: MESA
\code{screening\_mode='chugunov'} $\equiv$ pynucastro \code{chugunov\_2007} to
median ratio 0.99999, max 0.0021\,dex; \code{chugunov\_2009} does \textbf{not}
match (max 0.52\,dex) \resultsref{2026-07-10}. That determination is pinned as
executable policy in \code{SCREENING\_ALLOWED} (\S\ref{sec:guard-screening}) ---
a pin derived from a measurement, not a preference.

\subsection{The screened-\texorpdfstring{$\kappa$}{kappa} offset, derived}
\label{sec:screened-kappa}

Here is the derivation \S\ref{sec:kappa-db} promised, and it is short enough to
do in your head once you see it.

Screening multiplies each \emph{reaction} by $\exp(h)$ built from \textbf{that
reaction's own reactant pairs}. Consider a
capture\,/\,photodisintegration pair at true equilibrium:

\begin{itemize}
\item forward $A + a \to B$: reactants are two charged nuclei, so $h^+ > 0$;
\item reverse $B \to A + a$: a single reactant, \textbf{no pair at all}, so
      $h^- = 0$.
\end{itemize}

Let $f^+_0 = f^-_0 = f_0$ be the (equal) unscreened equilibrium fluxes. Then
%
\begin{equation}
\kappa
= \frac{\bigl|f_0e^{h^+} - f_0e^{h^-}\bigr|}{f_0e^{h^+} + f_0e^{h^-}}
= \left|\tanh\!\left(\frac{h^+-h^-}{2}\right)\right|
\;\approx\; \frac{|\Delta h|}{2}\ \ \text{for } |\Delta h|\ll 1
\label{eq:screenedkappa}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Verified numerically} on \msn{80} at $\Tn = 6.3$, $\rho = 10^9$, NSE
composition: $\kappa_{\rm screened} = |\tanh(\Delta h/2)|$ holds to
$1.4\times10^{-11}$ --- i.e.\ exactly, down to the unscreened $\kappa$ floor.
Median $\kappa = \mathbf{7.361\times10^{-2}}$, reproducing the \code{RESULTS.md}
$7.4\times10^{-2}$ row; median $|\Delta h| = 0.1475$. \derivedhere

Three consequences:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{This is real, not a bug.} Both pynucastro and MESA
      \code{net\_screen} screen per-reaction; a screened capture genuinely does
      pair with an unscreened photodissociation in any code that does this. It
      is a property of the \emph{rate configuration}, and the labels carry it.
\item \textbf{It is bounded by the screening factor itself.}
      $\kappa_{\rm screened} \le \tanh(h_{\max}/2)$, and with
      $h \le \ln(3.3) \approx 1.2$ on the heaviest pairs,
      $\kappa \lesssim 0.54$ in the worst case --- comparable to the v-flag
      floor it replaced. Hence the two-$\kappa$ rule below.
\item \textbf{Neutron pairs are exempt.} $h = 0$ on both sides for
      $(n,\gamma)/(\gamma,n)$, so their screened $\kappa$ is clean. Since those
      are the fastest-equilibrating pairs (\S\ref{sec:no-gamow}), the
      \emph{most} equilibrated pairs are also the ones least affected --- which
      softens the practical impact but does not remove the need for the rule.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{warn}
\warnglyph{}~\textbf{A discrepancy to raise, not to paper over.}
\code{docs/rate-crosscheck.md} and root \code{CLAUDE.md} characterise this as
``$\kappa \approx |\Delta \ln \mathrm{scor}|$''. The exact relation
\eqref{eq:screenedkappa} is $|\tanh(\Delta h/2)|$, i.e.\ \textbf{$|\Delta h|/2$}
in the small-offset limit --- a factor of two. The measured numbers confirm the
halved form: median $|\Delta h| = 0.1475$ gives $\kappa = 0.0736$, and 0.0736 is
the number in \code{RESULTS.md}, not 0.1475. The \emph{measured} row is right;
the \emph{characterisation} next to it is loose by a factor of two. Per the docs
contract this is a docs question, not a code question --- flag it, do not
silently edit.
\end{warn}

\subsection{The two \texorpdfstring{$\kappa$}{kappa} conventions}
\label{sec:two-kappa}

\begin{invariant}
\textbf{Equilibrium detection uses UNSCREENED $\kappa$. Screened $\kappa$ is for
screening-offset diagnostics only. Never mix them in one analysis.}
\end{invariant}

The source document states this rule beside the pf gate, because that is where
it is \emph{used}; it belongs here, immediately after \eqref{eq:screenedkappa},
because that is where it is \emph{derived}.

With \code{chugunov\_2007} on --- the label configuration --- the measured median
$\kappa$ at NSE is \textbf{$7.4\times10^{-2}$} \resultsref{2026-07-10}, within a
factor of a few of the spurious v-flag floor it replaced
(\S\ref{sec:vflag}). Two effects of similar size and completely different
natures; conflating them is exactly the failure the convention prevents. Every
$\kappa$ threshold in the project (the 0.1 active-set gate above all) is
evaluated on unscreened runs.

\subsection{The other equilibrium correction: Coulomb terms in NSE}
\label{sec:nse-coulomb}

There is a second place plasma physics enters --- not the rate side but the
\emph{equilibrium} side --- and the $\kappa$ screen turns it \textbf{off}. Worth
understanding, because ``off'' is a deliberate choice with a stated reason.

The Saha relation \eqref{eq:saha} assumed an ideal gas of nuclei. In a
strongly-coupled plasma each nucleus also carries a \textbf{Coulomb free
energy} --- the interaction energy with its screening cloud --- which shifts its
chemical potential by
%
\begin{equation*}
\mu_i \to \mu_i + \mu^c_i(Z_i, \Gamma_e)
\end{equation*}
%
pynucastro implements the \citet[eq.~28]{chabrier1998} fit:
%
\begin{equation*}
\frac{\mu^c}{kT} =
A_1\Bigl[\sqrt{\Gamma(A_2+\Gamma)}
- A_2\ln\bigl(\sqrt{\Gamma/A_2}+\sqrt{1+\Gamma/A_2}\bigr)\Bigr]
+ 2A_3\bigl[\sqrt\Gamma - \arctan\sqrt\Gamma\,\bigr]
\end{equation*}
%
with $A_1 = -0.9052$, $A_2 = 0.6322$,
\textbf{$A_3 = -\sqrt3/2 - A_1/\sqrt{A_2}$}, and
$\Gamma = \Gamma_e Z^{5/3}$.

\begin{aside}
\textbf{Notice the structure.} That $A_3$ definition is the \emph{same kind of
object} as \code{chugunov\_2007}'s $A_3 = \sqrt3 - A_1/\sqrt{A_2}$
(\S\ref{sec:chugunov}): a coefficient defined so the fit reproduces the
Debye--H\"uckel $\sqrt{\Gamma^3}$ limit at weak coupling. Two different fits, two
different sign conventions, one shared boundary condition. Once you have seen it
in one, you can read it in the other --- and you know immediately that ``clean
up $A_3$ to a literal'' is a bug in both.
\end{aside}

\textbf{The $Z^{5/3}$ scaling is the important physics.} $\mu^c$ grows much
faster than linearly with charge, so the correction \emph{differentially}
favours heavy nuclei in NSE --- it shifts the NSE composition toward the Fe peak
at fixed $T$, $\rho$, \Ye. At the box's cold, dense corner ($\Gamma_e$ large)
this is not a small effect \citep[screening factors in the equilibrium reach
${\sim}10^6$ at $\Tn = 3.5$, $\rho = 10^9$\,\gcc]{hix1996}.

\textbf{Why the $\kappa$ screen sets \code{use\_coulomb\_corr=False}.} The screen
compares \emph{bare} (unscreened) rates on both sides
(\S\ref{sec:kappa-db}). Detailed balance for bare rates balances at the
\emph{ideal-gas} NSE composition; balancing them at a Coulomb-corrected
composition would introduce a mismatch that looks exactly like a $\kappa$ floor.
The choice is not ``Coulomb corrections are negligible'' --- it is
\textbf{``the equilibrium condition must be the one the rates actually
satisfy.''} Screening on the rate side and Coulomb corrections on the
equilibrium side are the same physics entering two ways, and they must be
switched on and off together.

That is the same principle as the two-$\kappa$ conventions rule
(\S\ref{sec:two-kappa}), stated for the equilibrium side instead of the rate
side. It is worth extracting as a general rule, because it will recur in S9:

\begin{invariant}
\textbf{Rate-side and equilibrium-side plasma corrections are a matched pair.
Any consistency test must use both or neither.}
\end{invariant}
