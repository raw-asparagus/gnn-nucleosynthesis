\section{The guards: Tier 1 as executable policy}
\label{part:guards}

\code{fluxes/guards.py} is the tier's conclusions compiled into code that
refuses to run. Four gates, each traceable to a measurement in
\S\ref{part:db}--\S\ref{part:verification}:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{4.5cm} Y L{4.6cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Guard} & \textbf{Encodes} & \textbf{From} \\
\midrule
\code{assert\_pf\_gate} & no raw v-flag reverses, ever &
  \S\ref{sec:vflag} --- spurious $\kappa$ floor to 0.8 \\
\code{assert\_appendixb\_routing} & gh-575 channels never take stock r23.05.1
  values & \S\ref{sec:gh575} --- 10--23 dex \\
\code{assert\_screening\_allowed} & \code{chugunov\_2007} or \code{None}, nothing
  else & \S\ref{sec:screening-pairs}\,/\,\S\ref{sec:screening-determination} ---
  0.0021 dex determination \\
\code{assert\_reconciled\_build} & pinned tabular ordering $+$
  \code{provisional\_reaction\_set == False} &
  \S\ref{sec:tablefamilies}\,/\,\S\ref{sec:disposition} --- ADR 0003 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{The four gates, as code}
\label{sec:guard-code}

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\code{assert\_pf\_gate}}{assert pf gate}}
\label{sec:guard-pf}

Fed the \code{vflag\_sets} boolean of \S\ref{sec:vflag-extraction} (or
\code{derived\_from\_inverse} read straight from an npz), it raises rather than
warns:

\begin{srclisting}
class PfGateError(RuntimeError):
    """Raised when a flux/kappa computation would use raw v-flag reverse rates."""
\end{srclisting}

and compilation \emph{refuses}. The physics it protects is
\S\ref{sec:vflag}: the partition-function ratio is the one factor of
\eqref{eq:dbratio} with no common-mode partner, so an error in it survives into
$\kappa$ at full strength.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\code{assert\_appendixb\_routing}}{assert appendixb routing}}
\label{sec:guard-appendixb}

Refuses stock-r23.05.1 MESA values on the 9\,/\,11 excluded channels of
\code{configs/appendixb\_excluded\_channels.yaml} --- the gh-575 class derived in
\S\ref{sec:gh575} and measured in \S\ref{sec:appendixb}. The Zenodo labels were
generated with the authors' locally patched r23.05.1, so on those channels the
labels are clean and \emph{our} stock MESA is the outlier.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\code{assert\_screening\_allowed}}{assert screening allowed}}
\label{sec:guard-screening}

\begin{srclisting}
SCREENING_ALLOWED = frozenset({"chugunov_2007", None})
\end{srclisting}

--- a pin derived from a measurement, not a preference
(\S\ref{sec:screening-determination}: median ratio 0.99999, max 0.0021\,dex
against \code{chugunov\_2007}; 0.52\,dex against \code{chugunov\_2009}).

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\code{assert\_reconciled\_build}}{assert reconciled build}}
\label{sec:guard-reconciled}

This one encodes two conclusions. First the tabular precedence of
\S\ref{sec:tablefamilies}, pinned as a constant:

\begin{srclisting}
DEFAULT_TABULAR_ORDERING = ("suzuki", "pruet_fuller", "ffn", "oda", "langanke")
\end{srclisting}

and checked on every build, because a build with the wrong ordering would be
subtly wrong in a way no conservation test can catch:

\begin{srclisting}
if tuple(info.tabular_ordering) != DEFAULT_TABULAR_ORDERING:
    raise ValueError("... != pinned ADR-0003 ordering ...")
\end{srclisting}

Second, the reconciled-membership flag of \S\ref{sec:licenses}:
\code{provisional\_reaction\_set == False} plus \code{disposition\_sha256} in every
npz, which is the machine-readable form of ``this build is the reconciled one''.

\subsection{Four properties worth studying as design}
\label{sec:guard-design}

Independently of the physics:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{They raise, they do not warn.} A warning in a batch job is a log
      line nobody reads. \code{PfGateError} stops compilation.
\item \textbf{They are import-light on purpose.} ``No pynucastro import at
      module scope so the guards can gate cheap code paths (npz-only checks)
      without the heavy import.'' A guard that is expensive to call gets called
      less.
\item \textbf{They are placed at the chokepoint.} \emph{Every} flux\,/\,$\kappa$
      entry point goes through \code{compile\_network}, which calls three of the
      four (\code{assert\_screening\_allowed}, \code{assert\_reconciled\_build},
      \code{assert\_pf\_gate}); the fourth, \code{assert\_appendixb\_routing},
      guards a different chokepoint --- any request for MESA-side rate
      \emph{values} (the spot-check harness) --- since compilation never
      consumes stock-MESA values. No path into the flux engine bypasses the
      three compilation gates --- which is a stronger property than ``all
      current callers check''.
\item \textbf{They are tested for their failure behaviour}, not their success
      behaviour: \code{test\_flux\_guards.py} is mostly \code{pytest.raises}.
      Including \code{test\_exported\_npz\_trips\_the\_gate}
      (\S\ref{sec:oracles-db}), which asserts that a real artifact in the repo
      still fails the gate.
\end{enumerate}

This is the pattern Tier~0 \S VII.4 named (``invariants live in code that
refuses, not in comments''). Tier~1 is where you see it applied to five separate
physics conclusions in one file (the fourth guard encodes two: the ordering pin
and the reconciled-membership flag).
