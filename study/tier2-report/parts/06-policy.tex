\begin{invariant}
\textbf{The three worth internalising as \emph{habits}, not facts:}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Make it unrepresentable before you make it detectable.}
  \code{eligible\_mask} could have been a function that checks, after the fact,
  whether a mask contains weak columns. Instead it is the only way to build a
  mask, and it takes \code{weak\_mask} as a required argument. There is no code
  path that produces an invalid mask, so there is no code path that needs
  checking.
\item \textbf{A gate that can skip is not a gate.} \code{conftest.py}'s
  docstring says it outright. Regenerating a 7-second artifact is cheaper than
  the class of bug where the blocking test silently no-ops in CI for a month.
\item \textbf{Refuse ambiguous provenance at the point of display.} The notebook
  helper that will not plot a \kap{} histogram without a declared convention is
  doing more work than a comment ever would --- because the failure mode it
  prevents (comparing screened \kap{} against an unscreened threshold) produces
  a plot that looks entirely reasonable.
\end{enumerate}
\end{invariant}

\subsection{\ldots and the three pieces of discipline it does not encode}
\label{sec:discipline-intro}

The rest of this section is the mirror image: \textbf{three pieces of
measurement discipline the repo does \emph{not} encode}, and in two cases has
never applied. They sit here rather than in the register because each is a
substantive statement about what the tier's own numbers can support ---

\subsection{The sampling measure on the regime box}
\label{sec:samplingmeasure}

Tier~0 \S III.2 derives the regime box edge by edge and every edge is
defensible. Tier~0 \S III.4 covers Sobol sampling theory, and \S III.9 covers
stratification. \textbf{What no node asks is whether the uniform measure on that
box is the right measure} --- i.e. whether the density of training states
matches the density of states a star actually occupies.

\S\ref{sec:vacuous} already established a \emph{compositional} mismatch (random
Sobol compositions are nucleon-loaded and nowhere near equilibrium; the
cancellation structure lives only on relaxed states). This is the
\emph{thermodynamic} analogue, on the $(T, \rho)$ axes, and it is a different
measurement.

\subsubsection{Reading it honestly}
\label{sec:samplingmeasure-reading}

Two statements, and only the first is strong:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The occupation is locally one-dimensional.} At any instant, and
  along the central track through time, the star lies within
  ${\sim}0.12$--0.18\,dex of a \emph{line} in $(\log T, \log\rho)$. It is a curve
  with thickness, not an area. The Sobol grid samples the area uniformly.
\item \textbf{The box is not mostly empty, but it is far from uniformly
  occupied.} About two-thirds of the box lies more than 0.1\,dex from anything
  this progenitor visits; at 0.3\,dex tolerance, coverage is 82\%. So ``most of
  the box is unphysical'' would be an overstatement --- but ``the training
  density is uniform where the physical density is concentrated on a band'' is
  measured and true.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{warn}
\textbf{And the correct caveat, which changes the recommendation.} This is
\emph{one} progenitor (20\,\Msun), one final profile, one central history.
Different progenitor masses sweep different loci, and the box is presumably
sized as a \emph{union} over progenitors --- which is a legitimate design choice
for a general-purpose emulator. So the finding is \textbf{not} ``the box is too
big''. It is:

\textbf{Nobody has measured the induced density.} The box's \emph{support} is
defensible; its \emph{measure} has never been compared with the measure the
deployment distribution actually carries, and the two are measurably different
by at least a factor of a few in the region that matters.
\end{warn}

That matters for three concrete things, none of which is currently instrumented:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Where accuracy is bought.} Uniform sampling spends the same
  modelling capacity on a corner of the box the star crosses briefly as on the
  band where it spends its life. A tighter emulator on the band would be worth
  more than a uniformly tighter one.
\item \textbf{What the reported error statistics mean.} A p99 error over the
  uniform box is not the p99 error a star would experience. This is the same
  category of error as \S\ref{sec:clusterrobust} --- the \emph{reference
  measure} of a summary statistic is unstated.
\item \textbf{It sharpens the Sobol$\to$real-MESA gate.} \code{CLAUDE.md}
  already has the right instrument (``retrain with trajectory-aware sampling if
  real-track 99th-pct \Ye{} error $> 3\times$ the Sobol-measured value''). This
  measurement says what the gate is \emph{for}, and predicts it will fire: the
  two measures differ, so the two error statistics should too.
\end{itemize}

Open (register: \reg{R-07}) --- a density comparison on files already in
\code{data/zenodo/}, to be run across the progenitor set rather than one model
before any conclusion is drawn.

\subsection{Cluster-robust inference --- the effective sample size is
            \texorpdfstring{${\sim}300$}{~300}, not 657,000}
\label{sec:clusterrobust}

\subsubsection{The problem}
\label{sec:clusterrobust-problem}

\begin{warn}
\warnglyph{}~\code{RESULTS.md} reports statistics with impressive-looking sample
sizes:

Spearman$(\log\kap$ unscr, $\log\delta_r) = \mathbf{+0.491}$\,/\,$\mathbf{+0.498}$
over \textbf{657k\,/\,1.59M} carrying-pair samples \ldots
\resultsref{2026-07-12}

and, in the same block, the provenance of those samples:

pre-terminal rows of 20 shipped $+$ 209 rerun trajectories per net
(\textbf{4,693 rows/net} sampled $\le 24$/trajectory)
\end{warn}

Put those together. The 657,000 ``samples'' are (row $\times$ reaction-pair)
cells drawn from \textbf{4,693 rows}, which are themselves drawn from
\textbf{229 trajectories}. Both multiplications are \emph{within-cluster}:
consecutive rows of one trajectory are nearly the same physical state, and
reaction pairs within one row share that state entirely. \textbf{Neither
multiplication adds independent information.}

This is the classic clustered-sampling error, and it is not a pedantic one ---
it inflates apparent precision by the \emph{design effect} \citep{kish1965}
%
\begin{equation}
\text{deff} \;=\; 1 + (\bar m - 1)\,\rho_{\mathrm{ICC}}, \qquad
n_{\mathrm{eff}} \;=\; \frac{n}{\text{deff}},
\label{eq:deff}
\end{equation}
%
with $\rho_{\rm ICC}$ the intraclass correlation and $\bar m$ the cluster size
(strictly a weighted mean when clusters are unbalanced; the shipped-trajectory
measurement below is balanced at 26 rows each).

\subsubsection{The measurement: intraclass correlation and the design effect}
\label{sec:clusterrobust-measurement}

ICC computed by one-way random effects (the ICC(1,1) estimator of
\citealp{shrout1979}; review in \citealp{donner1986}) over 80 shipped \msn{80}
trajectories, 26 pre-stall rows each, for four per-row scalars of the kind
\code{RESULTS.md} aggregates. \derivedhere:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}Y L{1.3cm} L{1.1cm} L{1.7cm} L{2.0cm} L{1.5cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{per-row quantity} & \textbf{ICC} & \textbf{$\bar m$} &
\textbf{nominal $n$} & \textbf{design effect} & \textbf{$n_{\rm eff}$} \\
\midrule
\Ye{} & 0.630 & 26.0 & 2079 & 16.7 & \textbf{124} \\
Si-group mass fraction & 0.417 & 26.0 & 2079 & 11.4 & \textbf{182} \\
\# species above $10^{-3}$ & 0.219 & 26.0 & 2079 & 6.5 & \textbf{322} \\
$\log_{10} X(\nuc{Si}{28})$ & 0.704 & 26.0 & 2079 & 18.6 & \textbf{112} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\begin{srclisting}
def icc(vals):                      # vals: one 1-D array per trajectory
    k = len(vals); ns = np.array([len(v) for v in vals]); m = ns.mean()
    g = np.concatenate(vals).mean()
    msb = sum(len(v) * (v.mean() - g)**2 for v in vals) / (k - 1)
    msw = sum(((v - v.mean())**2).sum() for v in vals) / (ns.sum() - k)
    return (msb - msw) / (msb + (m - 1) * msw)
\end{srclisting}

\textbf{ICC of 0.22--0.70 at $\bar m = 26$ gives design effects of 6.5--18.6.}
Applied to the kill-test manifold (4,693 rows over 229 trajectories, so
$\bar m \approx 20.5$ and $\text{deff} = 1 + 19.5\rho$ ranges 5.3--14.7), the
row-level effective sample size is \textbf{$\approx 320$--890, and
$\approx 320$ for the \Ye-like quantities that matter here} --- and the reaction
axis, which takes 4,693 rows to 657,000 cells, adds no independent physical
systems at all.

\begin{result}
\textbf{So the honest denominator for the \kap$\leftrightarrow\delta$
correlation is a few hundred, not 657,000 --- an overstatement of roughly
$10^{3}$ in nominal $n$, i.e. ${\sim}30\times$ in standard error.}
\end{result}

\subsubsection{What this does and does not invalidate}
\label{sec:clusterrobust-scope}

Being precise matters here, because the temptation is to over-correct.

\textbf{Unaffected:}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Point estimates.} Clustering biases \emph{precision}, not (much)
  the estimate \citep{cameron2015}. Spearman $\approx +0.49$ is probably about
  right.
\item \textbf{Degenerate results.} ``Maskable columns: median 0 at every
  $\varepsilon$, p90 $= 0$'' \resultsref{2026-07-12} is a statement about a
  distribution that is identically zero. No sample size argument touches it.
\item \textbf{Structural results.} $\kap = 1.0$ exactly on all weak columns is
  an identity (\S\ref{sec:weakkappa}), not a statistic.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Affected:}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Every p-value and confidence interval.} ``$p \approx 0$'' on 657k
  clustered samples is not evidence of anything. The correct statement needs a
  trajectory-level bootstrap.
\item \textbf{Concentration statistics with no reported spread.} ``top-20 carry
  0.88'', ``top-5 $= 0.96$\,/\,0.75'', the bridge shares (0.20, 0.13,
  0.11\,\ldots). These are \emph{rankings and shares} whose between-trajectory
  variability is never reported. With $n_{\rm eff} \approx 300$ the ranking of
  \#4 vs \#7 in a 251-item list is likely not resolvable, and the current output
  gives no way to tell.
\item \textbf{The split verdict.} The 95\% coverage gate's most consequential
  row --- worst-dominant-isotope coverage falling to 0.0022--0.166 across
  $[4.0, 6.3)$ strata --- is a \emph{tail} statistic (a per-row minimum)
  computed within strata, where the effective $n$ is smaller still. A tail
  statistic at $n_{\rm eff} \sim$ tens is a different object from one at
  $n \sim 10^{5}$.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{The fix, which is cheap}
\label{sec:clusterrobust-fix}

\textbf{Bootstrap over trajectories, not rows} (the cluster bootstrap:
\citealp[\S3.8]{davison1997}; \citealp{field2007}). Resample the 229
trajectories with replacement, recompute the statistic on each resample, and
report the percentile interval. That is the correct unit of independence --- one
trajectory is one physical system integrated from one initial condition --- and
it automatically handles both levels of clustering without needing ICC estimates
at all. It is perhaps thirty lines in \code{killtest/}, and it converts every
headline number from a point estimate into a point estimate with an honest
interval.

Related and equally cheap: report \textbf{$\bar m$ and the number of clusters}
alongside every $n$. \code{RESULTS.md} already records both, in different rows
of the same block; joining them at the point of reporting would have made this
visible.

Open (register: \reg{R-08}). It does not overturn any verdict I can see, but it
means several published numbers carry no uncertainty statement at all, and one
of them (the split verdict's tail row) is the basis of a design decision.

\subsection{Verification practice --- three standard steps that are absent}
\label{sec:vandv}

The repo's testing culture is unusually good (layered oracles, external
cross-checks, executable policy). Three standard computational-science
verification steps are nonetheless missing, and they are missing in a way that
is characteristic: the tests verify \emph{agreement}, rarely \emph{order}.

\paragraph{(a) Convergence-order verification of the integrator.}
\code{test\_integrate.py} checks BDF $\equiv$ Radau $\equiv$ BDF(rtol
$10^{-10}$) to $5\times10^{-8}$ on the stiffest test state
\resultsref{2026-07-12} --- an agreement test. It never verifies that the error
decreases at the \emph{expected rate} as rtol tightens. Two methods agreeing is
strong evidence neither is grossly wrong; a measured convergence order is
evidence the error estimator is trustworthy, which is a different claim and the
one that licenses running at rtol $= 10^{-8}$ in production (code-to-code
agreement is explicitly \emph{not} verification in the V\&V canon:
\citealp{roache1998}; \citealp{oberkampf2010}). Cost: one loop over rtol on one
state.

\paragraph{(b) A manufactured solution.} The method of manufactured solutions
\citep{roache2002, salari2000} --- construct a source term making a chosen
analytic function an exact solution, then verify the code reproduces it to the
expected order --- is the standard way to verify a solver against \emph{itself}
with no reference implementation. Here it would need a toy $\nu$ (one already
exists: \code{test\_conservation.py}'s Layer-1 network, \S\ref{sec:oracles7})
and a synthetic rate law. This is the one verification technique that does not
require a second code, and the project's ground truth (MESA\,/\,bbq) is known to
carry a bug, so a reference-free check has real value.

\paragraph{(c) Trivial baselines.} Tier~0 \S III.2's caveat already observes
that ``at these densities NSE is effectively established from $\approx 5$\,GK
upward, so the top ${\sim}3$\,GK of the box is \emph{already} algebraic and the
emulator is being trained on states it does not strictly need to learn.'' The
obvious experiment that follows is not listed anywhere: \textbf{evaluate
NSE-as-a-predictor as a baseline} across the box. \code{qse/solver.py} already
computes it, and it costs milliseconds. If NSE matches the labels at
$\Tn \ge 5$ to within the \Ye{} gate, then the honest framing of the emulator's
job is ``$\Tn < 5$'', and the strata where it is hardest to train
(\S\ref{sec:costwall}: $\Tn \ge 5$ is unmeasurable \emph{and}
label-pathological) are also the strata where it is least needed. Other
near-free baselines in the same spirit: persistence ($\Delta Y = 0$), and
nearest-neighbour in the training set.

\begin{invariant}
A baseline you can beat trivially is not interesting; a baseline you
\emph{cannot} beat is the most valuable measurement in the project. Neither is
currently known.
\end{invariant}

free and may substantially redefine the target domain.
