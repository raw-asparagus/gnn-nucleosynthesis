\section{Concordance with the source document}
\label{app:concordance}

The re-architecture in this report is a change of \emph{order}, not of content:
the source's node-by-node parts were re-sequenced into a physics arc
(\hyperref[sec:status]{Status of this document}), which dissolves the S7--S10 labels and moves equilibrium
in front of the flux algebra. That makes a concordance necessary rather than
decorative: a source part's argument can now land in two places, and this
appendix is where you find them.

Everything below is mechanically checked by
\code{study/tier2-report/check\_coverage.py}.

\begin{aside}
Fifty-six source headings are unnumbered in the original (the part titles, the
front-matter blocks, the handoff blocks, and the un-numbered subsections such as
``The measurement'' and ``The geometry''); they carry ``---'' in the first
column. The source's
irregular identifiers --- \S0.5b, \S I.2b, \S I.7b, \S I.10b, \S II.4b,
\S II.7b, \S IV.4b and \S IV.8b --- are reproduced as they stand. Four source
headings carry the \emph{same} text (``The measurement'', at lines 1870, 4094,
4382 and 4487); each is disambiguated here by a distinct title and label.
\end{aside}

\begin{aside}
\textbf{Two places where the source contradicts itself, and how this report
resolves them.} Both resolutions follow the source's own majority reading; they
are recorded here because they are the only wording changes in the
transcription.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{``\kap, three ways'' vs ``four ways''.} The source's heading at
  line 2338 says \emph{three} and its lead-in at line 2340 says ``Three
  readings''; its Contents row (line 119) and its own full section list (line
  156) both say \emph{four}, and the section carries four lettered subsections,
  (a)--(d). This report reads \emph{four} (\S\ref{sec:kappa}), and the same
  correction is applied to the part's opening sentence, which in the source
  (line 2191) names three roles and omits entropy production. The B.1 row for
  line 2338 records the source heading as it stands.
\item \textbf{A bare \S IV.7 that means Tier~1's.} At line 2360 the source
  attributes the screened-\kap{} offset to ``\S IV.7''; by its own convention
  (line 70) a bare \S IV.x is \emph{this} file's, which would be ``Error control
  for \Phifl''. The intended target is Tier~1 \S IV.7, as the source itself
  writes at line 2433. \S\ref{sec:kappa-db} carries the disambiguated form.
\end{enumerate}
\end{aside}

\subsection{Register anchors}
\label{app:registeranchors}

The source's register table addresses each item's home section by a GitHub slug
anchor, which does not survive a re-ordering. Every one becomes a live
\code{\textbackslash ref} in \S\ref{sec:register}; this table is the map, and it
is what \code{check\_coverage.py} asserts against.

Three items are anchored weakly or not at all in the source body, and all three
now carry the anchor in the body as well, so the link survives in both
directions:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{R-21} is never cited at its own home section (\S0.3.4); its only
  body occurrence is inside \emph{another} item's write-up (\S VI.2's R-24,
  source line 4689, carried here at \S\ref{sec:r24}).
\item \textbf{R-22} appears nowhere in the source body at all.
\item \textbf{R-23} likewise appears nowhere in the source body: the sole body
  occurrence of the string ``R-23'' (source line 3747) is \emph{Tier~1's} R-23,
  rendered here as \foreignreg{1}{R-23} at \S\ref{sec:bdf}. Tier~2's R-23 gains
  its first body anchor at \S\ref{sec:energyidentity}.
\end{itemize}
