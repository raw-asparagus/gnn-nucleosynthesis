\section{The dataset as a physical object}
\label{part:dataset}

The dataset is a \emph{discretization of an operator}, and every design choice
in it encodes a physical assumption. Study it as physics.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{What one record is}
\label{sec:onerecord}

\begin{result}
\textbf{One record $=$ one (state, $\dd t$) pair.}
\end{result}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Inputs}: ($\log T$, $\log\rho$, $\mathbf{X}$, $\dd t$)
\item \textbf{Labels}: ($\mathbf{X}'$, $e_{\text{nuc}}$, $\varepsilon_\nu$)
\end{itemize}

Each underlying state is stepped at \textbf{nine} timesteps, giving nine records
per state. So the dataset does not sample a single map --- it samples the
operator \emph{family} $\{\Phi_{\Delta t}\}$ at nine $\Delta t$ values over the
same initial conditions. What is being taught is the flow map \emph{as a
function of $\Delta t$}, which is a strictly harder object than a fixed-step map
and a strictly more useful one: a host code chooses its own timestep.

\textbf{A consequence nobody exploits yet.} For an autonomous flow,
$\Phi_{s+t} = \Phi_s \circ \Phi_t$. Having nine $\Delta t$ values on the
\emph{same} states makes this a free, label-independent consistency test of any
learned $\hat\Phi$ --- arguably the cheapest available diagnostic of rollout
health (\S\ref{sec:rollouterror}).

\begin{repopointer}
\textbf{In code:} \code{data/schema.py::StepInputs} and \code{StepLabels}.
\code{StepInputs.\_\_post\_init\_\_} validates \code{dt\_seconds} against the
\textbf{measured} grid to \code{rel\_tol=1e-9} --- passing a nominal $10^k$
value is rejected (\S\ref{sec:dtgrid}).
\end{repopointer}

\subsection{The regime box, derived rather than assumed}
\label{sec:regimebox}

\begin{align*}
T &= 1.6\text{--}7.9\ \mathrm{GK} \qquad (10^{9.2}\text{--}10^{9.9}\ \mathrm{K})\\
\rho &= 10^{7}\text{--}10^{9}\ \gcc\\
0.45 &< Y_e < 0.5
\end{align*}

Each bound is physical, and now quantitative:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}l l Y@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Edge} & \textbf{Value} & \textbf{Derivation} \\
\midrule
$T$ lower & 1.6~GK &
  Below this, Si photodisintegration is negligible --- you are in oxygen
  burning, a different network \\
$T$ upper & 7.9~GK &
  Above this, matter is unambiguously in full NSE: composition is
  \emph{algebraic} in $(T,\rho,\Ye)$, so no ODE --- and no emulator --- is
  needed. \textbf{The emulator's domain ends where equilibrium begins}; see the
  caveat below on where it actually begins \\
$\rho$ lower & $10^{7}$ &
  Just below the free-proton EC threshold $\rho \approx 2.4\times10^{7}$
  (\S\ref{sec:ecthreshold}) --- the box brackets the turn-on of the
  \Ye{}-controlling process \\
$\rho$ upper & $10^{9}$ &
  Strongly degenerate ($E_F/kT \approx 10$); above this you are into collapse \\
\Ye{} upper & 0.5 & Oxygen-burning ash is near-symmetric \\
\Ye{} lower & 0.45 &
  By here the core is collapsing --- the box covers the \emph{entire} physical
  excursion \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

Also note what the box implies about screening: $\Gamma$ ranges
$\approx 0.4$--25 across it (varying with $\rho^{1/3}/T$, so the temperature
edges dominate), the intermediate-coupling regime where neither weak- nor
strong-screening asymptotics apply (\S\ref{sec:gamma}). That is the derivation
behind pinning \code{screening\_mode = 'chugunov'}.

\begin{warn}
\textbf{Do not read the $T$-upper row backwards.} ``Above 7.9~GK it is NSE'' is
true; ``below 7.9~GK it is not NSE'' is false. At these densities NSE is
effectively established from $\approx5$~GK upward (the NSE relaxation time
$\tau \approx \exp(196.02/\Tn - 41.645)$~s falls from ${\sim}10^{3}$~s at 4~GK
to ${\sim}0.1$~s at 5~GK --- \citealt{calder2007}), so the top ${\sim}3$~GK of
the box is \emph{already} algebraic and the emulator is being trained on states
it does not strictly need to learn. 7.9~GK is therefore a \textbf{conservative}
edge --- chosen so the box certainly contains the whole non-equilibrium region
--- not the location of the NSE transition. The honest statement of the
$T$-upper rationale is ``safely past the transition'', and
\S\ref{sec:selfcheck-dataset} item~5 is exactly the exercise of noticing this.
\end{warn}

\textbf{QSE onset $\approx 3$--3.3~GK}, kill-test priority window
\textbf{3.3--5~GK} --- where clustering has appeared but full NSE has not taken
over. Physically richest and hardest.

\begin{repopointer}
\textbf{In code:} \code{crosscheck/grids.py::state\_grid} is the single source;
\code{data/subsample.py} lifts the same edges into strata.
\end{repopointer}

\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{$\dd t$}{dt} grid --- and why nominal values are a
  lie}
\label{sec:dtgrid}

Nine nominal steps: $10^{-6} \ldots 10^{2}$~s, one per decade
(\code{DT\_GRID\_SECONDS}, \code{DT\_LABELS}).

\textbf{Why this range?} It covers \emph{most} of the timesteps a stellar
evolution code takes during silicon burning --- burning-limited steps once
$T \gtrsim 3$~GK, up to ${\sim}10^{2}$~s early on when other physics limits ---
which is exactly the claim the source paper makes (\citealt{grichener2025}:
``encompassing most timesteps taken by the MESA simulations''). It is not the
whole envelope: fully-coupled MESA steps near collapse drop below
$10^{-10}$~s \citep{jermyn2023}. Nine decades is the operator's main usage
envelope.

\textbf{The trap.} Nominal values are \textbf{labels only}. Real steps deviate
by up to ${\sim}5\%$ and \textbf{differ between networks} --- the
\code{"1e2"} file is 105.08~s for \msn{80} but 102.93~s for \msn{151}. Using
100~s where the data means 105.08~s is a 5\% error in the independent variable
of every fit.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}l l@{}}
\toprule
Measured values & \code{configs/dt\_grid\_measured.yaml} \\
Loader          & \code{schema.load\_measured\_dt()} \\
Producer        & \code{scripts/check\_training\_csvs.py} (from the real
                  \code{Age} column) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsubsection{Two deeper traps at opposite ends of the grid}
\label{sec:dttraps}

\textbf{Short end --- the linearity premise is void.} The premise that
$\dd t = 10^{-6}$~s is a ``small, linear step'' is false \emph{everywhere in
this box}: 99.99\% of (state, isotope) cells have destruction timescale
$\tau_i = Y_i/|\dot{Y}_i|$ shorter than $\dd t_1$, because Sobol initial
compositions carry free nucleons. Every label --- even the shortest --- encodes
a full stiff \textbf{relaxation}, not a linear increment. This is why the flux
target $\Phi$ is \emph{time-integrated}, $\Phi = \int\varphi\,\dd t$, rather
than an instantaneous rate.

\textbf{Long end --- the splitting premise gets shaky.} The labels are
constant-$(T,\rho)$ burns. The photodisintegration rates that control Si burning
have temperature sensitivity
$\nu_T = \partial\ln\lambda/\partial\ln T \approx 20$--40
($\nu_T \approx Q/kT$ with $Q \approx 8$--12~MeV at $\Tn = 3$--5;
charged-particle captures are milder, ${\sim}4$--17), so a 3\% temperature rise
can double a dominant rate. At $\Delta t = 10^{2}$~s in vigorous burning, the
energy released may well raise $T$ appreciably --- meaning a constant-$T$ burn
and a self-heating burn are different operators. Whether the host code calls the
network the same way at large $\Delta t$ is a genuine open question, developed
in \S\ref{sec:splitting}.

\subsection{Sobol sampling: the theory, and its limits here}
\label{sec:sobol}

\subsubsection{Discrepancy}
\label{sec:discrepancy}

For $P = \{x_1\ldots x_N\} \subset [0,1]^d$, the star discrepancy is

\begin{equation*}
D^*_N(P) = \sup_{B \text{ anchored at origin}}
  \left|\frac{\#(P \cap B)}{N} - \mathrm{vol}(B)\right|
\end{equation*}

The \textbf{Koksma--Hlawka inequality} bounds integration error by it
\citep{niederreiter1992,owen2021}:

\begin{equation*}
\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_k f(x_k) - \int f\right| \;\leq\; V(f)\cdot D^*_N(P)
\end{equation*}

with $V(f)$ the Hardy--Krause variation. Lower discrepancy $\implies$ better
coverage, for every function of bounded variation simultaneously.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}R{0.6} R{1.4}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Design} & \textbf{$D^*_N$} \\
\midrule
i.i.d.\ uniform &
  $O(N^{-1/2}\sqrt{\log\log N})$ --- probabilistic \citep{chung1949} \\
Sobol (digital $(t,m,s)$-net) &
  $O((\log N)^d / N)$ --- deterministic
  \citep{sobol1967,niederreiter1992} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

(The $(\log N)^d/N$ rate is the arbitrary-prefix sequence bound; the fixed
$N = 2^m$ net bound is one log factor sharper, $O((\log N)^{d-1}/N)$ ---
\citealt{owen2022}.)

\subsubsection{How Sobol is actually built}
\label{sec:sobolbuild}

Each dimension $j$ has a set of \textbf{direction numbers} $v_{j,1}$, $v_{j,2}$,
\ldots generated from a primitive polynomial over GF(2) via a recurrence
(\citealt{sobol1967}; implementations differ in their direction numbers ---
\citealt{bratley1988}; \citealt{joe2008}). The $n$-th point's $j$-th coordinate
is

\begin{equation*}
x_{n,j} = \bigoplus_k b_k\,v_{j,k} \qquad
(\oplus = \text{bitwise XOR},\; b_k = \text{bits of } n)
\end{equation*}

The construction guarantees exact equidistribution in \textbf{dyadic boxes}: an
elementary box of the form $[k/2^a, (k+1)/2^a) \times \ldots$ contains exactly
the right number of points once $N$ is a power of 2 --- though only down to the
resolution set by the net's $t$ parameter (boxes of volume $\geq 2^{t-m}$ for
$N = 2^m$; $t$ grows with dimension, so at $d \approx 82$ the guaranteed
resolution is coarse --- \citealt{niederreiter1992}; \citealt{owen2022}). This
is why \textbf{$N = 2^{20}$} --- powers of two are where the guarantee is tight,
and taking a non-power-of-2 prefix degrades it \citep{owen2022}.

\subsubsection{The gap you must fill honestly}
\label{sec:sobolgap}

\begin{warn}
Sampled dimension is not small: 2 thermodynamic $+$ 80 or 151 composition
coordinates $\implies d \approx 82$ or 153. At $d = 82$, $(\log N)^d/N$ with
$N = 2^{20}$ is astronomically larger than 1 --- \textbf{the asymptotic bound is
vacuous}.
\end{warn}

The real justification in high dimension is \emph{low effective dimension}
\citep{caflisch1997}: the integrand depends strongly on a few coordinates and
their low-order interactions, and Sobol's low-order projections are
well-distributed. This is an assumption, not a theorem.

Write down for yourself: which coordinates dominate, and does composition really
have low effective dimension when normalized to $\sum X = 1$ (which removes one
degree of freedom and correlates all the rest)? And note the sharper version of
the concern from \S\ref{sec:onezone}: uniform coverage of composition space is
coverage of a set that is \emph{mostly physically unreachable}.

\subsubsection{Scrambling}
\label{sec:scrambling}

Owen scrambling applies random permutations to the digits of each coordinate,
preserving equidistribution while making the estimator \textbf{unbiased} and
improving RMSE from $O(N^{-1})$ toward $O(N^{-3/2})$ for smooth integrands ---
the exact rate is $O(N^{-3/2}(\log N)^{(d-1)/2})$
(\citealt{owen1997a,owen1997b}; corrected in \citealt{owen2008}). It also breaks
the regular lattice artifacts plain Sobol shows in some 2-D projections (the
motivation for the \citealt{joe2008} direction numbers).

\subsubsection{The consequence that shapes this entire repository}
\label{sec:nonregenerable}

The grid used an \textbf{unseeded} scrambled sampler: permutations drawn from
system entropy and never recorded. Therefore:

\begin{result}
\textbf{The Sobol grid is NON-REGENERABLE. The shipped file is the only ground
truth.}
\end{result}

Everything follows: anything keyed to states persists \textbf{explicit
\code{state\_id} lists} (\code{configs/step5\_subsample\_*\_ids.json}), never a
re-samplable recipe; joins happen on \code{state\_id} only; and generating more
same-distribution data is impossible --- future data needs a fresh sampling
design.

Study \code{data/subsample.py} with this in mind: it looks like
over-engineering until you realize a re-drawn sample would be a \emph{different}
sample, and every measured number keyed to it would become unreproducible.

\subsection{The identity problem --- why
  \texorpdfstring{\code{state\_id}}{state\_id}}
\label{sec:stateid}

The raw CSVs carry \textbf{no sample id}. Three measured facts resolve identity:

\begin{enumerate}
\item The nine $\dd t$ files are \textbf{exactly row-aligned} --- row $i$ holds
      the same (\code{logT}, \code{logRho}, \code{initial\_*}) in every file.
\item \textbf{(\code{logT}, \code{logRho}) is NOT unique}: 154,405 of 1,041,400
      rows collide, because those columns are rounded to 3 decimals.
\item The \textbf{full initial state has zero duplicates}.
\end{enumerate}

Therefore \code{state\_id} $=$ row index (0-based);
(\code{state\_id}, \code{dt\_index}) identifies a record.

\subsubsection{Do this estimate yourself}
\label{sec:collisionestimate}

Rounding $\log T \in [9.2, 9.9]$ and $\log\rho \in [7, 9]$ to 3 decimals gives
${\sim}700 \times {\sim}2000 \approx 1.4\times10^{6}$ cells for
${\sim}1.04\times10^{6}$ rows, so Poisson occupancy $\lambda \approx 0.74$.

\textbf{Be precise about which fraction you are estimating} --- there are two,
they differ by nearly $2\times$, and picking the wrong one changes the
conclusion:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}R{1.000} l l@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Quantity} & \textbf{Formula} &
\textbf{Value at $\lambda = 0.74$} \\
\midrule
Rows \textbf{living in} a cell with $\geq2$ rows & $1 - e^{-\lambda}$ & 0.52 \\
\textbf{Excess} rows (total $-$ distinct occupied cells) &
  $1 - (1-e^{-\lambda})/\lambda$ & 0.29 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

The measured $154{,}405 / 1{,}041{,}400 = \mathbf{0.148}$ is a
\code{duplicated()}-style count --- rows minus distinct initial states --- so the
second row is the right comparison, and the measurement comes in at about
\textbf{half} the Poisson expectation. Against the first row it would look
$3.5\times$ low, which would be comparing two different things.

The direction is itself evidence: a low-discrepancy point set is \emph{more
uniform} than random, so it collides less. The pigeonhole argument survives
regardless --- collisions are structural, and coordinate joins are forbidden.

\begin{repopointer}
\textbf{In code:} \code{data/labels.py::join\_on\_state\_id} \textbf{refuses}
frames that are not \code{state\_id}-indexed. The invariant is not documented and
hoped for; a coordinate join is \emph{inexpressible}. Note the pattern --- the
repo uses it repeatedly (\code{guards.py}, \code{eligible\_mask}, the
screened-$\kappa$ refusal in \code{distributions.py}).
\end{repopointer}

\subsection{Label noise, floors, and censoring}
\label{sec:labelnoise}

\subsubsection{float32 labels}
\label{sec:float32labels}

Upstream models and label tensors are float32:
$\varepsilon \approx 1.19\times10^{-7}$. Any agreement band must be at least
${\sim}3\varepsilon(X_i + X_f)$ wide, or you are measuring rounding --- which is
exactly the band used in the handshake and integrator tests. This project parses
raw CSVs as \textbf{float64} and does conservation arithmetic in float64;
float32 is permitted in model internals only (\S\ref{sec:floatingpoint} derives
why).

\subsubsection{The \texorpdfstring{$1\times10^{-15}$}{1e-15} floor}
\label{sec:floor}

\code{final\_*} mass fractions are clamped at \code{FINAL\_X\_FLOOR = 1e-15}
upstream before logs were taken, and the floor is \emph{exactly attained} in
both networks. So:

\begin{result}
Values at the floor are \textbf{censored, not physical.}
\end{result}

A value at $10^{-15}$ means ``$\leq 10^{-15}$'', not ``$= 10^{-15}$''. The
statistically correct treatment is a one-sided (censored) likelihood, not a
squared error against the clamp. Note also the dynamic range: $X$ spans 15
decades, so plain MSE in linear $X$ is dominated entirely by the handful of
species near unity (\S\ref{sec:featureloss}).

\subsubsection{Energy normalization}
\label{sec:energynorm}

\code{eps\_nuc} and \code{eps\_nu} in the training CSVs are stored
\textbf{divided by $10^{16}$} (\code{EPS\_NORMALIZATION}); loaders must multiply
back. And \code{eps\_nuc} is \textbf{integrated} [erg/g] over the step while
\code{eps\_nu} is a \textbf{rate} [erg/g/s] --- different units in adjacent
columns.

\textbf{The convention trap.} The \emph{trajectory} (test-set) files use a
\textbf{different} convention: integrated per output row, \textbf{net of
neutrino losses}, and with \textbf{no} $10^{16}$ normalization. Mixing these up
produces a comparison that fails by orders of magnitude with a characteristic
$+1.00$ slope against $\dd t$-decade --- which is exactly how the mismatch was
eventually diagnosed. (Note the diagnostic logic: a \emph{slope} against a
control variable identifies a units error, where a raw magnitude discrepancy
would not.)

\code{EPS\_NU\_QUARANTINED} is machinery kept live (currently empty) so any
future re-extraction re-checks before training touches $\varepsilon_\nu$.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Leakage-safe splits}
\label{sec:splits}

All nine records of one state share its \code{state\_id} and therefore
\textbf{must} land in the same split. Otherwise the same $(T, \rho, X)$ appears
in train at $\dd t = 10^{-3}$ and in test at $\dd t = 10^{-2}$, and your test
score measures interpolation in $\dd t$, not generalization.

\code{SplitSpec.validate()} raises on any overlap; \code{split\_of()} is keyed on
\code{state\_id} only.

\textbf{Gap worth thinking about:} even state-level splitting may not suffice.
Sobol points are \textbf{not independent} --- consecutive points are constructed
to fill gaps left by their predecessors, so nearby indices are spatially
correlated \emph{by design}. A random \code{state\_id} split therefore puts
near-neighbours on both sides. Whether that is leakage depends on the smoothness
of $\Phi_{\Delta t}$ relative to point spacing. Form your own view; the
repository does not settle this.

\subsection{The missing rows}
\label{sec:missingrows}

1,041,400 rows against $2^{20} = 1{,}048{,}576$ grid points ---
\textbf{0.68\% missing}. Two mechanisms: the upstream builder silently dropped
runs whose output was truncated, plus a \code{logT}/\code{logRho} range guard.

With no sample id, rows are matched back by nearest-neighbour search in
box-normalized ($\log T$, $\log\rho$, $\Ye^{\text{initial}}$) space,
$\Ye^{\text{initial}}$ reconstructed via \eqref{eq:ye} ---
\code{crosscheck/sobol.py::ye\_from\_initial}. Validity is enforced by requiring
a \textbf{unique} grid index per row.

\textbf{Why care about 0.68\%?} Because \emph{silent} dropping is non-random:
runs were dropped because they were hard, so the missing set is biased toward
difficult states. \code{scripts/sobol\_missing\_rows.py} measures whether it is
uniform over the box or clustered. A clustered gap would mean the training set
has a hole exactly where the physics is hardest --- a very different problem
from losing 0.68\% at random.

\subsection{Stratification: encoding physics into sampling}
\label{sec:stratification}

The Sobol grid is uniform over the box; the \emph{physics} is not uniformly
interesting. \code{data/subsample.py} draws ${\sim}30{,}000$ states stratified
over 54 strata:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}l l@{}}
\toprule
$\Tn$      & $[1.6,\ 2.5,\ 3.3,\ 4.0,\ 5.0,\ 6.3,\ 7.95]$ \\
$\log\rho$ & $[7.0,\ 7.667,\ 8.333,\ 9.001]$ \\
\Ye{}      & $[0.45,\ 0.4667,\ 0.4833,\ 0.5001]$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

with the \textbf{QSE window $\Tn \in [3.3, 5.0)$ overweighted $\times2$}
(\code{OVERWEIGHT\_T9\_BINS = (2, 3)}, \code{OVERWEIGHT\_FACTOR = 2.0}).

That factor of 2 is a physics statement in code: the QSE window is where
clustering is emerging but equilibrium has not taken over, so it carries the
most structure per sample. Strata edges are the same as
\code{crosscheck/grids.py} --- one source, reused.

Note the design discipline: \code{stratified\_sample} takes an explicit
\code{seed = 20260710}, but the \emph{output} is persisted as an id list anyway,
because reproducibility-by-seed depends on library versions while an id list
does not.

\subsection*{Self-check --- the dataset}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{Self-check --- the dataset}
\label{sec:selfcheck-dataset}

\begin{selfcheck}
\item State Koksma--Hlawka and explain why the Sobol bound is vacuous at
      $d \approx 82$. Give the actual argument for using Sobol here, and name
      its assumption.
\item Explain in one sentence why unseeded scrambling makes the grid
      non-regenerable, and list three design decisions that follow.
\item Do the collision estimate of \S\ref{sec:stateid}. Explain why the measured
      value comes in \emph{below} Poisson, and what that says about the point
      set.
\item Given $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{float32}} \approx 1.19\times10^{-7}$ and the
      $10^{-15}$ floor, derive the narrowest defensible agreement band for
      $\Delta X$. Compare with
      $\max(0.1|\Delta X_{\mathrm{lab}}|, 3\varepsilon(X_i+X_f),
      2\times10^{-15})$.
\item The box's upper edge is 7.9~GK. Argue why an emulator is unnecessary above
      it --- then ask why the dataset includes states up to that edge anyway.
      \label{sc:tupper}
\item Sketch a \emph{trajectory-aware} sampling design and predict how it would
      change the measured error distribution.
\item Design the semigroup consistency test (\S\ref{sec:onerecord}): which
      $\dd t$ pairs, what metric, what constitutes failure.
\item Why is $N = 2^{20}$ specifically, and what is lost by taking a prefix of
      700,000 points?
\end{selfcheck}
