# Adversarial Referee Audit: Citation Verification for a Conservation-by-Construction GNN Emulator of Silicon Burning

## TL;DR
- **The report's load-bearing citations are overwhelmingly real and largely correctly described.** Of the 22 numbered references, the great majority are VERIFIED or PARTIAL (minor metadata slips); the single most important paper — NuGNN (arXiv:2606.04491) — exists and all three of its load-bearing Section V/VII quotes are verbatim-accurate, including the C=17 signed-log formula.
- **Two substantive errors must be fixed:** (1) the claim that Grichener et al. 2025's NNN uses a SEPARATE energy network ("DNN2") is **REFUTED** — the published architecture is a single shared network and the two-network design was explicitly tried and abandoned; and (2) the pynucastro 2.0 citation has the **wrong volume/page** (it is ApJ 947:65, not ApJ 951:23).
- **The masking-mechanism precedents (L0 hard-concrete, STE, sparsemax/entmax) all exist and are correctly described, but they are precedents for STATIC sparsification, not the trajectory-varying / input-conditioned active set the report's novelty requires.** That transfer is an unstated assumption the cited papers do not establish — the principal methodological gap in the project's novelty argument.

### Tier 2 — masking-mechanism precedents

**16. Louizos, Welling & Kingma 2018 — VERIFIED.** ICLR 2018; arXiv:1712.01312 (4 Dec 2017). The hard-concrete distribution provides stochastic gates whose expected L0 norm is differentiable and that "maintain the exact zeros in the parameters while still allowing for efficient gradient based optimization." Public code at github.com/AMLab-Amsterdam/L0_regularization.

**17. Bengio, Léonard & Courville 2013 (STE) — VERIFIED.** arXiv:1308.3432 (15 Aug 2013), "Estimating or Propagating Gradients Through Stochastic Neurons for Conditional Computation." Canonical straight-through estimator: hard 0/1 forward, identity-like gradient backward; biased but often effective.

**18. Martins & Astudillo 2016 (sparsemax) / Peters et al. 2019 (α-entmax) — VERIFIED.** Sparsemax (ICML 2016, PMLR 48:1614–1623) is the Euclidean projection onto the probability simplex, `sparsemax(z) = [z − τ1]₊`, with threshold τ a normalizer ensuring `Σ_i max(z_i − τ, 0) = 1`. It yields a **normalized (sum-to-one) sparse distribution over the simplex** with exact zeros; α=2 entmax recovers sparsemax. **The report's reason for rejecting sparsemax as an on/off membership gate — that it normalizes (sums to one over the support) — is technically correct.** Sparsemax is indeed the wrong primitive for an independent per-reaction gate g_j ∈ [0,1].

**19. Goussis 2012 / "2023 Michaelis–Menten paper" — VERIFIED.** Goussis 2012, Combust. Theor. Model. 16(5):869–926, DOI 10.1080/13647830.2012.680502 (Bibcode 2012CTM....16..869G), exists and concerns the relation/validity of QSS vs partial-equilibrium approximations ("it is shown that the quasi steady state approximation is a limiting case of the partial equilibrium approximation"). A **separate 2023 paper exists** and is identifiable: "Algorithmic criteria for the validity of quasi-steady state and partial equilibrium models: the Michaelis–Menten reaction mechanism," J. Math. Biol., DOI 10.1007/s00285-023-01962-0. It explicitly states the Goussis 2012 criteria "addressed the case where each fast time scale is due to one reaction" and adds "a new one that addresses the case where a fast time scale is due to more than one reactions" — **directly supporting the report's single-fast-timescale-per-reaction limitation claim**, and confirming the per-pair criterion does not directly capture coupled multi-reaction (QSE-group) equilibria.

## Details

**The central NuGNN scoping question (decisive for the report's masking/Target-B argument).** NuGNN's instability finding is explicitly tied to its own signed-log label preprocessing and the inverse transform back to ΔX, and the paper states the model learned conservation from data alone without an enforcing term. The report's reasoning is sound **only if it preserves this scoping**. If the report cites NuGNN as evidence that *hard conservation layers in general* cannot work, that is an overgeneralization the source does not support — and, given that the project's own design conserves baryon number and charge by construction through a fixed stoichiometric matrix (a different mechanism entirely, not a log-space inverse transform), the NuGNN instability is largely orthogonal to the project's approach. This should be stated explicitly rather than used as a blanket caution against hard constraints.

**The masking adversarial questions.**
- **(a) Trajectory-varying active set — the key unstated assumption.** All three cited gating precedents (L0 hard-concrete, STE, sparsemax/entmax) are demonstrated for *static* sparsification: pruning fixed weights/features once during training, or producing a sparse output distribution for a single prediction. The report's mechanism requires a gate g_j whose *membership re-selects per (T, ρ, Yₑ) state along a trajectory*. None of the cited papers establishes this input-conditioned / dynamic case. The transfer to a trajectory-varying active set is therefore an **unstated assumption**, not a result inherited from the precedents. The report should add citations to the conditional-computation / input-dependent-gating / mixture-of-experts-routing literature to ground this claim. (Note: the hard-concrete paper does mention "conditional computation in a principled way," which is the closest the cited set comes, but it does not demonstrate a state-dependent active set that re-selects along a dynamical trajectory.)
- **(b) Multi-reaction group equilibria.** The Guidry two-body A+B⇌C+D analytic criterion's restriction to a single fast timescale per reaction is corroborated by Goussis 2012 and explicitly by the 2023 Michaelis–Menten paper. The per-pair criterion does **not** directly capture the coupled multi-reaction (QSE-group) equilibria of Hix, where many reactions share an equilibrium — a genuine limitation the report should acknowledge as a known property, well-supported in the literature.
- **(c) asinh/sinh conditioning at large dynamic range.** A specific discussion of numerical conditioning, overflow, or the stable preimage range of the asinh/sinh latent transform (Döppel-Votsmeier 2023 or follow-ups) was **not located** — UNVERIFIED. The 2023 paper's stated motivation is sign-handling (the log transform fails on sign-changing source terms), not super-exponential gradient blow-up; the report should not attribute a conditioning-failure discussion to that paper without a located passage.

**Recency sweep (PARTIAL — see Caveats).** Two relevant recent items were identified: (i) "Deep Neural Networks for Modeling Astrophysical Nuclear Reacting Flows," arXiv:2504.14180, published ApJ DOI 10.3847/1538-4357/adf331 (2025), a DNN surrogate for nuclear reacting flows that situates Fan 2022 / Grichener 2025 / NuGNN as prior art; and (ii) **RHINE** = "R-process Heating Implementation in hydrodynamic simulations with NEural networks," Just, Xiong & Martínez-Pinedo et al., **arXiv:2507.09040, published in Physical Review D (2026)** — the first use of a deep-learning neural network to model r-process nucleosynthesis energy release in hydrodynamic merger simulations. Neither found item enforces *hard mass-and-charge conservation by construction* for a stiff reaction network via a fixed stoichiometric flux mapping with a differentiable equilibrium gate, so neither clearly supersedes the report's specific novelty; RHINE and 2504.14180 are adjacent (energy-focused surrogates), not direct competitors to the conservation-by-construction + equilibrium-masking design. A complete forward sweep was not possible (search budget exhausted).
