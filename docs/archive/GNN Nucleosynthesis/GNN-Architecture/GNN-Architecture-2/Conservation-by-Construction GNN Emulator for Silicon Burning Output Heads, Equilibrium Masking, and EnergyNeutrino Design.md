# COMPONENT B DESIGN — Output Heads, Conservation Map, Equilibrium Masking, and Energy/Neutrino Head for a Conservation-by-Construction GNN Emulator of Silicon Burning (Run 2 of 3)

## 1. Executive Summary
- **Target A (signed-net-flux → fixed stoichiometric map) is the recommended primary head; build it in a LINEAR output space with an INTERNAL asinh latent.** Conservation of baryon number and charge then holds by construction (∑_i A_i ν_ij = 0, ∑_i Z_i ν_ij = 0), independent of network accuracy — this is the Sturm & Wexler "train on fluxes, fixed final linear layer" result. Their GMD 13:4435–4442 (2020) abstract states verbatim: "we derive a mathematical framework for machine-learned replacements that conserves properties – say mass, atoms, or energy – to machine precision." DECISION threshold: adopt A as primary unless the Phase-0 kill-test shows Target B matches A's per-step |dYe| within a factor of 2 AND trains ≥30% faster.
- **The equilibrium-masking mechanism should be a HYBRID: a cheap Guidry partial-equilibrium test (|y_i − ȳ_i|/ȳ_i < ε, ε~0.01) computes an imposed prior mask, which a learned differentiable gate (L0 hard-concrete) is allowed to refine.** This is the load-bearing novelty. The mask must act on the LATENT flux representation, never by differencing two large learned fluxes (QSE-cancellation rule). Falsifiable fallback: if the learned gate fails to train stably (validation |dYe| regresses >2× vs imposed-mask-only over 3 seeds), FREEZE the gate to the imposed mask ("bottleneck-only Target A").
- **The energy head should predict e_nuc = ∑_j Q_j φ_j from the SAME signed net fluxes that drive composition (one consistent route), not from an independent DNN as NNN did.** Under the equilibrium mask, masked pairs carry near-zero NET Q·φ, so masking does NOT corrupt e_nuc — the key consistency win the design exploits. Biggest single risk: **the mask changing membership discontinuously along a trajectory could inject e_nuc/Ye jumps that destabilize Run-3 rollout** — flagged below for synthesis.

### 2.1 Target A head — signed net flux through a fixed stoichiometric layer
**Survey.** The Sturm & Wexler framework (GMD 13:4435–4442, 2020; architecture in GMD 15:3417–3431, 2022) trains a network to predict reaction fluxes (extents of reaction), then maps fluxes to species tendencies through a FIXED linear (stoichiometric) layer; the framework "conserves properties – say mass, atoms, or energy – to machine precision." [SOURCED, verbatim] The interpretable hidden layer "before these constraints can be interpreted as the net flux of atoms between molecules, or in terms of chemical kinetics, the extent of reaction." [SOURCED] Döppel & Votsmeier's atom-balance layer is constructed as "the reduced column echelon form of the null space basis of the molecular matrix … using the MATLAB functions null and rref." [SOURCED]

**Analysis.** For Target A the network emits one signed scalar φ_j per reaction j (never a forward/reverse pair). dY = ν·φ with ν the FIXED pynucastro stoichiometric matrix. Because each column of ν satisfies ∑_i A_i ν_ij = 0 and ∑_i Z_i ν_ij = 0 by construction of the reaction, dY conserves baryon number and charge EXACTLY for ANY φ — including a randomly initialized, untrained network. This is the structural guarantee; accuracy only affects WHICH conserving update is produced, never WHETHER it conserves.

The asinh question: the QSE-cancellation rule forbids the conserved/net quantity from being a nonlinear transform of two large numbers. The resolution is that the asinh acts INTERNALLY: the GNN's reaction-node latent h_R is decoded to a pre-activation z_j, and φ_j = sinh(z_j) (equivalently z_j = asinh(φ_j) is the latent). sinh is smooth and monotonic through zero and sign changes (unlike log), so it represents the many-orders-of-magnitude dynamic range of net fluxes while remaining differentiable at φ=0 — exactly Döppel & Votsmeier's 2023 motivation ("Instead of fitting the logarithm of rates, we embed the hyperbolic sine function into neural networks and fit the actual rates"). [SOURCED] The composition is: h_R → z_j (linear/MLP) → φ_j = sinh(z_j) → dY = ν φ (fixed linear). The conserved native output is dY, a LINEAR image of φ; the nonlinearity is upstream of the linear stoichiometric map, so conservation is untouched. This satisfies the rule "the conserved/net quantity must be the network's native LINEAR output, with any nonlinear transform kept internal/latent."

**DECISION (PARALLEL-WITH-KILL-TEST).** Target A = per-reaction signed φ via internal-asinh latent → fixed ν layer. Output/loss computed on dY (and on φ directly as an auxiliary). Threshold: the fixed-ν layer must drive baryon drift |∑A_i dY_i| and charge drift |∑Z_i dY_i| to ≤1e-12 per step (float64) in a units test on random weights; if not, the ν matrix export from pynucastro is wrong — STOP and fix the matrix, do not train.

### 2.2 Target B head — additive null-space projection in a linear space
**Survey.** The documented trap is now confirmed verbatim. NuGNN (Kim et al., arXiv:2606.04491, submitted 3 Jun 2026, unpublished preprint) predicts ΔX (change in mass fraction) via a two-channel sigmoid-difference head, with labels preprocessed as a SIGNED LOG: sign(ΔX)·(C + log10|ΔX/X|), C=17. [SOURCED] In Section V they state they tried both a physics-informed conservation loss AND a conservation-enforcing final activation (as in Fan 2022, Grichener 2025, Zhang 2025) but "found that such approaches made the training unstable because the target labels were preprocessed into logarithmic form … this inverse transformation made the optimization unstable and degraded the training performance." [SOURCED, verbatim] They abandoned hard conservation and rely on learning it from data, with a runtime retry: "if the sum of the predicted ∆X deviated significantly from zero, the evolution step was retried with a smaller ∆t." [SOURCED, verbatim] This is precisely the "NuGNN log-space conservation collapse" the brief names — and it is real, documented, and attributable.

**Analysis.** Target B predicts dY (or dX) directly and projects onto the constraint null-space {∑A_i dY_i=0, ∑Z_i dY_i=0 (and lepton rows, §2.4)} via a fixed additive projection P = I − Cᵀ(CCᵀ)⁻¹C, where C is the (A,Z,lepton) constraint matrix; equivalently the Döppel–Votsmeier reduced-column-echelon null-space basis applied to nuclear invariants. The lesson from NuGNN is unambiguous: **the projection is only safe in a LINEAR output space.** A log/signed-log output makes ∑ of the inverse-transformed outputs a nonlinear function whose projection is unstable — the exact collapse. Therefore Target B must emit dY linearly; asinh may be tried only as an INTERNAL latent (as in A), never as the output space on which the sum constraint is evaluated.

**DECISION (PARALLEL-WITH-KILL-TEST, fallback head).** Build B with a linear dY output and a fixed additive null-space projection layer; include an explicit NuGNN-replication unit test (train a small signed-log variant and confirm the conservation-loss instability reproduces — if it does NOT reproduce, our regime differs from NuGNN's and the trap reasoning must be re-examined). Switch-rule: prefer B over A only if Phase-0 shows B's per-step |dYe| ≤ 2× A's and B is materially simpler to train; otherwise B remains benchmark/fallback.

### 2.3 EQUILIBRIUM-MASKING MECHANISM (center of gravity)
**Why a mask at all.** Above ~3 GK silicon burning sits in QSE: Hix & Thielemann II (1999, *ApJ* 511(2):862, Bibcode 1999ApJ...511..862H; arXiv:astro-ph/9808203) state verbatim: "In all three cases, we find that the important abundances obey quasi-equilibrium for temperatures greater than approximately 3×10⁹ K, with relatively little nucleosynthesis occurring following the breakdown of quasi-equilibrium." [SOURCED] In QSE the forward/reverse pair of a reaction near-cancels; the NET flux is orders of magnitude below either gross rate. Guidry et al. (2011, arXiv:1112.4738; published Comput. Sci. Disc. 6:015003, 2013) formalize "microscopic equilibration … where taking the net flux in specific forward-reverse reaction pairs (f⁺−f⁻) leads to large errors if the timestep is too large." [SOURCED] A mask identifies equilibrated reactions so the head does not attempt to resolve a near-zero net difference between two large learned numbers — the core failure mode the project forbids.

**Candidate (a): learned end-to-end differentiable gate.**
- *Mechanisms surveyed.* Straight-through estimator (STE; Bengio 2013) — hard 0/1 gate forward, identity gradient backward, "biased but often effective." [SOURCED] Gumbel-sigmoid/concrete — temperature-annealed relaxation. L0 regularization with the hard-concrete distribution (Louizos et al., ICLR 2018) — stochastic gates whose expected L0 is differentiable, giving EXACT zeros. [SOURCED] Sparsemax/α-entmax (Martins 2016; Peters 2019) — output activations that project onto the simplex and yield exact zeros, with α=2 = sparsemax. [SOURCED]
- *Analysis for this problem.* A learned gate g_j∈[0,1] multiplies φ_j (Target A) so equilibrated reactions are driven to exactly zero net flux. Crucially, gating φ in Target A PRESERVES conservation: dY = ν(g⊙φ) still satisfies ∑A_i dY_i=0 because the masking scales each column, and column-wise ∑A_iν_ij=0 holds regardless of the scalar g_j. This is a major architectural advantage of masking-in-flux-space over masking-in-species-space. STE risks gradient bias; hard-concrete L0 gives principled exact-zero sparsity with a tunable sparsity penalty λ; sparsemax is attractive because the support is selected by a single differentiable projection but it normalizes (sum-to-one) which is wrong semantics for an on/off membership gate — so L0 hard-concrete is the better learned primitive here.
- *Risk.* Pure end-to-end gates can collapse (all-on or all-off) early in training when φ is itself untrained, and gate membership can flip discontinuously between steps.

**Candidate (b): imposed mask from a cheap inference-time equilibrium test.**
- *Mechanism.* Guidry's criterion: for a pair A+B⇌C+D, equilibrium abundance ȳ_i is the analytic root ȳ_i = −(1/2a)(b+√−q) with q≡4ac−b², and the pair is flagged equilibrated when |y_i(t)−ȳ_i|/ȳ_i < ε_i, ε_i~10⁻². [SOURCED] This is O(reactions) arithmetic from the current abundances — negligible cost — and requires NO training.
- *Analysis.* Deterministic, physically interpretable, and target-agnostic. It cannot adapt to systematic emulator bias (it tests the TRUE equilibrium condition, not the emulator's). It is the natural seed/prior.

**Candidate (c): HYBRID — cheap test seeds the mask, network refines.**
- *Mechanism.* Compute the Guidry mask m_j∈{0,1} each step; initialize/bias the learned gate logits toward m_j (e.g., gate logit = α·(2m_j−1) + MLP(h_R)), with the L0 prior centered on m_j. The network can only OVERRIDE the imposed mask where data demand it, and the override is penalized.
- *Analysis.* This combines the deterministic physics prior (stability, interpretability, zero-cost cold start) with learned correction for cases the analytic two-body criterion misses (multi-reaction equilibria, which Guidry's single-pair test handles poorly — the 2023 Michaelis–Menten criteria paper notes the original 2012 single-fast-timescale-per-reaction limitation). [SOURCED] It also gives a graceful fallback: set the learned override to zero and you recover candidate (b) exactly.

**DECISION (A/B-DEPENDENT only in that masking-in-flux requires Target A's φ; otherwise PARALLEL).** Adopt the **HYBRID**: Guidry partial-equilibrium test (ε=0.01) seeds an L0 hard-concrete learned gate acting on the latent flux φ_j. Falsifiable thresholds:
- *Adopt hybrid* if, over ≥3 seeds, hybrid validation per-step |dYe| ≤ imposed-mask-only AND learned overrides change <5% of mask memberships per step (membership stability).
- *Switch to imposed-mask-only (candidate b)* — i.e., FREEZE the gate to Guidry, the "bottleneck-only Target A" fallback — if the learned gate regresses |dYe| >2× vs imposed-only, OR if membership flips >5%/step (rollout-destabilizing churn).
- *Reject pure learned (candidate a)* unless it beats hybrid on |dYe| by >25% AND membership churn <5%/step.
Set ε via Phase-0 sweep over {3e-3, 1e-2, 3e-2}; ε too tight under-masks (QSE-cancellation errors return), too loose over-masks (real slow net flux is zeroed, biasing Ye). HYPOTHESIS (not established): ε~0.01 transfers from Guidry's α-network tests to a pynucastro heterogeneous network — must be measured.

### 2.4 Lepton bookkeeping and the constraint matrix
**Mechanism.** Build ν so that strong/electromagnetic reactions conserve Z and A column-wise, while WEAK reactions (electron capture, β±) change Z at FIXED A: a column for ²⁸Si(e⁻,ν)²⁸Al-type capture moves one unit of charge from nuclei to the electron/neutrino sector with ΔA=0. Concretely, append rows to the constraint matrix C: a baryon row (A_i), a charge row that includes the electron column (Z_i for nuclei, −1 for the electron, 0 for ν), and a lepton-number row (electron +1, neutrino +1, anti-ν −1). pynucastro exports A, Z, N, binding energy and supports weak rate tables (Suzuki 2016) and ΔY_e-changing reactions natively. [SOURCED] Ye = ∑_i Z_i X_i/A_i = ∑_i Z_i Y_i evolves because weak columns have nonzero ∑Z_i ν_ij (charge moves to leptons) while ∑A_i ν_ij = 0 (baryon number fixed). [DERIVED]

**Diagnostics (separating "A conserved" from "Ye evolving").** Report each step and accumulated:
1. baryon drift D_A = ∑_i A_i dY_i (target ≤1e-12, machine-precision by construction);
2. charge-to-lepton balance D_Q = ∑_i Z_i dY_i + dN_lepton (must close to ≤1e-12);
3. ∑X_i − 1 (mass-fraction closure);
4. dYe through weak reactions = ∑_{j∈weak} (∑_i Z_i ν_ij) φ_j — the PHYSICAL signal, which must be NONZERO and accurate.
The point: 1–3 are conservation residuals that must vanish; 4 is the physics that must survive. A design that zeroes 4 to make 1–3 vanish is wrong — hence weak reactions must NOT be maskable as "equilibrium" (β-decays are not in detailed-balance equilibrium in this regime). DECISION: exclude weak-reaction columns from the equilibrium mask's eligible set (PARALLEL-WITH-KILL-TEST). Threshold: if any weak column is ever masked, that is a correctness bug.

### 2.5 Energy / neutrino output head (PREFERENCE 3 — deeper consistency)
**Two routes and their relationship.**
- *Route 1 (flux route):* e_nuc = ∑_j Q_j φ_j, Q_j from mass excesses/binding energies (pynucastro uses AME binding energies + CODATA). [SOURCED]
- *Route 2 (composition route):* e_nuc = −∑_k (X_k^out − X_k^in)/A_k · (M_k c²)/Δt-integrated, i.e. from mass-excess differences of the predicted composition change. The MAESTROeX NN paper shows these are algebraically identical when fluxes are consistent: (ρḣ)^out = (ρh)^in + ρ^out e_nuc with "e_nuc a function of (X^in, X^out, q_k)," demonstrating e_nuc CAN be computed directly from updated mass fractions. [SOURCED]

**Consistency relationship.** With Target A, φ are the SAME signed net fluxes that produce dY = νφ. Then Route 1 (∑Q_jφ_j) and Route 2 (∑ mass-excess × dY_i) are identical up to the identity ∑_i (mass excess)_i ν_ij = −Q_j (definition of Q). So **deriving e_nuc from net fluxes is the consistent choice and is automatically equal to the composition route** — the consistency NNN lacked because NNN used a SEPARATE DNN₂ for e_nuc (Grichener; the MAESTROeX analysis explicitly questions "whether this additional DNN₂ is necessary"). [SOURCED] For context, Grichener et al. 2025 (*ApJS* 279:49, DOI 10.3847/1538-4365/ade717) report that their NNNs improve accuracy of the electron fraction by 280%–660%, the average atomic and mass numbers by 150%–360%, and nuclear energy generation by 250%–750% over a 22-isotope small net; the small-net e_nuc error during silicon core burning is large ("200–1000% error"), while the NNN e_nuc error runs roughly 70–90% (mesa_80) and mostly 60–100% (mesa_151), and neutrino-loss improvements range from 100% up to ~1,000,000%. [SOURCED] DECISION: compute e_nuc via Route 1 (∑Q_jφ_j) sharing Target A's φ; report Route 2 as a consistency diagnostic (their difference is a conservation residual that must be ≤ a tolerance). [PARALLEL-WITH-KILL-TEST] For Target B (no φ), only Route 2 is available — this is an additional reason A is preferred for energy consistency. [DERIVED]

**Behavior under the mask (the subtle, load-bearing question).** A masked (equilibrated) pair carries near-zero NET flux φ_j≈0 but HUGE gross throughput. Route 1 uses Q_j φ_j: since φ_j is the net flux and is ~0 for equilibrated pairs, the masked reaction contributes near-zero NET energy — which is physically correct, because an equilibrated pair releases and reabsorbs energy at near-equal rates. **Therefore masking the net flux does NOT corrupt e_nuc**, precisely because e_nuc is built from NET fluxes, not gross rates. This is the same structural reason the QSE-cancellation rule protects composition. The danger would only arise if the head tried to sum gross forward/reverse energies separately — which the design forbids. [DERIVED, grounded in Guidry's net-flux definition and pynucastro Q-values]

**Partition functions across QSE/NSE transition.** Reverse rates and Q-value temperature dependence enter through nuclear partition functions G(T); pynucastro derives reverse rates by detailed balance with the ratio of partition functions (Rauscher 1997/2003 tables). [SOURCED] To keep e_nuc consistent across the QSE→NSE transition, the Q_j used in the energy head must use the SAME partition-function-corrected, T-dependent values as the rates that the backbone sees, so that as more groups equilibrate the energy bookkeeping tracks the shifting effective Q. DECISION: tabulate Q_j(T) from pynucastro partition functions and feed T to the energy head; do not use T-independent Q. Threshold: e_nuc Route1−Route2 residual must stay ≤1% of |e_nuc| across the 3–4 GK transition band, else partition-function handling is inconsistent.

**Neutrino-loss head (DESIGN ONLY — accuracy floor OUT of scope).** Attach a parallel head ε_ν = ∑_{j∈weak} ⟨E_ν⟩_j φ_j, with ⟨E_ν⟩_j from the weak-rate ⟨E_ν⟩ tabulations, driven by the SAME weak-reaction net fluxes. This couples neutrino losses to the lepton bookkeeping (every masked-ineligible weak flux that changes Ye also emits ε_ν), giving a single consistent weak sector. The ⟨E_ν⟩ tabulation-uncertainty and neutrino accuracy floor are explicitly deferred to the later physics dive. [DESIGN-ONLY per scope]

## 3. Emulator Design v1 — Component B slice (implementable)

**Inputs from Run-1 backbone:** isotope-node latents h_I (per isotope) and reaction-node latents h_R (per reaction), from the heterogeneous bipartite pynucastro graph; thermodynamic scalars (T, ρ) broadcast.

**Target A head (PRIMARY):**
1. z_j = MLP_φ(h_R^j, T, ρ) ∈ ℝ (one scalar per reaction).
2. Equilibrium mask: m_j = Guidry(|y−ȳ|/ȳ<ε); gate g_j = HardConcrete(logit = α(2m_j−1) + MLP_g(h_R^j)); weak reactions forced g_j=1 (never masked).
3. φ_j = g_j · sinh(z_j) (internal asinh latent; LINEAR thereafter).
4. dY = ν φ (FIXED stoichiometric layer, float64) — native conserved linear output.
5. e_nuc = ∑_j Q_j(T) φ_j; ε_ν = ∑_{j∈weak} ⟨E_ν⟩_j φ_j.
6. Diagnostics: D_A, D_Q, ∑X−1, dYe_weak, Route1−Route2 energy residual.

**Target B head (BENCHMARK/FALLBACK):**
1. dY_raw = MLP(h_I, T, ρ) in LINEAR space (NOT log/signed-log).
2. dY = P·dY_raw with fixed P = I − Cᵀ(CCᵀ)⁻¹C, C = (A; Z+electron; lepton) constraint matrix (Döppel–Votsmeier reduced-column-echelon null-space construction on nuclear invariants).
3. e_nuc via Route 2 only; same diagnostics.
4. Includes a NuGNN-replication regression test (signed-log variant must reproduce the conservation-loss instability).

**Composition with neighbors (state explicitly):**
- **Conservation-in-latent (Run 3 interdependency, FLAG):** if Run 3 selects a latent-ODE / operator decode, the fixed-ν map (A) or the projection P (B) and the asinh-internal nonlinearity MUST live in the DECODE step, so the decoded native output is the linear conserved dY. Conservation cannot be enforced inside an opaque latent integrator. [FLAG for synthesis]
- **Mask ↔ rollout stability (Run 3 interdependency, FLAG):** a mask whose membership changes along a trajectory creates piecewise-defined dynamics; if Run 3 uses pushforward/multi-step rollout losses, membership flips inject discontinuities in dY and e_nuc that can destabilize the rollout gradient. Mitigations to hand to Run 3: (i) membership-churn penalty; (ii) temperature-annealed (soft→hard) gate schedule synchronized with rollout horizon; (iii) hysteresis (two thresholds) on the Guidry mask. [FLAG — do not solve here]

## 4. Hand to Phase 0 to measure (bbq/MESA-only quantities)
1. **Accumulation model of dYe** — systematic/linear vs random-walk/√N over a trajectory; sets whether per-step |dYe|≤3e-6 actually keeps end-to-end inside the 5e-3–1.5e-2 nuclear-physics floor. MEASURE, do not assume.
2. **Guidry ε that minimizes |dYe|** over {3e-3,1e-2,3e-2} on pynucastro silicon-burning trajectories — does ε~0.01 transfer from Guidry's α-network?
3. **Mask membership churn rate per step** along representative T(t),ρ(t) tracks — decides hybrid vs frozen-mask.
4. **Fraction of net flux carried by equilibrated reactions** — quantifies how much QSE-cancellation risk the mask removes.
5. **Empirical baryon/charge drift of Target B's projection** vs Target A's exact map under realistic dynamic range.
6. **e_nuc Route1−Route2 residual across the 3–4 GK QSE→NSE band** with partition-function-corrected Q(T).
7. **A vs B per-step |dYe| and training cost** — settles the deferred kill-test.

## 6. Open questions for the adversarial audit
1. **Asinh-internal dynamic range vs gradient health.** sinh(z) saturates explosively for large |z|; does the float32→float64 boundary (NuGNN casts back to float64 before X+=ΔX) suffice, or does sinh's super-exponential growth re-introduce a conditioning problem the log-space was avoiding? 2. **Masking-in-flux conservation claim.** I assert g⊙φ preserves ∑A_iν_ij=0 column-wise — TRUE algebraically, but the LEARNED gate's gradient flows back through ν; does that bias z_j to compensate for masking in a way that degrades Ye? Needs measurement.
3. **Single-pair Guidry criterion in a heterogeneous network.** Guidry's ȳ root is derived for A+B⇌C+D pairs; pynucastro QSE involves multi-reaction group equilibria (Hix group-totals). The 2012/2023 CSP work flags exactly this single-fast-timescale-per-reaction limitation. Does the analytic mask systematically mis-flag multi-body equilibria?
4. **Weak-reaction non-maskability vs near-equilibrium.** I forbid masking weak reactions, but some weak pairs (e.g., e-capture/β-decay) DO approach equilibrium near NSE; is a blanket no-mask rule leaving recoverable QSE-cancellation risk in the weak sector that directly hits Ye?

7. **Membership churn vs Run-3 rollout.** I flag but do not solve the discontinuity; if churn is intrinsically high (Phase-0 item 3), the entire hybrid mask may be incompatible with pushforward training, forcing frozen-mask and surrendering the learned-refinement novelty.
