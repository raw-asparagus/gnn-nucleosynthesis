Commun. 17:1045 (2026), DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-67802-5; Ono & Sugimura is genuinely **published** as ApJ 996, 9 (2026), DOI 10.3847/1538-4357/ae1ca9, online 13 Nov 2025 — not a preprint mislabeled as published).

## B. Defect List (ranked by severity)

**DEFECT 1 (load-bearing) — Silent re-derivation of the per-step Ye gate.** The inherited fixed input sets |ΔYe| ≲ 3e-6 under the conservative SYSTEMATIC (linear) accumulation model, derived from a floor F≈5e-3 to 1.5e-2, which implies N≈1.6e3 (5e-3/3e-6≈1667). The report headlines "5e-7 at N=1e4." The arithmetic is internally correct (F/N = 5e-3/1e4 = 5e-7), but it silently changes the assumed trajectory length from ~1.6e3 to 1e4 — a ~6× tighter gate — without stating or justifying N=1e4. **Fix:** state the assumed N explicitly, reconcile with the inherited 3e-6, and present 5e-7 as "the same systematic model evaluated at N=1e4," not as a new gate.

**DEFECT 2 (load-bearing) — Random-walk branch must not become the operative gate before measurement.** The random-walk gate F/√N gives ~5e-5 at N=1e4 (5e-3/100), ~17× looser than the inherited 3e-6 and 100× looser than the systematic 5e-7 at the same N (the "100× gap" arithmetic checks: 5e-5/5e-7=100). This is legitimate ONLY as a contingent consequence of the fixed input's instruction to MEASURE the accumulation model — it must NOT be adopted as the operative gate until D2's drift test demonstrates random-walk behavior. The conservative 3e-6 (systematic) must remain the operative gate until measured otherwise. If the report quietly treats 5e-5 as operative, that is an impermissible relaxation of an inherited constraint.

**DEFECT 3 (load-bearing) — C1 switch criterion stated two incompatible ways.** Exec summary and Hand-to-Phase-0 #4 say "if log-dt-grid rollout drift exceeds the ~3e-6 per-step Ye gate at dt≥0.1 s"; body C1 says "at any dt in [1e-6,1e2] s." These are materially different falsification triggers (a high-dt-only trigger will miss small-dt error-dominated drift — precisely the failure mode Ono & Sugimura warn about). Pick one; the whole-range version is the more defensible falsifier and is consistent with the small-dt instability literature.

**DEFECT 4 (overclaiming) — Asymmetric stiff-regime transfer in D1.** GNS (arXiv:2002.09405), MeshGraphNets (arXiv:2010.03409), Dynami-CAL (arXiv:2501.07373), and Brandstetter (arXiv:2202.03376) are all NON-STIFF particle/PDE dynamics. The report correctly demotes noise injection to a hypothesis, but if it promotes pushforward as "safest for STIFF systems" by leaning on Brandstetter's zero-stability (κ) claim, that is an unearned pass — Brandstetter's κ result is for PDE solving, not stiff QSE. **Both levers must be labeled equally as stiff-regime hypotheses/inferences.** This is the single most important symmetry failure to correct.

**DEFECT 5 (label accuracy) — Mixed INFERRED/SOURCED in the Grichener/Yao parenthetical.** Two distinct claims are bundled. (a) The "80- and 151-isotope networks" figure IS directly supported — Grichener et al. 2025 (ApJS 279, 49) trains its NNNs on the `mesa_80` and `mesa_151` networks (80 and 151 isotopes). NOTE: these exact isotope lists are flagged in the fixed input as bbq/MESA-only items the report must DEFER, not assert from literature — so citing them at all risks a scope violation (see Defect 10). (b) The "T/ρ training ranges spanning ~1–2 orders of magnitude" is NOT found in either Grichener or Yao and reads as an INFERRED detail dressed as SOURCED — relabel as INFERRED or delete.

**DEFECT 6 (citation precision) — Two Ono & Sugimura sub-claims unconfirmed.** (a) The "300K→600K drift test (twice the constant reference value)" — only a 300 K fixed-density test (Fig. 6c/d, n_H=10³ cm⁻³) is confirmed in the text; the 600 K endpoint and "twice the reference" framing are unverified. (b) The claim that Ono & Sugimura "note noise injection (Holdship 2021) and train-the-update (Maes 2024) are known to increase one-step prediction errors" — the *attribution of the two techniques to those two papers* is confirmed verbatim ("mitigated either by injecting Gaussian noise during training (Holdship et al. 2021) or by training the iterative updates themselves (Maes et al. 2024)"), but the specific "increase one-step prediction errors" tradeoff wording was not found in v1. Soften or re-verify against the published v2.

**DEFECT 7 (regime transfer, C3) — Out-of-regime governor; single point of failure.** Ono & Sugimura's method is from 6-species primordial/first-star collapse chemistry (H, H₂, e⁻, H⁺, H⁻, H₂⁺; 18 reactions), not silicon-burning stiff QSE. Adopting it as the rollout governor is itself a transfer the report must acknowledge. C3 resting ENTIRELY on this one out-of-regime paper is a single point of failure that should be hedged with at least a fallback (NuGNN's retry-with-smaller-dt) retained behind a measured comparison.

**DEFECT 8 (cosmetic) — Hix & Thielemann II dating.** The report dates the pair "1996/1998"; Paper II (astro-ph/9808203) was submitted Aug 1998 but published 1999 (ApJ 511, 862, DOI 10.1086/306692). Same paper. Cite as 1999 (submitted 1998) to match the fixed input and avoid a phantom-mismatch flag downstream.

**DEFECT 9 (label accuracy) — Goswami "mass-conserving" framing.** Mass conservation in Goswami et al. (arXiv:2302.12645; CMAME 419:116674) is the PoU-DeepONet *extension*, not a property of the base DeepONet. The framing should specify the PoU variant.

**DEFECT 10 (scope) — Verify no MEASURE-vs-DEFER violations.** The fixed input requires DEFERRING (to Phase 0, not "measuring" from literature): the per-reaction cancellation-ratio distribution, stoichiometric-matrix condition numbers, the exact mesa_80/mesa_151 isotope lists, and the fast/slow timescale-separation ratio. The Grichener mesa_80/151 citation (Defect 5a) is the one place this risks leaking in — confirm the report does not assert these as settled.

## D. What Survived (safe to build on as-is)
- **C2's grounding is solid.** MACE quote confirmed verbatim: a soft element-conservation loss "slowed down the training immensely, because, for every pass of training data through mace, the jacobian of the decoder neural network needs to be calculated and multiplied by the tensor coefficients of the latent ODE, which involves many operations on large matrices." Keeping conservation in the decode step is correctly justified. MACE's "mace outperforms its classical analogue on average by a factor 26" is also verbatim.
- **C3's source is real and published.** Ono & Sugimura (ApJ 996, 9 (2026), DOI 10.3847/1538-4357/ae1ca9): mechanism quote verbatim ("If the timestep is very short, the physical change over that step can be smaller than the prediction error... iterating such updates drives the solution away from the physical one"); update rule Δx(Δt)=(Δt/τ_ε)·Δx_pred(τ_ε) for Δt<τ_ε else Δx_pred(Δt), ε=0.1, 5 density subregions, 6 species, ">90% under 10%," and stability at 10⁻⁴ t_ff all confirmed. The method is sound; only the regime-transfer label (Defect 7) is missing.
- **D2 is exactly right.** The cumulative-sum drift test + slope regression (slope~1 ⇒ systematic ⇒ F/N; slope~0.5 ⇒ random-walk ⇒ F/√N) is the precise measurement the fixed input demands. The F/N and F/√N arithmetic all checks out.
- **The noise-injection hedge is correct and commendable.** Demoting GNS-style noise injection to a hypothesis near QSE (rather than treating it as a remedy) is the right call given that GNS/MeshGraphNets are non-stiff. Verified: GNS uses random-walk noise N(0, σ=0.0003); van de Bor 2025 (OJAp 8, DOI 10.33232/001c.142225) confirmed the Branca & Pallottini DeepONet instability under iteration.
- **Dynami-CAL, van de Bor, AMORE, Goswami, Holdship, GNS, Brandstetter, Hix & Thielemann I/II, NuGNN, Grichener, Yao/DeePODE, Sulzer & Buck, Döppel & Votsmeier 2023** — all exist and match their attributions (see enriched confirmations below). Holdship's "approximately 50 000 times faster" is verbatim from A&A 653, A76.
