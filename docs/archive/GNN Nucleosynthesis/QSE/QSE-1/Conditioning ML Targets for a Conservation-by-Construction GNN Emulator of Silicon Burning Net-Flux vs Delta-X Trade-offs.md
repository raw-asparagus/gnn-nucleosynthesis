# Conditioning of ML Prediction Targets for a Conservation-by-Construction GNN Emulator of Silicon Burning
### Run 1 — Strong-Reaction and Numerical-Conditioning Side

## TL;DR
- **Target A (signed net per-reaction flux → stoichiometric map) is the better-conditioned choice, but only if the model outputs are kept in a *linear* (or stably-invertible asinh) space and equilibrated reaction subsets are explicitly identified and removed/masked.** In silicon-burning quasi-statistical equilibrium (QSE) the net flux of a near-equilibrated reaction pair is the difference of two one-way fluxes that are "orders of magnitude" larger (Hix & Thielemann 1996, ApJ 460, 869), which is exactly the catastrophic-cancellation pathology the prior audit flagged for the "separate forward/reverse then subtract" option.
- **Target B (direct ΔX with a fixed null-space projection) is simpler and conserves exactly by one linear operation, but its conditioning degrades near equilibrium and becomes unstable the instant the output space is logarithmic/signed-log** — precisely the failure NuGNN (Kim, Chae, Ko, Mumpower & Smith 2026, arXiv:2606.04491) reported when combining a conservation constraint with signed-log preprocessing.
- **The decisive question is empirical, not in the literature**: across the bbq/MESA mesa_80 and mesa_151 trajectories, what is the actual per-reaction distribution of |net flux|/(gross forward + gross reverse), and how many reactions fall below the Yₑ-accuracy floor that Run 2 will define? We give a concrete "QSE-cancellation kill-test" to measure this directly on Zenodo record 14873443.

## Key Findings

1. **The cancellation problem is real and stated explicitly in the primary literature.** Hix & Thielemann (1996, ApJ 460, 869; astro-ph/9511088): during silicon burning "Net fluxes are often orders of magnitude smaller than either the forward or reverse reaction rates would indicate," and "the net reaction flux being the difference of two large numbers raises the potential danger of round-off errors." This is the exact pathology that makes the audited third option (predict non-negative forward and reverse fluxes, then subtract) unsound.

2. **The equilibria form a strict hierarchy.** Light-particle capture/photodisintegration pairs — (γ,α)/(α,γ), (γ,p)/(p,γ), (γ,n)/(n,γ) — equilibrate first and fastest, establishing QSE groups once the temperature exceeds roughly 3.3×10⁹ K (Thielemann, Nomoto & Hashimoto review, astro-ph/9802077: "Temperatures in excess of roughly 3.3×10⁹ K lead to a quasi-equilibrium (QSE) in the lower QSE-cluster which extends over the range 28 < A < 45 in mass number, while the path to heavier nuclei is blocked by small Q-values and reaction cross sections for reactions out of closed shell nuclei with Z or N=20"). The slow, well-conditioned net flow is carried by a handful of *bottleneck* reactions linking QSE groups.

3. **The bottleneck reactions are named and Yₑ-dependent.** At high Yₑ ≈ 0.498, ⁴⁵Sc(p,γ)⁴⁶Ti carries ≈70% of the flux from the silicon group into the iron-peak group — Hix & Thielemann (1996) recount that Woosley, Arnett & Clayton (1973) "contended that this linkage was dominated by a single reaction, ⁴⁵Sc(p,γ)⁴⁶Ti, with perhaps a quarter of the flow going through less important reactions like ⁴²Ca(α,γ)⁴⁶Ti" (i.e. ~75% through the one reaction), and Hix & Thielemann's own 299-nucleus calculation gives ≈70% at T₉=5, ρ=10⁷, Yₑ=0.498. At low Yₑ ≈ 0.46 the bottleneck shifts to neutron-rich Ca, with ⁴³Ca(n,γ)⁴⁴Ca carrying >90% of the inter-group flux. These bottleneck reactions are the ones whose *net* fluxes are large relative to their gross fluxes — the well-conditioned targets.

4. **NuGNN is the direct cautionary precedent for Target B in log space.** Kim et al. (2026, arXiv:2606.04491) predict ΔX for a 690-isotope X-ray-burst network with signed-log preprocessing; enforcing explicit mass conservation (loss term or output activation) "made the training unstable because the target labels were preprocessed into logarithmic form," since the inverse transform from signed-log back to ΔX before summing "made the optimization unstable and degraded the training performance."

## Details

### Part 1 — QSE/NSE structure and the cancellation problem

**1.1 Physics of the equilibria (verified).** A photodisintegration channel becomes competitive when the reaction Q-value drops below ≈30 k_B T; with Si-group Q-values of 8–12 MeV this occurs once T exceeds ~3×10⁹ K, with full QSE clusters established by ~3.3 GK (Hix & Thielemann 1996; astro-ph/9802077). Above this temperature the fast (γ,α)/(α,γ), (γ,p)/(p,γ), (γ,n)/(n,γ) pairs balance, and the abundance of any nucleus in QSE with ²⁸Si is fixed by just three abundances (free n, free p, ²⁸Si) plus thermodynamic state (Hix & Thielemann 1996, Eqs. 2–3):

  Y_QSE(ᴬZ) = [C(ᴬZ)/C(²⁸Si)] · Y(²⁸Si) · Y_n^(N−14) · Y_p^(Z−14)

  C(ᴬZ) = [G(ᴬZ)/2ᴬ]·(ρN_A/θ)^(A−1)·A^(3/2)·exp(B(ᴬZ)/k_BT)

As Y(²⁸Si), Y_p, Y_n approach their NSE values, Y_QSE → Y_NSE(ᴬZ) = C(ᴬZ)·Y_n^N·Y_p^Z, the global NSE result depending on only two abundances. The membership diagnostic r_QSE(ᴬZ) = log[Y_QSE/Y] is constant (=0 for the Si group) for all members of a group; distinct groups appear as plateaus offset from zero (Hix & Thielemann 1996, Eqs. 8–11).

**1.2 Group structure across temperature/density (verified).** A single NSE cluster exists for T ≳ 6 GK; as temperature drops it splits, first between ⁴He and ¹²C (~6 GK) and later between the Si-group and Fe-peak species (~4 GK) (Hix & Meyer 2006, Nucl. Phys. A 777, 188; astro-ph/0509698). In the 1.6–7.9 GK regime of this project there are three pools: a light-particle pool (n, p, α and light nuclei), a silicon group (A≈24–45: ²⁸Si, ³²S, ³⁶Ar, ⁴⁰Ca, ⁴⁴Ti), and an iron-peak group (A≈50+: ⁴⁸Cr, ⁵²Fe, ⁵⁶Ni, ⁶⁰Zn). The two heavy groups are separated by the Z=N=20 shell closures and the resulting small Q-values/cross-sections (Hix & Meyer 2006). Lower Yₑ delays the merging of the two groups into a single Mg-to-Ge cluster: at Yₑ=0.498 a merged group (within 10%) forms by X(Si group)=0.85, whereas at Yₑ=0.46 the iron-peak group reaches 90% of its silicon-QSE abundance only once 70–75% of the silicon-group mass is gone (Hix & Thielemann 1996). The NSE transition in this density range is fairly sharp in temperature (driven by the steep exp(B/k_BT) factor) but its location shifts with ρ and Yₑ.

**1.3 Bottleneck reactions (verified, quantitative).** As Finding 3. For the emulator the key point is: inter-group net flow is concentrated in a small number of reactions, while the overwhelming majority of strong/EM reactions are near-equilibrated and carry tiny net flux. This is precisely the structure that partial-equilibrium solvers exploit, and it is the structural fact that decides A vs B.

**1.4 Numerical-methods literature on cancellation/conditioning (verified).**
- Hix & Meyer (2006) quantify the stiffness directly: the overall stiffness ratio S = max|Re λ|/min|Re λ| "> 10¹⁵ is not uncommon in astrophysics," and near equilibrium the production and destruction timescales become so short relative to the dynamical timestep that the difference is "close to the numerical accuracy (i.e. 14 or more orders of magnitude)."
- This stiffness is why nuclear networks are integrated implicitly (Timmes 1999, ApJS 124, 241 — recommends variable-order Bader–Deuflhard time integration + the MA28 sparse solver as the best accuracy/efficiency balance; Longland et al. 2014, A&A 564, A90 confirm Bader–Deuflhard and Gear methods are robust where Wagoner's two-step method is not). bbq itself uses the Bulirsch–Stoer (Bader–Deuflhard-class) algorithm at constant T, ρ (Grichener et al. 2025).
- The hybrid QSE/NSE network of Hix et al. (1998, astro-ph/9805095; 2007, ApJ 667, 476) sidesteps cancellation by *not integrating equilibrated reactions at all*: it evolves group-total abundances (Y_SiG, Y_FeG, Y_αG) plus a few light nuclei (7 variables instead of 14 for the α-chain) and reconstructs members algebraically, giving ~order-of-magnitude speedups with under one-third as many variables and no significant loss of accuracy.
- Adaptive networks (Rauscher et al. 2002, ApJ 576, 323; ~700–2200 isotopes, isotopes added/removed by reaction-flow thresholds) are an orthogonal strategy that controls *cost*, not *cancellation*.
- The Guidry explicit-integration lineage is most directly relevant. They classify three stiffness types: (i) negative-population; (ii) macroscopic equilibration (F⁺−F⁻→0 for an entire species equation); (iii) **microscopic equilibration** (individual forward/reverse pairs f⁺−f⁻→0). Asymptotic and QSS methods (Guidry et al. 2011, arXiv:1112.4716, 1112.4750) remove (i)–(ii) but become non-competitive near equilibrium precisely because they cannot remove (iii). Partial-equilibrium methods (Guidry, Billings & Hix 2011, arXiv:1112.4738) target (iii) directly, detecting equilibrated pairs and replacing their net flux with an algebraic equilibrium constraint. Their explicit statement: "Near equilibrium the difference Fᵢ = Fᵢ⁺ − Fᵢ⁻ can be orders of magnitude smaller than Fᵢ⁺ or Fᵢ⁻ and small numerical errors in Fᵢ⁺ or Fᵢ⁻ can produce large errors in the difference."

### Part 2 — Conditioning of the two candidate targets

**2.1 Does Target A's signed net flux span a pathological dynamic range? (Yes — verified physics, ratios by inference.)** Within a QSE group the net flux of each internal reaction is driven toward zero while the gross fluxes remain enormous; only the bottleneck reactions retain large net flux. So the vector of *signed net fluxes* spans from O(bottleneck flux) down to values limited only by proximity to equilibrium — formally the round-off floor (Hix & Meyer's "14 or more orders of magnitude"). This is a genuine dynamic-range problem for Target A, but it differs fundamentally from the audited third option: in Target A the *target itself* is the signed net flux, so the model never forms the difference of two large *learned* numbers. The problem becomes one of *representing small signed numbers accurately*, not *cancellation between large learned outputs*. The combustion/QSS literature handles this exact case by identifying the equilibrated subset and not solving for their net flux at all: Guidry et al.'s partial-equilibrium criterion declares a reaction-pair equilibrated when all participating species satisfy |yᵢ − ȳᵢ|/ȳᵢ < ε with ε = 0.01 (arXiv:1112.4738; "typically of order 10⁻²" in arXiv:1112.4750), then imposes the algebraic equilibrium constraint. The ML analog: predict net flux only for the non-equilibrated (bottleneck) reactions and set the rest to their constraint-implied values.

**2.2 Conditioning of direct ΔX/ΔY (Target B).** Near equilibrium ΔX over a timestep is itself tiny for most isotopes (QSE members barely move while bottleneck-fed species change). Predicting ΔX directly is well-posed in a *linear* output space with an additive conservation projection: a fixed null-space layer onto the manifold {Σ Aᵢ ΔYᵢ = 0, Σ Zᵢ ΔYᵢ = 0} is a single linear operator and conserves baryon number and charge exactly to machine precision. The problem is dynamic range — a single linear output cannot resolve both O(1) bottleneck changes and O(10⁻²⁰) trace changes, which is why every prior ΔX emulator reaches for a log/signed-log transform, and that is where exact linear conservation breaks.

**2.3 The NuGNN precedent (verified, detailed).** Kim et al. (2026, arXiv:2606.04491):
- *Target:* ΔX per isotope for a 690-isotope X-ray-burst network. *Architecture:* a heterogeneous GNN with isotope nodes and reaction nodes and message passing along reaction edges, a flux preprocessor, attention/dropout/mixing layers, and a two-channel output head; benchmarked against a Res-U-Net and a fully-connected NN.
- *Preprocessing:* target labels transformed to signed-log form (their Section III.2) to span many decades with sign changes.
- *Conservation attempt and failure:* they note Σᵢ ΔXᵢ = 0 could be enforced via a physics-informed loss term or output activation "as done in previous studies (Fan et al. 2022; Grichener et al. 2025; Zhang et al. 2025)," but "in this study, we found that such approaches made the training unstable because the target labels were preprocessed into logarithmic form... Enforcing the explicit conservation constraint requires transforming the network outputs back from the signed log representation to ΔX... In practice, this inverse transformation made the optimization unstable and degraded the training performance." NuGNN therefore relied on the model learning conservation implicitly; per its abstract it "consistently achieves significantly better accuracy with errors of only a few percent" and, "when implemented in the network evolution code in place of the original solver, NuGNN successfully reproduces the final abundance patterns, whereas the other architectures fail to do so."
- *Contrast with Grichener et al. (2025, ApJS 279, 49):* the NNN is a fully-connected network with a **log-softmax output activation** on the composition so the mass fractions sum to one (12 layers for mesa_80, 9 for mesa_151; input layer → 1024 neurons → 2048-neuron hidden layers; ReLU). It predicts the *new composition* X rather than ΔX, with separate models per fixed timestep over T, ρ ranges spanning ~1–2 dex. Log-softmax enforces Σ Xᵢ = 1 (normalization/mass) but NOT charge conservation (Yₑ), and predicting X rather than ΔX hides the small-net-change problem inside a quantity of order the full abundance. Reported accuracy: "errors of no more than 1% per time step" (as summarized by Zhang et al. 2025, ApJ, 10.3847/1538-4357/adf331). Against the 22-isotope approx21_cr60_plus_co56 baseline (which folds all weak interactions into the single compound effective reaction ⁵⁶Fe + 4n + 2e⁻ → ⁶⁰Cr + 2ν_e), the NNNs "outperform the small nuclear reaction network by 280%–400% in the case of mesa_151 ... and by 390%−660% for mesa_80" on Yₑ, while the small net's nuclear-energy-generation error reaches "200–1000%" during silicon core burning.

**2.4 Output-space transforms and the linear-vs-nonlinear conservation clash (verified).**
- *asinh / signed-log:* Döppel & Votsmeier (2023, React. Chem. Eng. 8, 2620; and Proc. Combust. Inst. 40, 2024, 105507) embed a hyperbolic-sine (asinh) transform so the network fits *actual rates* (smooth through zero and sign changes) rather than log-rates, avoiding both log's singularity at zero and its inability to represent sign. This is the natural transform for *signed net fluxes* (Target A).
- *The fundamental clash:* a conservation projection (Aᵀ·ΔY = 0) is *linear* in ΔY, but log/signed-log/asinh output spaces are *nonlinear*. Exact conservation requires summing in linear space; if the native output is nonlinear you must invert the transform before projecting — and that inversion is what destabilized NuGNN.
- *Clean resolution in the literature:* make the *conserved* quantity the network's native linear output and keep the nonlinear transform internal/latent. Sturm & Wexler (2020, GMD 13, 4435; 2022, GMD 15, 3417) build a fixed final linear layer equal to the stoichiometric/A-matrix acting on predicted **fluxes** (non-negative via ReLU for photochemistry), so atoms are conserved to machine precision *by construction*: "Training ML algorithms on target fluxes S rather than tendencies ΔC would allow for mass conservation to numerical precision." This is exactly Target A's architecture — predict fluxes in a latent transformed space, multiply by the fixed stoichiometric matrix, obtain ΔY in linear space, conserved exactly. Döppel et al.'s atom-conserving CRNN extension and Kircher & Votsmeier (2025, J. Phys. Chem. Lett.) add positivity-preserving projection/backtracking layers.

**2.5 Prior art on flux-prediction surrogates in near-equilibrium stiff systems (verified).**
- Chemical Reaction Neural Networks (Ji & Deng 2021, J. Phys. Chem. A 125, 1082): embed the law of mass action and the Arrhenius law; output-layer weights *are* the stoichiometric coefficients, so the network natively produces reaction rates mapped through stoichiometry to species changes. The original CRNN does not conserve atoms; Döppel et al. (2024, Proc. Combust. Inst.) add a dedicated element-balance layer enforcing atom conservation as a hard constraint *without* requiring the stoichiometric matrix.
- Sturm & Wexler (atmospheric photochemistry): flux target + fixed stoichiometric layer = exact conservation (above).
- The combustion partial-equilibrium/QSS literature (Mott 1999 PhD thesis; Goussis 2012, Combust. Theory Model. 16, 869) formalizes identifying equilibrated reaction subsets and solving only for net flow through slow reactions — the chemistry-side analog of the silicon-burning bottleneck picture.

*Quantitative caveat:* the literature states the gross/net disparity as "orders of magnitude" (Hix & Thielemann 1996) and gives the overall network stiffness as S > 10¹⁵ with near-equilibrium cancellation reaching "14 or more orders of magnitude" (Hix & Meyer 2006), but provides NO single tabulated per-class gross/net ratio. The ratios above are inferences anchored on those statements plus the bottleneck flux fractions. The project's cited "6–8 orders of magnitude" figure for fast-equilibration vs slow-net-flow separation could **not be verified** in Hix & Thielemann 1996/1998/1999, Guidry et al. 2011, or Hix & Meyer 2006; the closest sourced numbers are S > 10¹⁵ overall stiffness, the "14+ orders of magnitude" near-equilibrium cancellation, and (for an α-network under Type Ia conditions) ~10¹² explicit timesteps implied by ~10⁻¹² s fastest rates vs ~1 s dynamical time (Guidry, Billings & Hix 2011). This figure must be measured directly from bbq trajectories (see kill-test).

## Recommendations

**Stage 0 — Run the kill-test (below) before committing architecture.** This is the gate. Until the |net|/gross distribution is measured on mesa_80/151 trajectories, neither target should be locked in. Benchmarks/thresholds depend on Run 2's Yₑ floor.

**Stage 1 — Default to a hybrid Target-A design** unless the kill-test shows net fluxes are hopelessly diffuse: predict signed net flux in an asinh latent space for the *active* (non-equilibrated) reaction set; detect equilibrated pairs with the ε≈0.01 partial-equilibrium criterion; map to ΔY through the fixed stoichiometric matrix so baryon and charge conservation are exact by construction. This mirrors the most successful solver (Hix QSE-reduced; Guidry partial-equilibrium) and ML-surrogate (Sturm–Wexler flux-target; CRNN) precedents.

**Stage 2 — Build Target B as benchmark/fallback**, in *linear* output space with an additive null-space projection onto {ΣAΔY=0, ΣZΔY=0}. Explicitly do NOT attempt conservation enforcement in signed-log space — that is the documented NuGNN failure mode. If dynamic range forces a transform, test asinh and verify the projection still trains stably (NuGNN replication check).

**Stage 3 — Benchmark both against bbq** on published metrics: Grichener report per-timestep errors ≤1%; the NNN beat the 22-isotope approx21_cr60_plus_co56 net by 280–400% (mesa_151) and 390–660% (mesa_80) on Yₑ. Compare per-isotope ΔX error, conservation residual (≈machine-ε for both by construction), and stability under autoregressive rollout (NuGNN's discriminating test: only the conserving GNN reproduced final abundance patterns when looped).

**Thresholds that change the recommendation.** (i) If >~90% of reactions have |net|/gross below the Run-2 Yₑ floor at the median timestep → Target A's reaction-space output is mostly noise → switch to Target B or to a bottleneck-only Target A. (ii) If the active-set stoichiometric matrix has condition number > ~10⁶ → flux errors amplify unacceptably → prefer Target B. (iii) If asinh+linear-projection Target B matches Target A's rollout stability → choose Target B for simplicity. **Final numeric thresholds depend on Run 2's Yₑ accuracy floor:** any reaction whose net flux contributes to ΔYₑ below that floor is, for accuracy purposes, equilibrated and need not be predicted accurately — this directly sets the masking threshold for Target A and the per-isotope error tolerance for Target B.

## QSE-Cancellation Kill-Test (draft design)

**Goal:** quantify cancellation severity and decide A vs B empirically.

**Inputs:** bbq one-zone trajectories from Zenodo 14873443 for mesa_80 and mesa_151; JINA REACLIB rates.

**Quantities to compute at each saved (state, timestep):**
1. For every reaction r: gross forward flux f_r⁺ = ρN_A⟨σv⟩∏Y_reactants and gross reverse f_r⁻; net φ_r = f_r⁺ − f_r⁻; and the **cancellation ratio** κ_r = |φ_r|/(f_r⁺ + f_r⁻) ∈ [0,1]. κ→0 = fully equilibrated (ill-conditioned as a "difference of large numbers"); κ→1 = net-dominated (well-conditioned).
2. The **equilibrium-deviation** δ_r = max over participating species of |Yᵢ − Ȳᵢ|/Ȳᵢ, with QSE/NSE Ȳ from the C(ᴬZ) relations; flag δ_r < 0.01 as equilibrated (Guidry criterion).
3. Per-isotope ΔYᵢ over the timestep, decomposed into contributing reaction net fluxes; the ratio of |ΔYᵢ| to Σ|stoichiometric gross contributions| (the isotope-level cancellation analog).
4. Condition number of the active-set stoichiometric matrix S (active = δ_r ≥ 0.01).
5. Fraction of total |ΔYₑ| carried by the top-k reactions (tests that net flow concentrates in bottlenecks).

**Sampling grid (T, ρ, Yₑ):**
- T₉ ∈ {1.6, 2.5, 3.3, 4.0, 5.0, 6.3, 7.9} (O-burning through the QSE window to near-NSE),
- ρ ∈ {10⁷, 10⁸, 10⁹} g cm⁻³,
- Yₑ ∈ {0.45, 0.48, 0.498},
- dt ∈ {10⁻⁶, 10⁻³, 1, 10²} s,
prioritizing the QSE-active window 3.3–5 GK where cancellation is worst and the bottleneck picture is sharpest.

**Provisional pass/fail thresholds (final values pending Run-2 Yₑ floor):**
- *Target A viable* if, at the median timestep, the set {r : κ_r > 0.1} captures ≥95% of |ΔYₑ| and of |ΔX| for the dominant isotopes — i.e., a tractable active set carries the physics. *Fail* (→ Target B or bottleneck-only A) if net flow spreads across >~30% of all reactions with κ near the floor.
- *Target B viable* if a linear (or stably-invertible asinh) ΔX output with null-space projection reproduces bbq ΔX to within the Run-2 Yₑ floor *without* the conservation layer destabilizing training. *Fail* if conservation enforcement degrades training as in NuGNN.
- *Stoichiometric-map health* (affects A): cond(S_active) < 10⁶ pass; > 10⁸ fail.
- *Cancellation-floor calibration:* report the full distribution of κ_r; if the 10th-percentile κ across active reactions is below the Run-2 Yₑ floor expressed as a relative flux error, those reactions are formally indistinguishable from equilibrated and must be masked.

## Caveats / Questions the literature cannot answer (require local numerical experiments on Zenodo/bbq data)
1. **The load-bearing unknown:** the actual per-reaction κ_r = |net|/(gross_f+gross_r) distribution for mesa_80/mesa_151 across this regime. The literature only says "orders of magnitude," never a distribution.
2. Whether the project's cited "6–8 orders of magnitude" fast/slow timescale-separation figure holds for these networks (unverified in primary sources; must be measured as the ratio of fastest equilibrated-pair rate to slowest bottleneck net rate).
3. The condition number of the mesa_80/151 stoichiometric matrices (full and active-set) — never published.
4. Whether MESA's softwired mesa_80/151 inverse (detailed-balance) rates reproduce equilibrium well enough that κ_r → 0 cleanly, or whether rate-table interpolation introduces a spurious κ floor. Note Grichener et al.'s Appendix B documents a real MESA bug: the four-particle reaction n+n+⁴He+⁴He→³H+⁷Li had a rate 20–24 orders of magnitude too large because MESA omitted a phase-space factor when computing endo-energetic multi-body rates from detailed balance. Such artifacts directly corrupt κ_r and must be screened before any kill-test conclusion.
5. How many bottleneck reactions are simultaneously "active" and how that set evolves with Yₑ along a real trajectory (the literature names them only at fixed grid points).
6. Whether asinh-latent flux prediction + fixed stoichiometric layer actually trains stably on this dataset (no prior work has applied the Sturm–Wexler flux-target architecture to a nuclear network of this size).
7. The empirical rollout stability of each target under autoregressive feedback at the project's timesteps — only measurable on the data.

*Source-conflict note:* Where sources differ, I have flagged rather than averaged. The principal flagged items are (a) the unverifiable "6–8 orders of magnitude" timescale-separation claim, and (b) the Yₑ-dependence of the dominant bottleneck reaction (⁴⁵Sc(p,γ)⁴⁶Ti at high Yₑ per WAC 1973 vs ⁴³Ca(n,γ)⁴⁴Ca / neutron-rich-Ca proton capture at low Yₑ per Thielemann & Arnett 1985 and Hix & Thielemann 1996) — both pictures are correct in their respective Yₑ regimes, which is itself a constraint the emulator must respect across the 0.45 < Yₑ < 0.5 range.
