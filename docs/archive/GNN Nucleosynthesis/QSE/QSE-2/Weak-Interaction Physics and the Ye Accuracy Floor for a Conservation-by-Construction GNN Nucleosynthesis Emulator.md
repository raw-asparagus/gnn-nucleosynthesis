# Weak-Interaction Physics and the Yₑ Accuracy Floor for a Conservation-by-Construction GNN Nucleosynthesis Emulator (Run 2 of 2)

## TL;DR
- **The defensible nuclear-physics floor on the central electron fraction Yₑ in the O/Si-burning–to–collapse regime is ΔYₑ ≈ 3×10⁻³ to 1×10⁻² end-to-end per trajectory**, anchored by the well-documented FFN→LMP table-swap shift of ΔYₑ = 0.005–0.015 (Heger, Woosley, Martínez-Pinedo & Langanke 2001, ApJ 560, 307) and bracketed below by modern-rate residual uncertainties; an emulator whose accumulated Yₑ error is well under this is physically converged.
- **The provisional per-step budget of 10⁻⁵–10⁻⁴ is confirmed ONLY under a random-walk (√N) error-accumulation assumption; under systematic (linear) accumulation over 10³–10⁴ steps it is ~1–2 orders of magnitude too loose** and must be tightened to ~5×10⁻⁷–5×10⁻⁶. The budget is meaningless without stating the accumulation model.
- **A conservation-by-construction GNN in the Si-burning/CCSN-progenitor regime appears novel as of June 2026**; the only architecturally similar work, NuGNN (Kim, Chae, Ko, Mumpower & Smith, arXiv:2606.04491, 3 June 2026), is a GNN for a 690-isotope Type I X-ray-burst network — a different physical regime, achieving "errors of only a few percent," without hard conservation.

## Key Findings

1. **Four tabulations dominate this regime, partitioned by shell:** FFN (sd+pf, A=21–60, independent-particle model) for legacy/gap-filling; Oda et al. 1994 (sd-shell, A=17–39, USD shell model); Langanke & Martínez-Pinedo 2000/2001 (LMP; pf-shell, A=45–65, large-scale shell model) for the Fe-peak nuclei that actually control Yₑ; and Suzuki et al. 2016 (sd-shell Urca pairs, fine grid). All share the FFN (T, ρYₑ) grid.
2. **LMP electron-capture rates are ~1 order of magnitude smaller than FFN** for pf-shell nuclei; switching FFN→LMP raises pre-collapse central Yₑ by 0.005–0.015, with Heger et al. 2001 stating "about half of this change is a consequence of including beta decay; the other half, the result of the smaller rates for electron capture."
3. **The Yₑ-controllers are Fe-peak pf-shell nuclei** — ⁵⁵Co, ⁵⁶Ni, ⁶⁰Co, ⁵⁹Co, ⁵⁴,⁵⁵,⁵⁶Fe, ⁵⁶Mn, ⁵³Cr — i.e., exactly the A=45–65 LMP regime. ⁵⁵Co is repeatedly identified as the single most important EC nucleus.
4. **Network truncation is a comparably-sized or larger floor:** Farmer et al. 2016 found that "at the onset of core collapse, we find ≈30% variations in the central electron fraction and mass locations of the main nuclear burning shells, a minimum of ≈127 isotopes is needed to attain convergence of these values at the ≈10% level." Both mesa_80 and (to a lesser degree) mesa_151 sit at or below this convergence threshold — but this is the emulator's *ground truth*, not an emulator error.
5. **Interpolation artifacts are real and documented:** raw tabulation of log-rates produces large errors across the coarse FFN grid; the effective-log(ft) method (FFN 1985) removes most of the phase-space variation and is the recommended practice. MESA, pynucastro, and bbq all ship FFN/Oda/LMP (and pynucastro additionally Suzuki) with documented interpolation behaviors that must be verified locally.

### PART 1 — The tabulated weak-rate machinery

**(ii) Interpolation in practice.** A direct tabulation of rates "can lead to large interpolation errors" (Fuller et al. 1985), because the phase-space factor varies rapidly across the coarse grid cells. The standard remedy is the **effective-log(ft) method** of FFN 1985 (ApJ 293, 1): the continuum capture phase-space factors and neutrino-loss integrals are expressed via standard Fermi integrals and removed from the rate, leaving a slowly varying effective log(ft) that is interpolated (bilinearly in log T, log ρYₑ), then the phase-space factor is reattached at the target point. Per the OSTI abstract of that paper, "Since the effective log(ft)-values have most of the rapid temperature and density dependence associated with the phase space integrals removed, interpolation in temperature and density to obtain stellar rates is greatly facilitated in speed and accuracy." Oda 1994 and LMP both provide effective ⟨ft⟩ tabulations expressly to support this.

- **MESA:** the `rates` module (formerly `weaklib`) ships FFN (1985), Oda (1994), and LMP (2000) with precedence LMP > Oda > FFN; tables are functions of (ρYₑ, T) and are **coarse — 11 points in ρYₑ (Δlog ρYₑ=1) and 12 in T (Δlog T≈0.25), spanning 1≤log ρYₑ≤11 and 7≤log T<10.5**, constructed assuming complete ionization (MESA `rates` overview docs; Paxton et al. 2011, ApJS 192, 3). Average neutrino energies for reactions not in the tables are flagged in MESA's own documentation as "mostly fantasies or inappropriate" — a critical caveat for the neutrino-loss output head. Tabulated weak rates use interpolation; MESA's `net`/`rate_list.txt` machinery also supports piecewise monotonic cubic interpolation for user rate tables.
- **pynucastro:** `TabularRate` stores weak rates as functions of (T, ρYₑ); the C++ `AmrexAstroCxxNetwork` does bilinear interpolation, and from v2.1 the `PythonNetwork` was made consistent (previously it returned the nearest table entry). Out-of-table evaluation does edge-value extrapolation with no error raised. `TabularLibrary` default precedence is `["ffn", "langanke", "suzuki"]`. Suzuki 2016 was the initial weak-rate set (Urca application); pynucastro also warns that the ReacLib n→p decay "does not account for degeneracy at high densities" and recommends the Langanke tabular rate.
- **bbq:** the wrapper (Farmer 2023) drives MESA's solver (Bulirsch–Stoer; Deuflhard 1983) directly, so it inherits MESA's weak-rate tables and interpolation exactly (Grichener et al. 2025). **Local verification required:** which softwired networks (mesa_80, mesa_151) pull which weak rates, and how MESA handles points off the coarse grid edges.

**(iii) How weak rates enter the ODEs and energy bookkeeping.** Weak rates appear as the rₐᵦ terms in the abundance ODEs dXᵢ/dt (Grichener et al. 2025, Eq. 1) and change Yₑ directly (EC lowers Yₑ, β⁻ raises it). Energy bookkeeping is **separated**: nuclear energy generation uses nuclear mass differences (Q-values, from JINA ReacLib masses in MESA's `chem`), while neutrino losses are computed from the **tabulated average neutrino energies** ⟨Eν⟩ supplied per process per (T, ρYₑ) cell. Heger et al. 2001 emphasize that LMP neutrino energies during post-silicon-burning (T₉≈5) are generally *larger* than FFN's, so the entropy/energy loss for a given amount of EC differs between tables. For the emulator's neutrino-loss head this means ⟨Eν⟩ tabulation choice is itself a source of floor uncertainty. (Note: *during* silicon burning, neutrino losses are dominated by thermal/pair processes, not β-process neutrinos; β-process neutrinos dominate in the post-Si, pre-collapse phase — Patton, Lunardini & Farmer 2017, ApJ 851, 6.)

### PART 2 — The Yₑ-controlling nuclei

**(iv) Ranked controllers.** Aufderheide, Fushiki, Woosley, Stanford & Hartmann 1994 (ApJS 91, 389, "Search for important weak interaction nuclei in presupernova evolution") tabulated the **top ~90 EC nuclei averaged over the trajectory for 0.40 ≤ Yₑ ≤ 0.50** (their Table 25), extending FFN to A>60 and quenching GT strength. Heger et al. 2001 (ApJ 560, 307; PRL 86, 1678) re-ranked these in self-consistent presupernova models and identified the dominant EC and β-decay nuclei stage by stage.

| Evolutionary stage | Dominant EC nuclei (lower Yₑ) | Dominant β-decay nuclei (raise Yₑ) | Network membership |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oxygen burning (T₉≈1.5–2.5) | ³³S, ³⁵Cl, ³⁷Ar (sd-shell EC; Fields et al. 2018), ⁵⁴,⁵⁶Fe | (β minor) | sd-shell EC → check Oda coverage; likely partly in mesa_80 |
| Silicon burning (T₉≈3–4) | ⁵⁵Co (top), ⁵⁶Ni, ⁵⁵Fe, ⁵⁴Fe, ⁵¹V, ⁵³Cr | ⁵⁶Mn, ⁵⁶Cr, ⁵⁹Fe, ⁶¹Fe, ⁶¹,⁶³Co | Fe-peak pf-shell (LMP regime A=45–65) |
| Pre-collapse / β-equilibrium (T₉≈4–10) | ⁶⁰Co (EC rate dropped ~2 dex FFN→LMP), ⁵⁹Co, neutron-rich Fe/Mn | β⁻ on neutron-rich Fe/Mn establishes transient β-equilibrium (Aufderheide 1994; Heger 2001) | Neutron-rich → partly mesa_151; some beyond both nets |

⁵⁵Co is identified by Heger et al. 2001 as the most important nuclide for electron capture in 25 M⊙ stars, and is "among the top ten most important electron capture nuclei during the presupernova evolution" in Aufderheide et al. 1994 Table 25 (confirmed in multiple pn-QRPA follow-ups, e.g. arXiv:2505.01024).

**Network-membership annotation (must be verified locally against MESA isotope lists):** The Yₑ-controllers cluster in the Fe-peak A=45–65 band. mesa_151 (>127 isotopes) very likely contains the full stable+mildly-neutron-rich Fe-peak set including the LMP nuclei; mesa_80 likely contains the main controllers (⁵⁴,⁵⁶Fe, ⁵⁶Ni, ⁵⁵Co, ⁵⁶Mn) but is more likely to truncate the neutron-rich β-decay partners (⁶¹Fe, ⁶¹,⁶³Co) and the most neutron-rich EC nuclei. I could not find published explicit mesa_80/mesa_151 isotope lists in open sources; **this is a required local check** (Appendix A of Grichener et al. 2025 lists them). For context, the GENEC GeValNet25 reduced network (A&A 2025, aa51816-24) explicitly adds ⁵³Fe, ⁵⁵Fe, ⁵⁵Co, ⁵⁷Co to model core neutronisation and uses the minimal A=56 EC chain (⁵⁶Fe→⁵⁶Mn→⁵⁶Cr) to "mimic the effect of a much larger network … (cf. Aufderheide et al. 1994; Langanke & Martínez-Pinedo 2000)."

**(v) Documented Yₑ effect of table swaps.** Heger, Woosley, Martínez-Pinedo & Langanke 2001 (ApJ 560, 307; PRL 86, 1678): going from the Woosley–Weaver FFN-EC + old-β-decay set to the LMP set raises central Yₑ at iron-core collapse by **ΔYₑ = 0.005–0.015** (≈1–4%, ~3% most common, larger for more massive stars). Verbatim: "Values for the central electron mole number at the time of iron core collapse in the new models are typically larger, by ΔYe = 0.005-0.015 … About half of this change is a consequence of including beta decay; the other half, the result of the smaller rates for electron capture." The Langanke & Martínez-Pinedo review (arXiv:1008.2144) quotes "The central values of Ye at the onset of core collapse increased by 0.01-0.015 for the new rates. This is a significant effect." Paradoxically, iron-core masses are *smaller* despite the larger Yₑ, due to reduced outer-core entropy. The ⁶⁰Co EC rate alone changes by ~2 orders of magnitude FFN→LMP.

### PART 3 — The accuracy floor (primary deliverable)

**(vi) Uncertainty-propagation studies.**
- **Sullivan, O'Connor, Zegers, Grubb & Austin 2016 (ApJ 816, 44; arXiv:1508.07348):** built a weak-rate library and varied individual EC rates by factors "consistent with uncertainties indicated by comparing theoretical rates to those deduced from charge-exchange and β-decay measurements." Result, verbatim: "+16/−4 % range in the mass of the inner-core at the time of shock formation and a ±20% range of peak νe-luminosity during the deleptonization burst. These ranges are each five times as large as those seen from a separate progenitor study in which we evaluated the sensitivity of these parameters to 32 presupernova stellar models." The collapse is more sensitive to *reductions* than *enhancements* in EC rates, and most sensitive to neutron-rich nuclei near the N=50, Z=28 closed shell. (This is core-collapse, slightly past the emulator regime, but bounds the EC sensitivity.)
- **Pascal et al. 2020 (PRC 101, 015803):** independently found "the individual EC rates are the most important source of uncertainty in the simulations, while the other inputs [EOS, mass model, progenitor] only marginally influence the results." As summarized in the N=50 follow-up (Johnston et al. 2022, arXiv:2202.09370), EC-rate uncertainties "can span orders of magnitude and produce larger variations in core-collapse properties than changes to the nuclear equation of state (EOS) or stellar progenitor (Sullivan et al. 2016; Pascal et al. 2020)."
- **Fields, Timmes, Farmer, Petermann, Wolf & Couch 2018 (ApJS 234, 19; arXiv:1712.06057):** 1000 Monte Carlo 15 M⊙ MESA models, 665 *thermonuclear* rates sampled (from STARLIB) with a 127-isotope network, **weak rates held FIXED** (LMP/Oda/FFN). Central Yₑ is among the narrowest distributions in the survey: 95% CI spread ≲1% at C-depletion, ≲0.25% at Ne-depletion ("Y_e,c is not strongly affected by the uncertainties in the reaction rates"); at O-depletion (their terminal epoch) central Yₑ,c ≈ 0.492 (solar)/0.493 (subsolar) with an asymmetric negative tail "extending to ≃ −2%" (ΔYₑ ≈ −0.010 at the extreme), driven by the thermonuclear ¹⁶O(¹⁶O,n)³¹S rate (Spearman rₛ≈−0.6). **Interpretation:** even *strong*-rate uncertainties move central Yₑ by only a few ×10⁻³ in the bulk during this phase (and reach ~10⁻² only in the extreme tail); weak-rate uncertainties (held fixed here) are the genuinely dominant residual but are bounded by the table-swap and Sullivan-style sensitivity studies. **No published study propagates weak-rate uncertainties through the exact O/Si-burning presupernova phase to a central-Yₑ band.**

**(vii) Synthesized Yₑ floor and per-step budget.**

*End-to-end (per-trajectory) floor.* Combining the table-swap shift (ΔYₑ = 0.005–0.015, FFN→LMP, an upper bound representing a historical *systematic* correction) with the modern-rate residual (charge-exchange-calibrated EC uncertainties; Fields-style bulk Yₑ spreads of a few ×10⁻³), I recommend:

> **Yₑ nuclear-physics floor: ΔYₑ(floor) ≈ 3×10⁻³ (range 3×10⁻³ – 1×10⁻²) per trajectory, central value.** Use **5×10⁻³** as the working point.

The lower bound (3×10⁻³) reflects residual EC/β uncertainties within the modern shell-model rate set; the upper bound (10⁻²) reflects the full table-to-table systematic still possible for poorly-constrained neutron-rich nuclei (Sullivan 2016; Pascal 2020). An emulator with accumulated Yₑ error below ~3×10⁻³ is physically converged against the weak-rate physics.

*Translation to a per-step budget* (N = 10³–10⁴ burn steps), stated explicitly under both accumulation models, using the working floor F = 5×10⁻³:

| Accumulation model | Per-step budget δ | N=10³ | N=10⁴ |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Systematic / linear** (biased per-step error) | δ = F/N | 5×10⁻⁶ | 5×10⁻⁷ |
| **Random walk / √N** (zero-mean per-step error) | δ = F/√N | ~1.6×10⁻⁴ | ~5×10⁻⁵ |

> **Recommendation on the provisional 10⁻⁵–10⁻⁴ budget:** *Confirmed* under the random-walk assumption (it brackets 5×10⁻⁵–1.6×10⁻⁴). *Challenged* under the systematic assumption, where it is 1–2 orders of magnitude too loose (the systematic-safe target is ~5×10⁻⁷–5×10⁻⁶). Because neural-network emulators typically exhibit *biased* (non-zero-mean) per-step errors, the conservative, defensible target handed to the kill-test run should be **per-step |ΔYₑ| ≲ 3×10⁻⁶**, relaxing toward ~10⁻⁴ only if the emulator's per-step Yₑ residuals are *empirically* demonstrated to be zero-mean and serially uncorrelated.

**(viii) Network-truncation (secondary) floor.** Farmer, Fields, Petermann, et al. 2016 (ApJS 227, 22; arXiv:1611.01207), verbatim: "at the onset of core collapse, we find ≈30% variations in the central electron fraction and mass locations of the main nuclear burning shells, a minimum of ≈127 isotopes is needed to attain convergence of these values at the ≈10% level." **Interpretation/caveat:** a literal 30% of Yₑ≈0.49 (=0.15) is unphysical; the percentages almost certainly refer to a *neutron-excess* measure η=1−2Yₑ (η≈0.01–0.04 in this regime), so ~10–30% maps to absolute ΔYₑ of a few ×10⁻³ to ~10⁻². **This must be confirmed locally against Farmer's figures/tables.** If so, the network-truncation floor is *comparable to or larger than* the weak-rate floor. mesa_151 (>127 isotopes) is near convergence; mesa_80 is below it. **The crucial point for target-setting:** the emulator only needs to reproduce its *own* ground-truth network (mesa_80 or mesa_151), so network truncation is NOT charged against the emulator — but it caps the physical meaningfulness of any mesa_80-trained emulator at the ~10% (Farmer) level regardless of emulator fidelity. For scale, Grichener et al. 2025 report their NNNs "outperform the small nuclear reaction network by 280-400% in the case of mesa_151 … and by 390-660% for mesa_80" on Yₑ (energy generation 250–750%; neutrino losses 100–10⁶%) — i.e., the emulators already substantially beat the α-chain small net on Yₑ.

**Verdict:** A **conservation-by-construction GNN emulator in the Si-burning/CCSN-progenitor regime is unclaimed as of June 2026.** NuGNN establishes that the GNN-on-reaction-network idea is now public (so speed of publication matters), but it (a) targets a different physical regime, (b) does not enforce hard conservation, and (c) does not address the Yₑ accuracy floor. The novelty window is open but narrowing; the conservation-by-construction + weak-interaction-regime + Yₑ-floor combination remains distinctive.

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt ΔYₑ(floor) = 5×10⁻³ (range 3×10⁻³–10⁻²) as the per-trajectory Yₑ physics floor.** Hand this to the kill-test run as the convergence target. Rationale: it sits just below the historical FFN→LMP systematic correction (0.005–0.015) and above the modern-rate residual, so beating it means the emulator is no longer the dominant error source relative to the weak-rate physics itself.
2. **Set the per-step kill-test threshold to |ΔYₑ| ≲ 3×10⁻⁶ (systematic-safe), not 10⁻⁴.** Then run the decisive diagnostic: measure whether per-step Yₑ residuals are zero-mean and serially uncorrelated across a full trajectory. *If* they are (random walk), relax to ~5×10⁻⁵–10⁻⁴; *if* biased (systematic), hold at ~3×10⁻⁶. This diagnostic is the single most decision-relevant experiment for the kill-test run.
3. **Verify locally (code-level checks flagged above):** (a) mesa_80 vs. mesa_151 isotope lists (Grichener Appendix A) and which Yₑ-controllers each contains, especially the neutron-rich β-decay partners; (b) which weak-rate tables bbq/MESA r23.05.1 actually load for these nets and the off-grid-edge extrapolation behavior; (c) MESA's average-neutrino-energy handling for the neutrino-loss head (documented as unreliable for reactions outside the tables); (d) the precise meaning of Farmer 2016's "30%/10%" (η vs. Yₑ).
4. **Treat the neutrino-loss head's floor separately** — it inherits the ⟨Eν⟩ tabulation uncertainty (FFN vs. LMP differ substantially at T₉≈5; Heger et al. 2001), which is larger in relative terms than the Yₑ floor and depends on which table is loaded.
5. **Publish promptly** given NuGNN (June 2026). Lead with the two distinguishing features NuGNN lacks: hard conservation by construction, and the Si-burning weak-interaction regime with a quantified Yₑ floor.

**Thresholds that would change these recommendations:** If a future Monte Carlo study propagates *weak-rate* uncertainties (not just strong rates as Fields 2018 did) through presupernova MESA models and finds a central-Yₑ spread below 3×10⁻³, tighten the floor accordingly. If mesa_80 is shown to be the production ground truth, the Farmer truncation floor (~10%, ≈ few ×10⁻³ in absolute Yₑ) dominates and the emulator target can be relaxed to match it. If the per-step residual diagnostic confirms zero-mean random-walk behavior, the per-step budget can be relaxed by ~1.5 orders of magnitude.

## Caveats
- **Verified vs. inferred:** Table coverages, grids, the FFN→LMP ΔYₑ=0.005–0.015, Sullivan ±20%/+16−4%, Fields Yₑ spreads (≲1% C-dep, ≲0.25% Ne-dep, 0.492–0.493 with −2% tail at O-dep), Farmer 127-isotope/10%/30% claim, Grichener 280–660% improvement, NuGNN "few percent," and all dates/DOIs are **verified from primary sources**. The synthesized floor (5×10⁻³), the per-step arithmetic, the η-reinterpretation of Farmer's 30%, and the mesa_80/mesa_151 membership of specific nuclei are **inferences** explicitly flagged for local verification.
- **Conflict flagged (resolved):** Heger et al. 2001 gives ΔYₑ "0.005–0.015"; the Langanke & Martínez-Pinedo review quotes "0.01–0.015." These are consistent (the review cites the upper/central part of the range), not contradictory.
- **Regime mismatch:** Sullivan 2016 and Pascal 2020 quantify *core-collapse/infall* sensitivity, slightly past the emulator's T₉≤7.9 (≈10^9.9 K) training ceiling; they bound EC importance but do not directly give a presupernova Yₑ band. Fields 2018 is in-regime but varied strong (not weak) rates and stops at O-depletion (it never reaches Si-depletion or collapse). The floor estimate therefore *bridges a genuine literature gap* by combining bounds rather than reading a single number.
- **Citation correction (important for the referee audit):** the Fields et al. 2018 preprint is **arXiv:1712.06057**, NOT arXiv:1801.01972 (the latter is an unrelated mathematics/ML paper). The journal reference ApJS 234, 19 / DOI 10.3847/1538-4365/aaa29b is correct. The Fields paper's terminal epoch is O-depletion; there is no Si-depletion/collapse Yₑ datapoint in it.
- **Suzuki pf-shell tables:** the well-established Suzuki et al. 2016 paper (ApJ 817, 163) covers only sd-shell A=20–27 Urca pairs; the pf-shell GXPF1J electron-capture tables attributed to the Suzuki group appear in later screening/Type Ia papers (e.g. ApJ 2020, abbb32) and should be cited separately if used, not conflated with the 2016 sd-shell paper.
