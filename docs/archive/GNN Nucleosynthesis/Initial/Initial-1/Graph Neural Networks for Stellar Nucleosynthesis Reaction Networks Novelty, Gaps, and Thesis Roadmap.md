# Graph Neural Networks for Stellar Nucleosynthesis Reaction Networks: A Research-Landscape Report

## TL;DR
- The thesis idea — applying graph neural networks (GNNs) to stellar nucleosynthesis reaction networks — is **no longer fully novel as of mid-2026**: the single closest existing work, **NuGNN (Kim, Chae, Ko, Mumpower & Smith, arXiv:2606.04491, submitted 3 June 2026)**, already builds a GNN surrogate for a 690-isotope nuclear network under X-ray-burst conditions, while Grichener et al. 2025 (arXiv:2503.00115, ApJS 279, 49) used a *fully-connected* network for the late-burning supernova-progenitor problem. The genuinely open space is now narrower but real: conservation-enforcing, geometry-aware GNN emulators that are *stably coupled into a stellar-evolution code* (MESA) across many timesteps for advanced burning.
- Grichener et al. 2025 is a careful proof-of-concept that improves electron-fraction accuracy by 390–660% (mesa_80) / 280–400% (mesa_151) over the standard 22-isotope α-chain net and yields a 20–34× speedup over the `bbq` large-network solver, but it is a fully-connected emulator with no enforced baryon/charge conservation, large per-isotope composition errors, demonstrated error growth over many recursive timesteps, no extrapolation, and the MESA coupling is not yet implemented.
- A PhD thesis can realistically target: (i) a stoichiometry-aware/graph architecture with *hard* conservation constraints (mass, charge/Ye, and energy), (ii) a coupled-in-the-loop demonstration inside MESA's split-burning framework with controlled long-horizon error, and (iii) generalization across variable timesteps and thermodynamic regimes — drawing on the much more mature combustion-chemistry and astrochemistry emulation literature, which has already solved several of these subproblems.

## Key Findings
1. **The computational bottleneck is real and quantified.** Late burning stages (O/Si burning to core collapse) require hundreds of isotopes and weak reactions to get Ye, the effective Chandrasekhar mass (M_ch^eff ∝ Ye²), explodability, energy generation, and pre-supernova neutrino losses right; the coupled nucleosynthesis ODEs are stiff (large Jacobian eigenvalue ratio), so most massive-star studies fall back to ~21-isotope α-chain networks (the "approx21 family") that omit the physics that matters for explodability.
2. **Two independent 2025 ML emulators for *stellar/astrophysical* nuclear networks now exist plus a third in 2026**: Grichener et al. 2025 (fully-connected, MESA/bbq, Si-burning, ApJS 279, 49), Zhang et al. 2025 "Deep Neural Networks for Modeling Astrophysical Nuclear Reacting Flows" (DeePODE framework, arXiv:2504.14180, ApJ), and NuGNN (Kim et al. 2026, the first explicit *graph* neural network for a nuclear reaction network). An earlier proof-of-concept, Fan et al. 2022 "Neural Networks for Nuclear Reactions in MAESTROeX" (arXiv:2207.10628), used a 3-isotope carbon-burning network for Type Ia flames.
3. **The GNN-physics-simulator and stiff-chemistry-emulation literatures are mature and directly transferable.** DeepMind's GNS (Sanchez-Gonzalez et al. 2020, arXiv:2002.09405) and MeshGraphNets (Pfaff et al. 2020, arXiv:2010.03409) established learned message-passing simulators; combustion has ChemNODE, autoencoder+neural-ODE latent emulators, DeepONet/FNO operator-learning approaches, and crucially **conservation-enforcing architectures** (CRNN with an atom-conserving layer; ANN-hard with hard mass/energy/element constraints). Astrochemistry has a full lineage of emulators (Holdship/Grassi/Sulzer/Branca & Pallottini/Maes).
4. **Conservation and long-horizon stability are the recognized frontier.** Grichener's NNN enforces only that mass fractions sum to one (log-softmax) and does not enforce baryon/charge conservation or thermodynamic consistency of the energy terms; combustion work (CRK-PINN, ANN-hard) has shown that *hard* constraints prevent the error accumulation that otherwise diverges over long rollouts. This is the clearest methodological gap a thesis can exploit.

## Details

### 1. Field context: the nucleosynthesis bottleneck in massive-star evolution

**Why large networks matter.** A star of M ≳ 8 M_⊙ ends as a core-collapse supernova (CCSN). The outcome (neutron star vs. black hole, explosion energy, nucleosynthesis) is set largely by the *pre-collapse* core structure, which in turn depends on the composition evolution during advanced burning. The key quantity is the electron fraction Ye ≡ Σ_i X_i Z_i / A_i, because the effective Chandrasekhar mass scales as M_ch^eff ∝ Ye². During O and Si burning the core is reshaped by weak interactions (electron captures and β-decays) that are **absent from small α-chain networks**. Accurate Ye, nuclear energy generation ε_nuc, and pre-supernova neutrino losses require networks of *hundreds* of isotopes including neutron-rich species and weak rates (Farmer et al. 2016, arXiv:1611.01207; Renzo et al. 2017; Patton et al. 2017). Silicon burning and the Si/O shell structure that seeds the explosion are sensitive to network size.

**Why it is computationally hard.** The nucleosynthesis ODEs dX_i/dt = A_i (m_u/ρ)(−Σ_j(1+δ_ij) r_ij + Σ_kl r_kl,i) are *stiff*: the ratio of largest to smallest absolute eigenvalue of the Jacobian is enormous because reaction timescales span many orders of magnitude. Stiff systems demand implicit integrators (backward Euler, Bulirsch–Stoer, semi-implicit), which require repeated Jacobian construction and matrix inversions at each step. When fully coupled to the stellar-structure and mixing equations, the cost and the risk of numerical non-convergence explode. As a result, late burning is routinely done with ~21-isotope hardwired α-chain nets (the "approx21 family," modern descendants of Timmes et al. 2000), which give acceptable energy generation but the *wrong* Ye and neutrino physics.

**State of the art in stellar-evolution codes.** MESA (Paxton et al. 2011–2019; Jermyn et al. 2023) solves structure + composition simultaneously and offers "hardwired" approximate nets (e.g. `approx21_cr60_plus_co56`, 22 isotopes) and "softwired" nets where all allowed pathways between listed isotopes are linked (`mesa_79`, `mesa_127`, `mesa_160`, `mesa_204`; the latter is tuned to reproduce the final Ye of a 3298-isotope net in one-zone burns, giving Ye = 0.4032 vs. the large-net value, per Farmer et al. 2016). Mitigation strategies in the literature include: NSE/QSE group approximations (Hix & Thielemann 1996), adaptive networks (Rauscher et al. 2002), operator/"split" burning that decouples the burn from structure (Jermyn et al. 2023), rate-boosting factors (Cristini et al. 2017), and small-net-plus-large-net post-processing (Sukhbold et al. 2016). Grichener et al. used **`bbq`** (Farmer 2023) — a wrapper that calls MESA's network solver (Bulirsch–Stoer; Deuflhard 1983) on a single zone at fixed T, ρ, making the problem embarrassingly parallel and able to evolve hundreds of isotopes for one zone in minutes. The relevant network/post-processing code ecosystem includes SkyNet (Lippuner & Roberts 2017), WinNet (Reichert et al. 2023, arXiv:2305.07048), XNet (Hix & Thielemann), Torch (Timmes), and **pynucastro** (Willcox & Zingale 2018, arXiv:1803.08920; v2.1 arXiv:2311.17357), all drawing rates from the **JINA REACLIB** database (Cyburt et al. 2010, ApJS 189, 240; the live JINA-CEE/CeNAM library now advertises >60,000 rates).

### 2. Critical analysis of Grichener et al. 2025 (arXiv:2503.00115; ApJS 279, 49, DOI 10.3847/1538-4365/ade717)

**Methods.** The "Nuclear Neural Network" (NNN) is a **fully-connected** PyTorch-Lightning MLP, *not* a graph network. Input: log T, log ρ, and composition {X}_i; output: new composition {X}_i, a normalized nuclear energy term e_nuc, and a normalized neutrino-loss term ε_ν after a fixed timestep dt. Architecture: first hidden layer 1024 neurons, subsequent hidden layers 2048 neurons (12 layers for the mesa_80-trained model, 9 layers for mesa_151), ReLU activations, **log-softmax on the composition output to force Σ X_i = 1**, L1 loss (log-scale for composition, linear for energy terms), Adam at lr=10⁻⁴, batch 512. Training data: ~10⁶ (1024²) combinations per network, generated with `bbq` using the softwired `mesa_80` and `mesa_151` networks, sampled with a Sobol quasi-random sampler over 10^9.2 K < T < 10^9.9 K and 10⁷ < ρ < 10⁹ g cm⁻³, with initial compositions drawn randomly over 0.45 < Ye < 0.5. Crucially, a *separate model is trained for each of nine logarithmically spaced timesteps* from 10⁻⁶ to 100 s. Training-set generation cost: a few × 10⁵ CPU-hours; ~80 GB of training sets, ~4 TB of raw bbq output. The authors also discovered and fixed a real bug in MESA's large nets — endo-energetic multi-particle reaction rates were miscomputed by 20–24 orders of magnitude due to an omitted phase-space factor (Appendix B), a useful reproducibility contribution in its own right.

**How it couples to the solver.** The NNN is designed to *replace* the ODE solver for the burn step, used within an operator-split scheme (burn decoupled from structure/mixing, as MESA's split-burning already permits). At runtime a "burner" would fix the timestep, pick the NNN trained on the nearest timestep, and interpolate between the two nearest trained times if needed. **The MESA coupling is not actually implemented — this is an offline proof-of-concept** validated against ~700 held-out bbq test sets, not in-the-loop stellar evolution.

**Results.** Against the standard 22-isotope approx21 net, the NNN improves Ye accuracy by 390–660% (mesa_80) and 280–400% (mesa_151) — absolute NNN Ye error stays below 0.75% across timesteps — average atomic/mass number by 150–360%, nuclear energy generation by 250–750%, and short-timestep neutrino losses by 100% to 10⁶%. Speed: the NNN is 20–27× (mesa_80) and 25–34× (mesa_151) faster than bbq at producing the test outputs, and even 1.5–4× faster than the small 22-isotope net (NNN inference time is roughly constant in timestep size, whereas solver cost grows with dt and isotope count); these figures exclude the one-time training-data generation cost.

**Limitations the authors acknowledge.** (i) Per-isotope composition errors are large (10⁻⁴ ≲ ΔX_i ≲ 10⁻¹; worst-case predictions "completely unrelated to the target"), so they explicitly caution against using the NNN where detailed composition matters; (ii) neutrino losses become *worse* than the small net for dt ≳ 0.1 s; (iii) fully-connected nets extrapolate poorly, so the model is valid only inside the training regime — explicitly unsuitable for electron-capture SNe (lower T) and pulsational pair-instability SNe (lower ρ); (iv) over a 1000-timestep constant-(T,ρ) test, errors in quantities other than Ye become comparable to or exceed the small net, flagging error accumulation; (v) energy terms are emulated *without enforcing thermodynamic consistency*; (vi) the actual MESA integration is left to future work.

**Limitations beyond what the authors stress (my assessment).**
- **No hard conservation of baryon number or charge.** Log-softmax enforces ΣX_i = 1 (mass-fraction normalization) but not baryon number under the reaction bookkeeping, and not charge/Ye directly — Ye is read out from the predicted composition rather than constrained, so its good performance is empirical, not structural. Combustion work (CRK-PINN; ANN-hard, Combustion and Flame 2025) shows hard element/mass/energy constraints both reduce error and *prevent the long-horizon divergence* the NNN exhibits.
- **The per-timestep-model design is brittle.** Training 9 separate networks and interpolating between them is a workaround for the fact that an MLP cannot represent the *flow map* as a function of dt. Operator-learning (DeepONet/FNO) and neural-ODE formulations natively parameterize dt and would remove the interpolation hack.
- **The architecture ignores the reaction-network graph.** A dense MLP must relearn the sparse stoichiometric coupling from data; a message-passing GNN over isotope/reaction nodes (as NuGNN later did) bakes in that inductive bias and empirically gives "errors of only a few percent" — significantly better than dense/CNN baselines on the analogous task.
- **Training-set cost and coverage.** A few × 10⁵ CPU-hours per regime and the admission that broad (even unphysical) sampling was *required* for generalization both point to data-efficiency as a bottleneck; physics-informed losses or differentiable-solver training reduce data needs.
- **No uncertainty quantification.** A surrogate that occasionally returns "completely unrelated" isotope vectors with no flag is dangerous in-the-loop; Bayesian or ensemble UQ (cf. Bayesian CRNN) is needed for safe deployment.

**Citation status.** Published in ApJS (279, 49, 2025 Aug 1; DOI 10.3847/1538-4365/ade717; lead author Grichener at Steward Observatory, University of Arizona). It is already cited and extended by NuGNN (Kim et al. 2026, arXiv:2606.04491) — which explicitly contrasts its variable-timestep GNN with Grichener's fixed-timestep fully-connected approach — and by Zhang et al. 2025 (arXiv:2504.14180). As of this report the citing literature is small and entirely 2025–2026; there is no published critique beyond the implicit "we do it better with a graph" framing of NuGNN.

### 3. Adjacent ML literature — novelty check (highest-priority section)

**(a) GNN physics simulators / learned message passing.** The foundational line is Battaglia et al. 2018 (relational inductive biases / graph networks, arXiv:1806.01261), Sanchez-Gonzalez et al. 2020 "Learning to Simulate Complex Physics with Graph Networks" (GNS; arXiv:2002.09405), and Pfaff et al. 2020 "Learning Mesh-Based Simulation with Graph Networks" (MeshGraphNets; arXiv:2010.03409). These established the encode–process–decode message-passing simulator that generalizes across timesteps and system sizes and runs 1–2 orders of magnitude faster than the solver. Physics-informed/conservation-aware GNNs now exist (e.g. a momentum-conserving physics-informed GNN, Nature Communications 2025, that demonstrates *stable error accumulation over long rollouts and extrapolation* — exactly the properties a nucleosynthesis emulator needs). These are the methodological templates, but none had been applied to nuclear networks before 2026.

**(b) Neural ODEs and latent-space/autoencoder emulators for stiff chemistry.** ChemNODE (Owoyele & Pal 2021) learns chemical source terms as a neural ODE, explicitly to avoid the divergence that occurs when a plain regression surrogate is integrated. The autoencoder+latent-ODE family is mature: Grassi et al. 2022 (interpretable autoencoders compressing a chemical network into a latent space evolved by a stiff integrator, A&A 668, A139, arXiv:2104.09516); Sulzer & Buck 2023 (latent neural ODE; arXiv:2312.06015, reporting 55× speedup with a neural ODE and up to 4000× with a linear latent model); RONODE (stiffness-reduced neural ODE, AIAA 2022); Phy-ChemNODE (autoencoder+neural ODE with *embedded elemental mass conservation*, Frontiers 2025). DeePODE (Zhang et al. ApJ 2025, arXiv:2504.14180) is the direct astrophysical analogue — DNN surrogates trained by evolutionary Monte Carlo sampling that replace stiff solvers in nuclear reacting-flow codes (Castro/MAESTROeX/FLASH context), tested on 3- and 13-isotope networks.

**(c) Operator learning (DeepONet/FNO) for chemical kinetics.** Goswami et al. 2023 "Learning stiff chemical kinetics using extended deep neural operators" (arXiv:2302.12645) applied DeepONet + autoencoder-DeepONet + PoU-DeepONet to ROBER, POLLU, and syngas combustion, and proposed a *mass-conserving operator*. Extended FNO (Combustion and Flame 2024) imposes element/mass conservation as physical constraints and generalizes to unseen conditions. Branca & Pallottini 2024 brought DeepONet to ISM chemistry (A&A 684, A203; arXiv:2402.12435), reporting that ≃2×10⁷ predictions take ~25.36 CPU s vs. 3240 CPU s for the KROME solver — an ≈128× speedup — on a network "up to H₂ formation (9 species, 52 reactions)." AMORE (arXiv:2510.12999) is an adaptive multi-output operator network with a mass-conserving design for stiff kinetics. These solve the variable-dt generalization that defeats Grichener's per-timestep MLPs.

**(d) Physics-informed / conservation-enforcing architectures.** The Chemical Reaction Neural Network (CRNN; Ji & Deng 2021, arXiv:2002.09062) embeds the law of mass action and Arrhenius law so weights *are* stoichiometric coefficients and rate parameters — an interpretable, structurally-constrained kinetics network. Its atom-conserving extension (Combustion and Flame 2024) adds a dedicated layer enforcing physically realizable stoichiometries, improving stability and data efficiency. CRK-PINN (Combustion and Flame 2024) and ANN-hard (Combustion and Flame 2025, Xu group) enforce *hard* mass/energy/element conservation and show this prevents the error accumulation and unphysical divergence that plague unconstrained surrogates over long integrations. This subfield is the strongest evidence that the conservation-enforcing thesis direction is both feasible and valuable.

**(e) ML in r-process and explosive nucleosynthesis.** RHINE (Just, Xiong & Martínez-Pinedo, Phys. Rev. D 2026; arXiv:2507.09040, GSI/FAIR) is a neural-network emulator of r-process *heating* coupled into hydrodynamic neutron-star-merger simulations, tracking a small set of composition summary quantities (free-neutron/proton/alpha/heavy mass fractions, mean A, mean mass excess per baryon) rather than thousands of species — the first ML nuclear-heating scheme validated in-the-loop against full networks, with public model data and routines. Rodríguez-Sánchez et al. 2025 (arXiv:2502.17700) use NNs to predict particle-induced fission cross sections for r-process networks. There is also a body of NN nuclear-mass and rate-prediction work (e.g. GPR-NN mass models, arXiv:2509.08314). None of these is a GNN *solver* for the network ODEs.

**EXPLICIT VERDICT.** *"GNNs applied to stellar nucleosynthesis reaction networks" is no longer novel as a bare concept, but is only minimally explored and the specific thesis framing remains open.* The single closest existing work is **NuGNN: a Graph Neural Network for Nuclear Reaction Network Equations (C. H. Kim, K. Y. Chae, S. Ko [Sungkyunkwan University], M. R. Mumpower [Obsidian Research / Notre Dame], M. S. Smith [Stellar Science Solutions]; arXiv:2606.04491v1 [nucl-th], submitted 3 June 2026)** — the first GNN built directly on the bipartite isotope/reaction graph of a nuclear network. Per its abstract, NuGNN is "a deep learning surrogate solver for a large 690-isotope nuclear reaction network under general Type I X-ray burst conditions … [which] directly reflects the structure of the reaction network through heterogeneous isotope and reaction nodes and message-passing along reaction connections," and "consistently achieves significantly better accuracy with errors of only a few percent" relative to a Res-U-Net and a fully-connected baseline; it is also the only one of the three architectures that reproduced the final abundance patterns when swapped in for the solver. The remaining novelty for a thesis lies in: (i) GNN emulation specifically for *late-stage massive-star / CCSN-progenitor* burning (Grichener's regime) rather than X-ray bursts; (ii) *hard* conservation (baryon, charge/Ye, energy) built into a graph architecture; (iii) a *stable, validated in-the-loop coupling into MESA* across long timestep sequences; and (iv) variable-timestep generalization via neural-ODE/operator formulations on the graph. No published work yet combines a conservation-enforcing GNN with an in-the-loop MESA demonstration for advanced burning.

**Relevant codes.** Stellar evolution: **MESA** (with `bbq` single-zone wrapper). Network/post-processing: **SkyNet, WinNet, XNet, Torch, PRISM, NucNet, pynucastro**. Reacting-flow hydro: Castro, MAESTROeX, FLASH. ML tooling: PyTorch/PyTorch-Lightning, PyTorch Geometric (for GNNs), torchdiffeq (neural ODEs), and TensorRT (NuGNN used it for inference acceleration).

**Public datasets.** The **Zenodo reproducibility package for Grichener et al. (record 14873443)** — MESA models defining the parameter space, the ~10⁶-sample training sets, trained NNN models, test sets, and analysis scripts; this is the obvious starting dataset for a thesis. Also: **JINA REACLIB** (rates), STARLIB (rates + uncertainties), BRUSLIB, MESA inlists/test cases (`20M_pre_ms_to_core_collapse`), and the **RHINE** public ML data/routines for the r-process side.

**Open problems a thesis could realistically target.**
1. A stoichiometry/graph-structured emulator with **hard conservation** (baryon number, charge → exact Ye, and energy via q = e_nuc + ∫ε_ν dt) for advanced massive-star burning, benchmarked against Grichener's Zenodo data.
2. **Variable-timestep** emulation via a neural-ODE or operator (DeepONet/FNO) head on the graph, eliminating the per-timestep-model interpolation hack.
3. **In-the-loop MESA coupling** through split burning, with quantified long-horizon (10²–10⁴ step) error and a fallback-to-solver trigger when the surrogate is out-of-distribution.
4. **Uncertainty quantification / OOD detection** so the surrogate flags untrustworthy predictions.
5. **Transfer/generalization across regimes** (O-burning, Si-burning, electron-capture and pulsational-pair-instability SNe) and across network sizes, exploiting the graph's natural size-invariance.
6. **Weak-reaction and neutrino-loss fidelity**, the physics most poorly captured by small nets and (at long dt) by the current NNN.

## Recommendations

**Stage 0 (months 0–3) — reproduce and stress-test the baseline.** Download Zenodo 14873443, reproduce Grichener et al.'s fully-connected NNN results, and independently re-derive the long-horizon (1000-step) error growth and the per-isotope error distribution. *Benchmark to beat:* Ye error <0.75%, 20–34× speedup vs bbq. *Decision threshold:* if you cannot reproduce the headline metrics within a factor of ~2, fix the pipeline before any architecture work.

**Stage 1 (months 3–9) — build the graph baseline.** Re-implement on the same data as a message-passing GNN over the isotope/reaction bipartite graph (PyTorch Geometric), mirroring NuGNN's heterogeneous-node design but for the Si-burning regime. *Benchmark:* match or beat the dense MLP per-isotope error and reproduce NuGNN's "few-percent" accuracy and clear win over dense/CNN baselines on your data. *Decision threshold:* if the GNN does not beat the MLP on composition error, the graph inductive bias is not paying off in this regime — pivot to operator-learning instead.

**Stage 2 (months 9–18) — add hard conservation and variable dt.** Implement a conservation layer (baryon + charge, following the CRNN atom-conservation and ANN-hard templates) and a dt-parameterized neural-ODE/operator head. *Benchmark:* exact conservation to machine precision and stable error over ≥10³ timesteps (the property RHINE and physics-informed GNNs demonstrate). *Decision threshold:* if hard constraints don't reduce long-horizon drift below the small-net baseline, the value proposition collapses — reconsider.

**Stage 3 (months 18–30) — couple into MESA and demonstrate science.** Integrate via split burning, run a 20 M_⊙ progenitor to core collapse, and compare Ye, core structure, and neutrino losses against a full large-network run. *Benchmark:* end-to-end progenitor agreement in M_ch^eff-relevant quantities with net wall-clock speedup. Add UQ/OOD fallback. *Decision threshold:* publishable if the coupled run is both faster and closer to the large-net truth than the approx21 baseline.

**Throughout:** engage early with the Grichener/Renzo/Farmer (MESA) and Kim/Mumpower/Smith (NuGNN) groups to avoid duplicating in-progress work, and track the SJTU (Xu/Zhang) hard-conservation methods.

## Caveats
- **The field is moving fast and the novelty window is closing.** NuGNN appeared only days before this report (submitted 3 June 2026); the precise thesis framing must be checked against the very latest arXiv listings, because a conservation-enforcing GNN or a MESA-coupled version may appear at any time. Treat the "open problems" as provisional.
- **Some inference-time specifics for NuGNN could not be verbatim-confirmed.** Search snippets indicate NuGNN inference was timed on an NVIDIA RTX 5090 (vs. an Intel Xeon Platinum 8360Y for the original PRISM solver) with PyTorch and TensorRT engines, and that GNNs are intrinsically slower per call than plain NNs; the exact ms figures and net speedup factor should be read directly from the NuGNN Results section before citing a number.
- **Press-release sources for RHINE** (phys.org, GSI press) were used for context; the technical claims should be cited from the arXiv/PRD paper (arXiv:2507.09040) itself.
- **"Speedup" comparisons are not apples-to-apples across papers** (different hardware, network sizes, whether training-set generation cost is amortized). The Grichener 20–34× figure explicitly excludes the one-time few × 10⁵ CPU-hour training-data cost; the Branca & Pallottini ≈128× and Sulzer & Buck 55×–4000× figures are for much smaller astrochemical networks.
- **The ">60,000 rates" REACLIB figure** comes from the live JINA-CEE/CeNAM database description; the original Cyburt et al. 2010 (ApJS 189, 240) paper documents library V1.0 and does not itself state that count, so cite the database description for the current size.
- This report prioritized depth on the closest works (NNN, NuGNN) and the directly transferable methods; the broader nuclear-data-prediction ML literature (masses, cross sections, fission yields) is summarized but not exhaustively surveyed, as it is tangential to a *solver-emulation* thesis.

## Annotated reading list (ranked by relevance to a GNN-emulation-of-nucleosynthesis thesis)

1. **Grichener et al. 2025, "Nuclear Neural Networks," ApJS 279, 49 (arXiv:2503.00115).** The direct predecessor and benchmark; defines the problem, data, metrics, and the limitations your thesis should overcome. Read with the Zenodo package open.
2. **Kim et al. 2026, "NuGNN: a Graph Neural Network for Nuclear Reaction Network Equations" (arXiv:2606.04491).** The single closest existing work; the template for a heterogeneous isotope/reaction graph with variable-dt inputs. Study its architecture and its critique of fully-connected approaches.
3. **Sanchez-Gonzalez et al. 2020, "Learning to Simulate Complex Physics with Graph Networks" (arXiv:2002.09405).** Foundational GNS message-passing simulator; the encode–process–decode pattern and rollout-stability lessons transfer directly.
4. **Pfaff et al. 2020, "Learning Mesh-Based Simulation with Graph Networks" (arXiv:2010.03409).** MeshGraphNets; adaptive, resolution-independent learned simulation 1–2 orders of magnitude faster than the solver — the efficiency argument for your thesis.
5. **Ji & Deng 2021, "Autonomous Discovery of Unknown Reaction Pathways… Chemical Reaction Neural Network" (arXiv:2002.09062).** CRNN embeds mass-action/Arrhenius laws structurally; the blueprint for stoichiometry-aware, interpretable kinetics networks.
6. **Atom-conserving CRNN (Combustion and Flame 2024, "Robust mechanism discovery with atom conserving chemical reaction neural networks").** Adds a hard atom-conservation layer; directly motivates baryon/charge conservation in your architecture.
7. **CRK-PINN / ANN-hard (Combustion and Flame 2024 & 2025, Xu group).** Hard mass/energy/element constraints shown to prevent long-horizon error accumulation — the core methodological argument for conservation-enforced surrogates.
8. **Zhang et al. 2025, "Deep Neural Networks for Modeling Astrophysical Nuclear Reacting Flows" (arXiv:2504.14180, ApJ).** The DeePODE astrophysical-network surrogate; closest non-GNN comparison and a source of sampling/robustification techniques.
9. **Just, Xiong & Martínez-Pinedo 2026, "RHINE" (arXiv:2507.09040, Phys. Rev. D).** The exemplar of an ML nuclear scheme *coupled in-the-loop* and validated against full networks; model for your Stage-3 coupling and summary-variable strategy.
10. **Goswami et al. 2023, "Learning stiff chemical kinetics using extended deep neural operators" (arXiv:2302.12645).** DeepONet variants incl. a mass-conserving operator; the route to variable-dt generalization.
11. **Grassi et al. 2022, "Reducing the complexity of chemical networks via interpretable autoencoders" (A&A 668, A139; arXiv:2104.09516).** Latent-space reduction of a stiff reaction network; the dimensionality-reduction option for large nuclear nets.
12. **Sulzer & Buck 2023, "Speeding up astrochemical reaction networks with autoencoders and neural ODEs" (arXiv:2312.06015).** Latent neural-ODE emulator with quantified speedups (55×–4000×); transferable latent-ODE design.
13. **Owoyele & Pal 2021, "ChemNODE" (Energy and AI).** Neural-ODE source-term learning that guarantees ODE-solver compatibility — the antidote to integrate-time divergence of plain regressors.
14. **Farmer et al. 2016, "On Variations of Pre-Supernova Model Properties" (arXiv:1611.01207).** The reference quantifying how network size controls Ye and pre-SN structure; the physics justification for the whole enterprise.
15. **Timmes et al. 2000 / Timmes 1999 ("An Inexpensive Nuclear Energy Generation Network"; "Integration of Nuclear Reaction Networks for Stellar Hydrodynamics," ApJS 124, 241).** Origin of the approx21 family and the classic stiff-network integration methods you are trying to replace.
16. **Paxton et al. 2011–2019 + Jermyn et al. 2023 (MESA instrument papers).** Required to understand the host code, softwired vs hardwired nets, and the split-burning machinery you will couple into.
17. **Reichert et al. 2023, "The Nuclear Reaction Network WinNet" (arXiv:2305.07048) & Willcox & Zingale 2018 / Smith et al. 2023, "pynucastro" (arXiv:1803.08920, 2311.17357).** The open network codes and Python tooling for building/exporting networks and generating training data.
18. **Battaglia et al. 2018, "Relational inductive biases, deep learning, and graph networks" (arXiv:1806.01261).** The conceptual foundation for why graph inductive biases suit reaction networks.
19. **Branca & Pallottini 2024, "Emulating the interstellar medium chemistry with neural operators" (A&A 684, A203; arXiv:2402.12435).** Operator-learning for a stiff astrochemical network with ≈128× speedup; methodologically the closest astrochemistry analogue.
20. **Lippuner & Roberts 2017, "SkyNet: A Modular Nuclear Reaction Network Library" (ApJS 233, 18; arXiv:1706.06198) & Hix & Thielemann 1999.** Canonical references on network solver numerics and a modular, well-documented network library for benchmarking and ground-truth generation.
